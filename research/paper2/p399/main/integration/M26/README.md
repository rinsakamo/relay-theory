## Two levels of the Discrimination Requirement

### General provenance condition

Whenever structural similarity plays a substantial role in an identity argument, the proponent owes some Q/C-relevant justification for treating that similarity as source-theoretic or target-level rather than merely a product of the preservation scheme.

This does not require enumerating or experimentally eliminating every possible representation.

### Activated condition

The stronger undercutting condition arises only when a specific scientifically motivated alternative representation:

- remains compatible with current evidence,
- bears on the same Q and C,
- preserves a C-relevant distinction suppressed by the similarity-supporting representation,
- changes the evidential status of that similarity, and
- remains unresolved.

If the rival instead directly establishes non-identity, the issue is rebutting evidence rather than the target undercutting case.
