\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cognitive ontology; structural similarity; cognitive-capacity identity; scientific representation; theory comparison; philosophy of cognitive science


\begin{abstract}

Structural comparison can reveal genuine similarities without yet showing that those similarities support the identity of a cognitive-capacity type. This paper proposes a \emph{Discrimination Requirement}: when a structurally identified similarity plays a substantial role in an inference to cognitive-capacity identity, the evidential case must discriminate similarity supported by the source or target organization from similarity introduced or amplified by the representation used to compare the sources. The requirement is question-relative: rival representations matter as defeaters only when they are recruited to bear on the same identity question under the same declared individuation criterion. A similarity plays a substantial role when removing it, while holding the rest of the stated evidential package fixed, would remove a needed premise or require replacement evidence to preserve the inference. The empirical corpus supplies representation-sensitivity diagnostics, including a process-level Behrens--Friston case; it does not by itself instantiate a defeated capacity-identity inference. A concrete two-system example supplies the full undercutting logic. The paper offers a necessary evidential condition on structurally supported inferences to cognitive-capacity identity, not a necessary condition on capacity identity simpliciter and not a sufficient theory of cognitive capacities.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cognitive science inherits a large vocabulary of capacities: memory, attention, learning, belief, skill, prediction, control, concepts, and many others. These labels are indispensable for communication and literature organization, but they can also acquire evidential force before the comparison that would justify that force has been made explicit. The same problem arises in the opposite direction when a formal framework reconstructs heterogeneous models successfully: representational unification can look like evidence of a shared capacity structure even when the relevant notion of sameness has not yet been stated.

The construct-validity tradition already denies that a construct validates itself by being named \citep{CronbachMeehl1955ConstructValidity,BorsboomMellenberghVanHeerden2004Validity}, and cognitive-ontology projects explicitly represent relations among concepts, tasks, and measurements \citep{PoldrackEtAl2011CognitiveAtlas,TurnerLaird2012CogPO,PoldrackYarkoni2016CognitiveOntologies}. The question here is narrower and more demanding: \emph{what must be true of a comparison before the resulting structural similarity is entitled to play a substantial role in an inference that two targets instantiate the same cognitive-capacity type?}

The paper's central contribution is a necessary evidential condition on the promotion of structural similarity into capacity-identity evidence, which I call the \emph{Discrimination Requirement}:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Discrimination Requirement.} When a structurally identified similarity plays a substantial evidential role in support of cognitive-capacity identity, the evidential case must discriminate similarity supported by the relevant source or target organization from similarity introduced or amplified by the representation, preservation rule, or granularity through which the comparison is made.
\end{quote}

The requirement does not deny evidential value to explanatory or unificatory success. It separates two epistemic questions that can otherwise be run together:
\[
\text{scientific warrant for }R
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{warrant to treat pattern }I\text{ as evidence of capacity identity}.
\]
The first asks whether a representation is scientifically warranted for organizing or explaining the target. The second asks whether a particular shared pattern within that representation is entitled to play a substantial evidential role in an identity inference. The Discrimination Requirement does not say that an undiscriminated similarity is evidentially irrelevant or incapable of shifting credence at all. It says that representation-level success alone does not justify treating the similarity as a substantial premise for capacity identity. Here \emph{substantial} is argumentative rather than numerical: holding the rest of the stated evidential package fixed, removing or substantially weakening the similarity would either remove a premise required by the stated identity argument or force the proponent to supply replacement evidence to preserve the same level of support. Prediction, intervention, mechanistic fit, contrastive superiority, objective perspicuity, or convergence with a differently constructed representation may contribute to the second question; successful representation by itself answers only the first.

This makes the novelty claim narrower than generic representation dependence and more specific than the general injunction not to infer beyond one's evidence. North's objective perspicuity concerns whether one representation can be non-pragmatically better aligned with target structure \citep{North2026Perspicuous}; such an argument can help discharge the requirement. Brousalis makes epistemically relevant similarities and differences central to normatively significant theory comparison \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}. The Discrimination Requirement adds a second-order provenance question for the special case of identity inference: when structural similarity is doing substantial argumentative work, the evidential package must bear on whether that similarity is supported by the target organization or by the preservation choices that made the comparison possible. It does not demand a new species of evidence in every case; an existing mechanistic, predictive, interventionist, or perspicuity argument can simultaneously supply the bridge and the required discrimination. In short, justifying a representation does not by itself justify treating the similarities it exposes as evidence of capacity identity.

The distinction survives even if one grants that a representation is objectively superior for a genuine scientific target. A concrete example makes the point. Let ordinary observations be binary, \(o_t\in\{0,1\}\). System A has one internal bit, with \(x_{t+1}=o_t\) and ordinary response \(r_{t+1}=x_{t+1}\). System B has two internal bits, with \(u_{t+1}=o_t\), \(v_{t+1}=u_t\oplus v_t\), and the same ordinary response \(r_{t+1}=u_{t+1}\). For every ordinary input history the systems therefore have the same next-response mapping. A predictive representation \(R_{\mathrm{pred}}\) that preserves only \(o_t\mapsto r_{t+1}\) is perfectly adequate for that forecasting target and represents the systems as identical with respect to it. A mechanistic representation \(R_{\mathrm{mech}}\), however, preserves the additional state and update coupling in B. Under a diagnostic perturbation \(P^\star\), let A continue to report \(x_{t+1}\) while B reports \(u_{t+1}\oplus v_{t+1}\); the representations then support different counterfactual profiles. Now fix the identity question \(Q\): \emph{are these the same capacity type under an individuation criterion \(C\) that includes the relevant update and intervention profile?} If the ordinary predictive similarity is used as a substantial premise for a positive answer, \(R_{\mathrm{mech}}\) undercuts that evidential use. The point is criterion-relative: under a narrower \(C\) defined only by ordinary input--output mapping, the same mechanistic difference need not count against identity. The defeater is therefore \emph{undercutting}, not rebutting. It targets the warrant supplied by a particular similarity under a declared \(Q,C\), not the truth of the identity hypothesis independently of a criterion.

Cognitive-capacity labels are at most prima facie guides to individuation; neither label continuity nor a successful structural redescription supplies that second-stage warrant by itself.

Beni's structural-realist treatment of consciousness provides the clearest contemporary application, but the Discrimination Requirement is not derived from that case and does not depend on completing a PP--FEP-specific empirical comparison. Beni is a demanding positive interlocutor because the proposal explicitly assigns structural invariance a role in kind individuation. The general argument is that any framework-guided route to identity must distinguish source-side recurrence from framework-enabled commonality; a direct PP--FEP/source-first alignment would be an especially informative later application of that principle.

The direct analytical objects in this paper are source-grounded \emph{scientific claims about cognitive capacities}, not capacities themselves. The identity claims at issue are type-level scientific claims, not token identity claims. This paper does not choose one universal individuation criterion. Instead, an application must declare an identity question \(Q\) and a criterion \(C\)---for example a functional profile, a mechanistic organization, or another scientifically motivated classificatory standard. Representation choice becomes defeater-relevant only relative to that declared \(Q,C\). To make the claim-to-capacity inference explicit, claim-level recurrence becomes evidence for capacity identity only under an additional bridge:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{claim-level recurrent structure}
+\text{ target alignment}\\
&\quad +\text{ capacity-relevant bridge warrant}
+\text{ representational discrimination}\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow\text{ defeasible evidence for capacity identity}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \emph{target alignment} asks whether the source claims genuinely bear on the same capacity-level target rather than merely sharing a label. A \emph{bridge warrant} connects the recurrent structure to the capacity through mechanism, causation, explanation, intervention, or another relation relevant under the declared criterion \(C\). \emph{Representational discrimination} asks whether the similarity survives representations that are genuine rivals for the same \(Q,C\), or is otherwise supported as more than a by-product of the chosen comparison. These conditions need not be supplied by separate pieces of evidence: a strong mechanistic bridge, for example, may also discriminate the relevant representational rival. The Discrimination Requirement is a constraint on what the evidential package must distinguish, not a demand for an additional independent dataset or evidence type. This is an evidential schema, not a sufficient definition of capacity identity.

The method suppresses historical construct labels and selected source-authority metadata at the comparison stage, preserves the failure of an initially over-discriminating whole-claim comparison, asks a separately declared bounded-reuse question, and tests representation sensitivity. The large corpus, prospective reconstruction study, formal audits, and model replays are supporting diagnostics rather than the philosophical contribution. Their role is to make the Discrimination Requirement inspectable under controlled changes of representation and procedure, not to estimate population frequencies or independently establish a capacity taxonomy.

The full reconstruction sequence and audit staging are reported in the Supplementary Methods. The main text retains only the steps needed to evaluate the philosophical inference from structural comparison to individuation.



\section{The Discrimination Requirement}

The requirement concerns an asymmetry between evidence for a \emph{representation} and evidence for the \emph{identity relevance of an invariant represented within it}. Successful reconstruction can support the former while leaving the latter underdetermined.

The argument is:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Successful reconstruction under a representation \(R\) can provide genuine abductive evidence that \(R\) organizes, explains, predicts, or unifies relevant source material.
\item A structurally supported capacity-identity inference requires a more specific conclusion: that a shared pattern preserved by \(R\) is relevant under the declared identity question \(Q\) and individuation criterion \(C\).
\item When \(R\) helps constitute the comparison space, the appearance of commonality is compatible with multiple sources: recurrence in the source theories, expressive or coarse-graining resources of \(R\), or an interaction between them.
\item Reconstruction success by itself does not discriminate among those sources.
\item Therefore, when the similarity plays a substantial role in answering \(Q\) under \(C\), the evidential case must contain some consideration capable of discriminating target-supported similarity from framework-enabled commonality relevant to that same \(Q,C\).
\end{enumerate}

Call step 5 the \emph{Discrimination Requirement}. Its added burden is not simply ``justify the representation.'' It asks whether the evidential package distinguishes two explanations of the similarity when that similarity plays a substantial role in the stated identity argument: target-supported commonality and commonality made available by the chosen preservation scheme. Weak support can remain weak support. A second piece of evidence need not come from a wholly independent dataset, and a bridge warrant may itself do the discriminating work. The requirement is therefore a second-order condition on the use of structural evidence, not a universal threshold separating all evidence from non-evidence.

A useful corollary separates a \emph{trigger} from the defeater itself:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Representational-Defeater Trigger.} Fix an identity question \(Q\) and an individuation criterion \(C\). Two scientifically motivated, source-compatible representations count as defeater-relevant rivals only if they are recruited to bear on that same \(Q,C\) and their different preservation choices change the evidential status of a structural similarity relevant to the answer.

\textbf{Representational Defeater.} If the disputed similarity plays a substantial role in the identity inference, and the live same-question rival is unresolved, that rival undercuts the evidential use of the similarity until the case discriminates which preserved distinctions are relevant under \(C\). The defeater targets the warrant supplied by the similarity, not the truth of the identity hypothesis itself.
\end{quote}

The trigger condition is subject to the following restrictions:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=!,labelwidth=3.3cm]
\item[Scientifically motivated.] The alternative must serve an independently articulable modeling, explanatory, measurement, or abstraction aim; it cannot qualify merely because it produces the desired overlap verdict.
\item[Same question and criterion.] The representations must be recruited to answer the same identity question \(Q\) under the same declared criterion \(C\). A coarse representation answering ``is there any temporal dependence?'' and a fine representation answering ``is the temporal implementation the same?'' do not become defeater-relevant rivals merely because their descriptions differ.
\item[Live alternative.] The rival must remain compatible with the relevant source material for its stated purpose, change the support available for the same \(Q,C\), and not already be ruled out by evidence germane to that question.
\item[Discrimination.] The evidence must bear differentially on the rival preservation choices---for example through prediction, intervention, a mechanistic bridge, independently justified perspicuity, or convergence across differently constructed routes. Showing only that both representations can encode the sources is not enough.
\end{description}

This is not a global skeptical defeater. It leaves intact evidence that a representation is explanatory, predictive, or otherwise useful, and it need not erase every increment of support supplied by a structural similarity. Nor does every imaginable redescription trigger it. In particular, \emph{representation sensitivity is not yet a defeater trigger}: different representations can answer different legitimate questions and both be correct. The trigger arises only when the disagreement survives after \(Q\) and \(C\) are held fixed and changes the evidential support for that same identity inference.

This formulation also explains why stronger forms of explanatory success matter. Mere accommodation shows that a framework can encode the cases. A novel prediction whose success depends on the proposed invariant can rule out some accommodation stories. An intervention targeting the invariant can supply causal discrimination. A mechanistic bridge can connect the shared structure to the capacity. A North-style perspicuity argument can privilege one representation on grounds other than its ability to fit the present cases. Convergence with a differently constructed reconstruction route can provide cross-representational robustness. These are not interchangeable forms of evidence, but each can do discriminating work that reconstructability alone cannot.

The empirical analyses below establish the more modest antecedent fact that source-grounded structural overlap can be representation-sensitive. They do not by themselves establish a same-\(Q,C\) Representational-Defeater Trigger. Scientific-representation theory supplies the background fact that representational equivalence and surplus structure depend on what a mapping preserves \citep{Nguyen2017RepresentationEquivalence,NguyenTehWells2020Surplus,FriggNguyen2020ModellingNature,NguyenFrigg2022ScientificRepresentation}. The additional claim here is inferential: once a representation-sensitive similarity is recruited as a substantial premise for a fixed identity question, unresolved same-question alternatives create a specific evidential burden.

\section{Structural invariance as a live target}

Beni's structural-realist proposal for consciousness science provides the clearest contemporary application of the Discrimination Requirement \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. The application is not imposed on a weak reconstruction of his view. In Sect.~4, Beni explicitly says that structural invariance furnishes an \emph{objective criterion for kind individuation} \citep[Sec.~4, publisher PDF p.~8]{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}; Sect.~4.1 then introduces PP--FEP as the formal and empirical scaffolding meant to articulate that invariant organization \citep[Sec.~4.1, publisher PDF pp.~8--9]{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. The relevant proposal should therefore be read in its strongest form. Predictive processing and the free-energy framework are not offered merely as arbitrary notational schemes. Their formal precision and explanatory reach make them scientifically motivated candidates for redescribing otherwise heterogeneous consciousness theories so that relational invariants become visible. If that redescription is coherent and explanatorily fruitful, the success is evidence in favor of the candidate framework. The present argument should not be understood as denying that abductive support. This is not an attribution of a simple fallacy to Beni; the issue is the evidential scope of a genuine explanatory success.

The remaining question is exactly the Discrimination Requirement: \emph{what does that success discriminate?} In Beni's two-step proposal, the requirement enters between using PP--FEP to articulate candidate structural invariants and assigning those invariants objective force for kind individuation; it does not challenge the legitimacy of PP--FEP simply as a scientific scaffold. Suppose a PP--FEP description organizes several theories within a shared inferential architecture and supports illuminating explanations of their relations. At least two epistemic achievements follow. First, PP--FEP has shown itself capable of accommodating and organizing heterogeneous theoretical commitments. Second, if the resulting explanations outperform alternatives, fit independent phenomena, generate novel predictions, or support interventions, PP--FEP may acquire additional evidence as a representation of the target domain. Neither achievement is trivial.

What does not follow automatically is a third claim: that the particular invariants exposed by the PP--FEP redescription are thereby shown to be common to the source theories in a way that is independent of the candidate framework. The problem is not that the explanation is unsuccessful; it is that the source of its success can be mixed. A framework chosen in advance can reveal source-side structure, exploit its own expressive resources to accommodate diverse structures, or do both. Explanatory success becomes evidence for identity-bearing invariance only to the extent that the evidence discriminates among those possibilities.

This leaves room for a strong reply on Beni's behalf: empirical natural-kind constraints can themselves discharge the Discrimination Requirement. Beni's own complementary picture assigns natural-kind reasoning the empirical criteria and clustered content that populate the structural form supplied by PP--FEP. If those independently measured property clusters, interventions, novel predictions, or explanatory relations bear specifically on why the PP--FEP invariant is relevant to kind identity rather than merely available within the framework, the required discrimination is supplied rather than ignored. A systematic comparison showing that plausible rival frameworks fail where PP--FEP succeeds would provide a further contrast class, and a North-style argument that PP--FEP is independently more perspicuous with respect to the identity-relevant target could likewise discharge the burden. The present proposal therefore does not claim that Beni lacks empirical constraints. It specifies what those constraints must accomplish when structural invariance is assigned individuative force.

The present reconstruction supplies a complementary route. It is \emph{source-first} only in a limited and explicit sense: structured records are built from source-local scientific commitments before any comparison with PP--FEP, and they are not produced by first translating every source into PP--FEP vocabulary. The records still use a representation, a role basis, and explicit preservation rules; the method is not framework-free or representation-neutral.

This yields a triangulation strategy:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{framework-guided:}\quad
&\text{candidate framework} \longrightarrow \text{redescribed theories}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{candidate invariants},\\[1mm]
\text{source-first:}\quad
&\text{source theories} \longrightarrow \text{structured reconstruction}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{recurrent organization}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Convergence between the two routes would not prove a unique ontology, but it would be stronger evidence that the relevant organization is not merely an artifact of one candidate vocabulary. Divergence would also be informative: it would identify where the unifying framework contributes structure not recovered by the source-first route under its different representational commitments. The present paper establishes and stress-tests the second route. A systematic alignment with PP--FEP would be an especially probative application of the Discrimination Requirement, but it is not a premise of the general argument and is not reported as a completed result here.

Wajnerman-Paz and Rojas-L\'ibano sharpen a related issue from cognitive ontology by making across-context invariance central to simultaneous individuation of mechanisms and capacities \citep{WajnermanPazRojasLibano2026Bidimensional}. Their proposal gives a substantive answer about which forms of stability matter for capacity individuation: stability across scientifically relevant contexts can constrain both sides of the mechanism--capacity relation. The present contribution is complementary. It asks a second-order question about that stability: before it is assigned substantial identity-evidential force, is the apparent stability robust to preservation choices that remain live for the same identity question?

North's defense of objective or non-pragmatic perspicuity gives one way to discharge the burden \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. If a representation is independently shown to display the target structure more perspicuously than its rivals, that result can combine with explanatory success rather than being replaced by it. The burden principle is therefore not a thesis that every representation is equally interest-relative.

Brousalis makes epistemically relevant similarities and differences central to theory comparison \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}. The present claim adds a discrimination condition to that insight: when similarity is used for individuation, the evidential case should distinguish source-side recurrence from commonality introduced or amplified by the comparison framework. Francken, Slors, and Craver, Krickel, and Koh\'ar likewise show why abstraction, operationalization, mechanism, and system-boundary choices must remain tied to explanatory practice rather than silently hardening into a taxonomy \citep{FranckenSlorsCraver2022CognitiveOntology,Krickel2024CognitiveOntology,Kohar2025ScalingUp}.

The paper therefore targets a reconstruction-to-identity inference in a restricted sense: not every inference that uses successful reconstruction, but one in which reconstruction success does more evidential work than it can discriminate. The additional evidence may include the success itself; what matters is that the identity claim is supported by considerations capable of ruling out relevant framework-induced alternatives.

\section{Methods}

The empirical component is a designed literature stress test rather than a prevalence study. Sixty source-grounded scientific claims were selected across eight historical construct strata plus a predeclared challenge surface. Each claim was encoded in a machine-readable record containing source identity and locators, claim scope, typed nodes and relations, temporal and control information, approximation state, and bridge assumptions. A language model assisted initial extraction, but all retained records were reviewed by the author against source text; independent human extraction reliability was not established. Full selection, masking, chronology, and audit procedures are reported in the Supplementary Methods.

Comparison suppresses author identity, venue, citation metadata, historical construct labels, source-local identifiers, and free-text presentation. The declared working basis is
\[
B_{P2}=\{S,\Pi,K,O,T,C,Q,P\},
\]
used as a structural coordinate system rather than a cognitive ontology. Here \(S\) marks state-like carriers, \(\Pi\) partitions or subsystems, \(K\) typed dependencies or transitions, \(O\) observation or access structure, \(T\) temporal organization, \(C\) resource or capacity constraints, \(Q\) evaluation or relevance criteria, and \(P\) probes, interventions, or transformations. Coordinate presence does not itself imply capacity identity. The pre-specified whole-claim projection preserves typed topology, active basis coordinates, temporal and control states, approximation mode, and bridge-grounding multiplicity while forgetting source-authority and presentation metadata. All \(\binom{60}{2}=1770\) unordered pairs were compared under those fixed rules.

Universal whole-claim incomparability was preserved as a failed-granularity result rather than repaired. The whole-claim question was: \emph{can two complete reviewed claims be aligned under the fixed preservation contract?} The secondary analysis asked a different question: \emph{does a reusable typed substructure survive after an explicitly declared restriction of the claim?} It therefore changed the \emph{unit of reuse} rather than repairing the original verdict. The bounded forgetting operator permits restriction but not arbitrary rewiring or semantic relabeling, and successful bounded recurrence cannot retrospectively change a whole-claim verdict. Other procedural and formal diagnostics are reported in the Supplementary Methods and play no premise role in the philosophical argument.

Representation sensitivity was tested in two ways. An invertible refactorization asks whether reconstructability uniquely selects a primitive factorization; because this lossless control is secondary to the present identity argument, its details are confined to the Supplement. A deliberately lossy Coarse Process--Constraint Graph groups fine distinctions into broad structural families while retaining only coarse process, interface, constraint, and temporal information. The map was fixed before the journal-facing witnesses were selected, and both survival and collapse were reportable outcomes. These tests diagnose sensitivity of source-claim comparison; by the same-question condition above, they are not automatically capacity-identity defeaters.

The epistemic boundary is correspondingly narrow. Exact corpus counts support auditability and locate witnesses; they are not estimates of how cognitive science is distributed. The corpus shows that overlap verdicts can change under an independently motivated preservation rule. The philosophical argument adds the further condition that such a change becomes defeater-relevant only when the rival representations bear on the same \(Q,C\).

\section{Results}

\subsection{Whole-claim failure and bounded recurrence}
\label{sec:wholefail}

The pre-specified whole-claim comparator classified all 1,770 unordered pairs as incomparable. This is treated as a granularity diagnosis, not as evidence that cognitive capacities are globally incommensurable. The comparator is not degenerate by construction: constructed calibration cases reach direct, stateless-repair, and history-bearing-repair classes, and source-derived counterfactual perturbations likewise change repair class when a load-bearing interface or delay-spanning trace is hidden. The 1,770/1,770 result is therefore an observed failure of the pre-specified whole-claim granularity, not the only verdict the procedure can return. The subsequent bounded-reuse question is distinct: under its separately declared forgetting rule, 206 reusable structural subobjects survive, including 99 cross-stratum families. Thus complete-claim incomparability and recurrent bounded organization coexist on the same reviewed corpus; further controls and family counts are reported in the Supplementary Methods.

\subsection{What corpus representation sensitivity does---and does not---show}

A process-level witness avoids relying on the difference between past-directed content and dynamical dependence. Behrens et al.'s volatility-learning claim explicitly describes recent outcomes updating a current volatility-sensitive weighting that influences the next decision \citep{BehrensEtAl2007ValueInformation}. Friston's predictive-coding claim describes recurrent mismatch-driven changes in hierarchical cause estimates during perceptual inference \citep{Friston2005CorticalResponses}. The reviewed adjudications mark genuine process dynamics in both sources: Behrens et al. require a history window, recurrence, a future horizon, and time indexing; Friston requires recurrence, persistence, and time indexing.

Under the fine bounded representation, the pair has no exact shared bounded object. One selected family for Behrens et al. retains a history window plus time index; the corresponding Friston family retains persistence plus time index. Under the pre-specified CPCG projection, both enter the same coarse \(N+T\) signature because the projection records temporal presence while discarding the history-window--persistence distinction. Thus a zero fine overlap becomes positive coarse overlap without appealing to Tulving's subjective-time vocabulary. This witness was selected post hoc from the already frozen M11 collision output for exposition; it is not a new blinded test.

The licensed conclusion is deliberately limited. The Behrens--Friston case is \emph{corpus-based evidence of representation sensitivity at the source-claim level}. It does not by itself satisfy the Representational-Defeater Trigger, because no fixed identity question \(Q\) and individuation criterion \(C\) are supplied under which the fine and coarse representations are competing answers. The coarse claim that both sources contain temporally organized state dynamics and the fine claim that their temporal organizations differ can both be true. M25 therefore no longer infers a defeater trigger from a change of granularity alone.

The earlier Botvinick--Tulving example remains useful as a boundary case rather than as positive trigger evidence. Botvinick et al. describe past conflict dynamically influencing subsequent control \citep{BotvinickEtAl2001ConflictMonitoring}; Tulving characterizes episodic memory partly through past-directed subjective time \citep{Tulving2002EpisodicMemory}. Encoding the latter as a working \emph{history-window} feature is an analyst-imposed representation of past-directedness, not a source claim that episodic remembering is a dynamical history buffer. The fact that both can be projected to a generic temporal signature shows how easy it is for a coarse comparison to conflate distinct kinds of temporality. It therefore motivates the same-question restriction rather than serving as evidence that the restriction has already been met.

The effect remains corpus-wide. Applied to the complete bounded surface, the pre-specified CPCG projection maps 206 fine-grained reusable objects to 143 coarse signatures, and 63 of the 99 fine cross-stratum families collide with at least one other original family. These aggregate collisions establish neither scientific importance nor capacity identity. Information-losing maps are expected to create collisions. Their evidential role here is only to show that representation sensitivity is not confined to a hand-picked pair and hence must be handled explicitly whenever a collision is later recruited for a fixed identity question.

The lossless refactorization remains useful as a separate non-uniqueness control, but it carries less philosophical weight because no information is discarded; its full treatment is left to the Supplement. The philosophical work is done by the distinction between source-claim representation sensitivity and same-\(Q,C\) undercutting, together with the concrete two-system counterexample above.
\subsection{Secondary procedural robustness diagnostics}

A prospective source comparison remains a secondary non-selection control, and the pattern is partly built into the contrast between those criteria. Model replay remains a \emph{procedural robustness diagnostic}. These checks, together with formal audits, are reported only as supporting diagnostics in the Supplementary Methods; none supplies the philosophical premise or is treated as population evidence, independent human reliability, provider-independent replication, or a positive capacity taxonomy.

\section{Capacity-level consequences}

The claim-level analysis constrains capacity identity only through the evidential schema introduced above. A recurrent structural pattern is not yet a capacity-relevant invariant. It can play a substantial role in identity evidence only insofar as (i) the source claims are aligned to the relevant capacity-level target, (ii) there is a substantive bridge from the recurrent structure to that target under the declared criterion \(C\), and (iii) the evidential case satisfies the Discrimination Requirement. The cases below are illustrations of where those conditions can fail; they are not independent proofs of the requirement.


\subsection{Different labels can share bounded organization}

Duncan's work on object-based visual attention and Behrens et al.'s work on volatility-sensitive learning begin under sharply different historical labels \citep{Duncan1984SelectiveAttention,BehrensEtAl2007ValueInformation}. Their complete structured claims remain incomparable. At a separately declared bounded granularity, however, both instantiate the same general organization: a probe maps to an outcome through typed conditional structure. Because construct labels are excluded from the comparison, this is not a lexical match.

The inference stops there. The shared probe-to-outcome pattern is deliberately low-complexity and could recur widely; it may reflect experimental organization as readily as capacity structure. Their fuller role profiles differ, and their complete claims remain incomparable. The licensed conclusion is therefore modest: label difference does not by itself justify a capacity split, and the comparison identifies a possible bounded structural motif for further cross-capacity analysis; it does not generate an identity verdict or, by itself, a substantive bridge hypothesis.

\subsection{A shared label need not supply the bridge}

Tulving's episodic-memory analysis and Patterson, Nestor, and Rogers' account of semantic/conceptual knowledge both sit near the historical label \emph{semantic memory}, but the label plays different roles in the two source claims \citep{Tulving2002EpisodicMemory,PattersonNestorRogers2007Semantic}. In Tulving's analysis, the primary target is a past-oriented episodic subsystem, with a knowledge-oriented subsystem entering as a contrast and dependence. In Patterson et al., semantic/conceptual knowledge is itself the direct target of an architectural claim involving distributed representations, convergence, and cross-modal generalization.

The bounded structural supports of the two claims are disjoint under the fine representation and remain disjoint under the tested coarse projection. This does \emph{not} show that semantic memory is not one capacity. It diagnoses a failure earlier in the evidential schema: the two source claims are not automatically target-aligned merely because the same historical label appears in their vicinity. Label recurrence therefore cannot supply the bridge by itself.

A unified semantic-memory thesis would therefore need additional evidence that aligns the targets of the two descriptions, states the granularity at which their organizations are to count as evidence about the same capacity, specifies what is preserved across the bridge, and explains why the chosen representation is appropriate for the identity question. The capacity thesis remains open, but the preservation debt is explicit.

\subsection{Different questions are not yet rival identity representations}

The Botvinick et al.--Tulving comparison makes the same-question restriction concrete \citep{BotvinickEtAl2001ConflictMonitoring,Tulving2002EpisodicMemory}. Under the fine bounded representation the pair has no exact bounded overlap; under the coarse temporal projection it acquires one. But the two descriptions answer different questions: the coarse representation asks only whether some temporal organization is present, whereas the fine representation preserves how temporal organization is implemented or encoded. Those claims can both be true. Without a fixed identity question \(Q\) and criterion \(C\) under which that forgotten distinction changes the support for the same capacity-identity inference, this is representation sensitivity, not a Representational-Defeater Trigger. The case is retained precisely to mark that boundary.
\section{Worked example: from source evidence to a capacity constraint}

The Duncan--Behrens comparison can be followed through the complete analysis without consulting the repository. Duncan's source evidence relates report accuracy to whether attributes belong to the same or different perceptual units; Behrens et al. relate outcome history and inferred environmental change to subsequent choice \citep{Duncan1984SelectiveAttention,BehrensEtAl2007ValueInformation}. The structured records preserve those source-specific organizations before any bounded comparison is attempted.

At the whole-claim level, the two organizations are incomparable. The analysis then changes the question rather than changing that verdict: under the separately declared bounded forgetting rule, both instantiate the same probe-to-outcome organization with typed conditional dependence. That organization also recurs elsewhere in the corpus, so it is not an ad hoc resemblance invented for this pair.

Restoring the surrounding structure reintroduces the differences. The licensed inference is therefore only that differently labelled attention and learning claims can contain the same bounded motif, so label difference alone cannot settle a capacity split. What is not licensed is the stronger claim that object-based attention and volatility-sensitive learning are one capacity, or even that this particular motif is capacity-constitutive. Further comparison would first need to show that the motif is not merely inherited from experimental organization and is relevant under a declared identity criterion.

\section{Boundary conditions on the comparison}

The comparison does not supply a universal criterion for where a cognitive system ends. Internal transformation, cross-boundary coupling, and experimental scheduling are kept distinct so that an experimental condition or environmental state is not silently promoted to an internal cognitive role. System boundaries therefore remain application-relative, a point compatible with dynamical, embodied, enactive, and extended approaches. The explicit system--environment--experiment typing and the treatment of experiment start as a cut through a longer history are preserved in the Supplementary Methods.

\section{What could satisfy the Discrimination Requirement?}

The requirement is deliberately permissive about the form of evidence. What matters is not that the evidence come from a different dataset, but that the total evidential package discriminate among relevant explanations of the structural similarity for the same \(Q,C\).

A mechanistic result can do this if the recurrent structure is shown to constitute or organize the target capacity rather than merely describe it. Intervention or causal evidence can discriminate if manipulating the proposed invariant changes the capacity-relevant phenomenon in the predicted way. Novel prediction can matter when success depends specifically on the proposed invariant rather than on the general flexibility of the framework. A contrastive representation test can matter when plausible rivals fail to recover the same organization. North-style objective perspicuity can matter if one representation is independently better aligned with target structure \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. Cross-representational convergence can matter when a framework-guided and differently constructed source-first route recover the same organization.

A limited positive illustration already occurs within the analyzed literature. Patterson, Nestor, and Rogers do not merely redescribe semantic knowledge in a preferred architecture; the shared-convergence architecture yields a differential lesion prediction, namely that relatively focal damage to the common convergence system should produce impairment generalized across input and output modalities and item classes, and the review evaluates semantic-dementia evidence against that prediction \citep{PattersonNestorRogers2007Semantic}. This is not a successful cross-source capacity-identity verdict and is not presented as one. It illustrates the relevant evidential form: a structural proposal gains weight because it makes a discriminating empirical commitment against an architectural rival, rather than merely because the source can be reconstructed in its vocabulary.

These possibilities locate the present proposal relative to nearby work. Wajnerman-Paz and Rojas-L\'ibano use across-context invariance to constrain mechanism and capacity individuation \citep{WajnermanPazRojasLibano2026Bidimensional}; their stability criterion operates at the object level, whereas the Discrimination Requirement adds a second-order question about whether the observed stability itself survives relevant representational preservation choices before it is assigned individuative weight. Brousalis emphasizes epistemically relevant similarities and differences in theory comparison \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}; the present condition specifies what relevance must accomplish when similarity is used for identity. Onishi and Serpico show why natural-kind boundaries can remain interest-relative \citep{OnishiSerpico2022HPC}; the present proposal does not remove inquiry-relative choices but requires their individuative use to survive relevant alternatives. Krickel and Koh\'ar likewise show why mechanistic usefulness need not mechanically determine a taxonomy \citep{Krickel2024CognitiveOntology,Kohar2025ScalingUp}.

Beni remains the clearest positive application because PP--FEP is offered as a substantive unifying framework rather than a neutral notation \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. The bridge from Beni's natural-kind setting to cognitive-capacity identity is inferential rather than ontological: in both cases, an invariant exposed within a representation is recruited as evidence for sameness of a scientific kind or capacity. The Discrimination Requirement applies to that shared promotion step, not because consciousness kinds and cognitive capacities are assumed to have the same metaphysics. A direct comparison between PP--FEP-guided reconstructions and the source-first structures studied here would provide especially strong evidence about the source of any convergence, but the general claim does not stand or fall with that future comparison.

\section{Limitations and evidential boundary}

The corpus is purposive and diagnostic. It is not a probability sample, so neither the frequency of structural roles nor the frequency of prospective reconstruction outcomes should be interpreted as population prevalence. The exact counts are useful for auditability and for locating counterexamples, but the philosophical argument depends primarily on the existence of the representation-sensitivity witnesses.

Independent human re-adjudication was not performed. The retained source records were reviewed against source text, and the model replays provide a separate procedural check, but neither substitutes for independent human coding. Procedural auditability is established; inter-rater reliability remains unmeasured.

The modeled system boundary is application-relative rather than a discovered universal cognitive boundary. Structural typing helps prevent internal transformation, environmental coupling, and experimental scheduling from being silently conflated, but it does not determine which boundary every scientifically appropriate theory must adopt.

Only one deliberately lossy rival representation is used in the corpus sensitivity analysis. Its temporal abstraction has an independent modeling rationale in temporal factorisation, but that does not establish that it is the correct representation of any source or that every reasonable rival would behave similarly. More importantly, a coarse/fine disagreement that answers different questions is not yet an identity defeater. The corpus result establishes representation sensitivity; same-question defeater relevance must be argued separately.

The prospective 40-paper analysis is a non-selection control, not an independent random holdout. The papers were purposively chosen for architectural coverage, and the analysis does not support binomial significance or population inference. Its role is limited to showing that successful reconstruction persists under weaker encoding-capable views and therefore cannot by itself privilege the full working basis.

The formal verification checks declared typing, dependencies, and transformation rules. It is an audit layer rather than an empirical validation layer: it can expose inconsistent formal commitments but cannot establish empirical adequacy, cognitive truth, or a uniquely correct ontology.

Finally, the claim-anchored procedural robustness diagnostic was designed after the initial unanchored replay exposed a claim-selection confound. It is therefore prospective relative to its own outputs but not independent confirmation of the earlier diagnosis. The resulting same-source compatibility is informative about decomposition reproducibility under the tested conditions, not about unseen-source generalization, provider independence, or human reliability. The subsequent source correction was kept separate from the historical replay score and was subjected to a deterministic sensitivity analysis rather than used to rescore the robustness diagnostic post hoc.

\section{Discussion}

The paper's central contribution is the \emph{Discrimination Requirement}. Explanatory and reconstruction success is genuine evidence for a candidate framework, and a representation-relative structural similarity can still contribute weak support. The requirement applies when that similarity plays a substantial role in a fixed identity inference. The associated Representational Defeater is an undercutting defeater only when a live alternative bears on the same \(Q,C\) and changes the evidential status of that similarity. Mere disagreement between different legitimate questions is not enough.

The representation tests have a deliberately limited role. The lossless refactorization, detailed in the Supplement, shows only that reconstructability does not uniquely select primitive factorization. The Behrens--Friston process-level witness shows that a lossy preservation rule can create overlap between source-grounded dynamic claims without relying on a past-content/process conflation. Botvinick--Tulving is retained as a caution that source compatibility at a weak granularity does not make two temporal descriptions rivals for the same identity question. The full defeater logic is supplied by the concrete two-system counterexample, where \(Q\) and \(C\) are held fixed.

The relation to Beni is constructive rather than eliminative \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. PP--FEP may have substantial abductive support as a unifying framework, and Beni's empirical natural-kind constraints may in principle supply exactly the discrimination required here. The question is not whether empirical constraints exist, but whether they bear specifically on why the invariants recruited for kind individuation track the target rather than merely the redescription. A systematic PP--FEP/source-first alignment would be especially informative, but the philosophical contribution is not a Beni-specific empirical result.

The same point clarifies the relation to North and Brousalis. North shows how a representation might be privileged by objective perspicuity rather than mere reconstructability \citep{North2026Perspicuous}; such an argument can satisfy the requirement if it discriminates relevant alternatives. Brousalis emphasizes epistemically relevant similarity \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}; the Discrimination Requirement specifies what such relevance must do in an individuation argument: it must bear on why the similarity should be attributed to the target rather than to the comparison setup.

The capacity-level illustrations expose different missing terms in the evidential bridge. Duncan--Behrens identifies a low-complexity bounded motif but leaves both target relevance and bridge warrant open. Tulving--Patterson shows that label continuity does not secure target alignment. Botvinick--Tulving shows why a coarse/fine difference must not be mistaken for a same-question rival merely because overlap changes. The paper therefore does not offer a positive theory of capacity identity. It offers a necessary condition on when claim-level structural similarity may play a substantial role in a capacity-level identity argument---an evidential condition on that use, not a condition on capacity identity simpliciter.

The larger corpus, prospective reconstruction study, formal audits, and model replays remain supporting procedural diagnostics. Their exact counts matter for auditability, not for the novelty claim. The philosophical result would survive if those counts were less visually striking: what matters is the demonstrated separation between reconstructability, preservation, and individuation, and the resulting requirement that identity evidence discriminate among relevant sources of apparent invariance.

\section{Conclusion}

Explanatory success and successful structural reconstruction are evidence. The Discrimination Requirement does not deny that an undiscriminated structural similarity may weakly support an identity hypothesis. It constrains a more specific use: when that similarity plays a substantial role in answering a fixed capacity-identity question under a declared criterion, the evidential package must discriminate target-supported commonality from commonality introduced or amplified by the comparison representation, preservation rule, or granularity.

This requirement is not a complete theory of capacity identity or individuation. Claim-level structural recurrence becomes capacity-level evidence only when target alignment, a capacity-relevant bridge warrant, and representational discrimination are also in place relative to a declared \(Q,C\). The same evidence may satisfy more than one of these roles. Mechanistic evidence, intervention, novel prediction, contrastive representation tests, objective perspicuity, and cross-representational convergence are among the ways the required discrimination might be supplied.

Beni provides an important application rather than the sole target. Framework-guided PP--FEP unification and source-first reconstruction can be treated as complementary evidential routes. Convergence would strengthen the case that an invariant is not merely framework-enabled; divergence would identify where representational contribution requires explanation. Performing that systematic alignment would extend the present argument, not supply a missing premise for it.

The paper does not establish a universal cognitive ontology, a representation-neutral identity criterion, a uniquely perspicuous basis, population prevalence, unseen-source replay generalization, or independent coding reliability. Its positive contribution is a necessary evidential condition on structurally supported identity inferences: identity-relevant structural similarity must be discriminated from similarity that the comparison procedure itself helps create. Procedural auditability is established; inter-rater reliability remains unmeasured.
