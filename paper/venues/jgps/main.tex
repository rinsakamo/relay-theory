\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cognitive ontology; structural comparison; cognitive capacities; pre-individuation; representation; philosophy of cognitive science


\begin{abstract}

Scientific claims about cognition are routinely grouped by inherited construct labels or unified within common formal representations. This paper asks when a structural comparison licenses an inference about cognitive-capacity identity. Its central claim is that such an inference is epistemically licensed only if the comparison representation, preservation criterion, and granularity are specified and warranted for that inferential use. Specification makes the comparison inspectable; warrant requires a non-circular reason for treating what is preserved as identity-relevant rather than merely reconstructable. The direct objects of analysis are source-grounded scientific claims rather than capacities themselves. A representation-sensitive case provides the clearest result: ATT03 and MEM04 have zero bounded overlap under the fine working representation but positive overlap after a deliberately lossy coarse projection. A lossless invertible refactorization independently shows that successful representation does not privilege the exact role inventory. Capacity-level applications then show both that a shared historical label can lack a preserved bridge and that different labels can share bounded organization without thereby denoting the same capacity. A prospective 40-paper study is retained as a secondary non-selection control: direct reconstruction succeeds under several weaker encoding-capable representations. The result is an epistemic constraint on identity inference, not a natural-kind theory or a representation-neutral ontology. Independent human inter-rater validation was not performed.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cognitive science inherits a large vocabulary of capacities: memory, attention, learning, belief, skill, prediction, control, concepts, and many others. These labels are indispensable for communication and literature organization, but they can also acquire evidential force before the comparison that would justify that force has been made explicit. The same problem arises in the opposite direction when a formal framework reconstructs heterogeneous models successfully: representational unification can look like evidence of a shared capacity structure even when the relevant notion of sameness has not yet been stated.

The construct-validity tradition already denies that a construct validates itself by being named \citep{CronbachMeehl1955ConstructValidity,BorsboomMellenberghVanHeerden2004Validity}, and cognitive-ontology projects explicitly represent relations among concepts, tasks, and measurements \citep{PoldrackEtAl2011CognitiveAtlas,TurnerLaird2012CogPO,PoldrackYarkoni2016CognitiveOntologies}. The question here is narrower and more demanding: \emph{what must be true of a comparison before the resulting similarity or difference can count as evidence for capacity individuation?}

The paper's central thesis concerns epistemic entitlement rather than the existence of evidence in the abstract: \emph{An inference from structural comparison to cognitive-capacity identity is epistemically licensed only if the comparison representation, preservation criterion, and granularity are specified and warranted for that inferential use.} Specification is a transparency condition: a reader must be able to inspect what counts as preserved and what is allowed to disappear. Warrant is a further, non-circular condition: the identity relevance of the preserved structure cannot be supplied solely by the reconstruction success whose significance is under dispute. Here \emph{independent} means inferentially independent of that success, not necessarily statistically independent or drawn from a wholly separate dataset. Cognitive-capacity labels are at most prima facie guides to individuation, and the working basis is not claimed to be uniquely correct or representation-neutral.

The primary target is a \emph{reconstruction-to-identity inference} (RII): successful unification or reconstruction within one representation is allowed to support a claim about capacity identity before the identity relevance of the preserved structure has been warranted. Two familiar failure modes are treated as corollaries rather than co-equal targets. A \emph{terminological carryover inference} (TCI) lets an inherited label contribute continuity without a separately justified bridge; a \emph{local-invariance promotion} (LIP) lets invariance within one chosen representation or transformation class stand in for identity without an argument that this is the relevant invariance. The paper does not attribute these forms wholesale to any single author.

These inference forms are live because stronger philosophical projects need some account of which similarities matter. Mechanistic cognitive ontology faces operationalization, abstraction, and boundary problems \citep{FranckenSlorsCraver2022CognitiveOntology}; invariance-based accounts make preservation across contexts central to mechanism/capacity identity \citep{WajnermanPazRojasLibano2026Bidimensional}; and natural-kind analyses show that causal delineation can remain interest-relative \citep{OnishiSerpico2022HPC}. Beni's structural-realist proposal for consciousness science provides a particularly useful positive contrast: predictive processing is offered as a candidate formal basis for identifying invariant relations across competing theories \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. The present argument does not reject such a candidate. It asks what evidential burden must be met before successful structural unification is used as identity evidence.

North's argument for objectively more perspicuous physical representations sharpens the issue further \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. Nothing here rules out objective or non-pragmatic perspicuity. On the contrary, a perspicuity argument could supply exactly the additional warrant that successful encoding lacks. The present method is prior to that verdict: it exposes what a representation preserves, what rivals collapse, and which comparative judgments change, without claiming that the working basis thereby mirrors reality more directly. Brousalis likewise makes epistemically relevant similarities and differences central to theory comparison \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}. The preservation criterion here is a bounded way of making such relevance explicit before a similarity is allowed to do normative evidential work.

The direct analytical objects are source-grounded \emph{scientific claims about cognitive capacities}. The inferential relation is therefore
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{claim-level structural comparison}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{evidential constraint on capacity comparison}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{capacity individuation}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Most of the analysis concerns the first arrow. One downstream semantic-memory application is then carried further: it shows how the procedure changes the status of an actual capacity-level grouping from label-supported continuity to an open thesis carrying explicit preservation debt.

The method suppresses historical construct labels and selected source-authority metadata at the comparison stage, preserves the failure of an initially over-discriminating whole-claim comparison, asks a separately declared bounded-reuse question, reverse-projects the surviving structure into a compact role grammar, and tests representation sensitivity and prospective reconstruction. The procedure is therefore \emph{construct-label-suppressed at the comparison stage}, not theory-neutral or representation-neutral.

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the reconstruction sequence. Its important feature is not the amount of audit machinery but the separation of comparison questions: a failure at one granularity is retained rather than silently relaxed, and a success under one representation is not automatically promoted to representation-independent identity.

\section{From specification to licensed identity inference}

Three layers must be kept apart. First, a comparison must be \emph{specified}: its representation, preservation criterion, and granularity must be explicit enough to determine what counts as sameness and what may disappear. Second, those choices must be \emph{warranted for the identity inference}: there must be a reason to treat the preserved structure as relevant to the capacity boundary at issue. Third, the observed comparison result must actually support the particular capacity-level conclusion being drawn.

The second requirement is the central philosophical claim. It is a non-circularity condition. If representation \(R\) reconstructs two source claims successfully, that success cannot by itself establish that the structures privileged by \(R\) are the ones that constitute, individuate, or track the relevant capacity. Otherwise the same fact---success under \(R\)---both fixes the comparison dimensions and certifies their identity relevance. Nor does specification alone solve the problem: an entirely explicit but overly fine representation may preserve irrelevant differences, while an entirely explicit coarse projection may erase differences that matter.

The burden can be discharged in more than one way. A mechanistic argument may show that the preserved organization constitutes the phenomenon; a causal argument may identify capacity-relevant difference-makers; a cross-context or intervention-sensitive invariance argument may show that the same organization remains stable under independently motivated changes; an explanatory argument may show that the preserved structure carries the relevant explanatory work; and an objective-perspicuity argument may show that one representation is better aligned with the target structure. These routes need not use statistically independent data. What they must not do is merely redescribe reconstruction success as its own warrant.

The empirical analyses below therefore function principally as diagnostics of this inferential burden. They show that both overlap and non-overlap can change with representation and granularity, while some conclusions remain stable. Scientific-representation theory supplies the general background: representational equivalence and surplus structure depend on what a mapping preserves \citep{Nguyen2017RepresentationEquivalence,NguyenTehWells2020Surplus,FriggNguyen2020ModellingNature,NguyenFrigg2022ScientificRepresentation}.

\section{A live dialectical target: which invariance can individuate?}

Beni's structural-realist proposal for consciousness science makes the RII problem concrete rather than hypothetical \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. Heterogeneous theories may be mapped into a shared formal framework; predictive processing and the free-energy framework are offered as a candidate basis for exposing invariant relational organization; and such invariance may guide theory convergence and kind individuation. The present argument accepts the methodological value of that project. It does not argue against PP/FEP and does not require that Beni treat the candidate formalism as uniquely final.

The additional question is narrower: \emph{what licenses the move from invariance exposed by a candidate representation to identity-bearing invariance?} A formally coherent common representation can reveal similarities without yet establishing that those similarities mark the relevant natural or cognitive boundary. The problem is not that the invariance is unreal; it is that its identity relevance requires an argument about why this representation, transformation class, and granularity preserve what matters.

Wajnerman-Paz and Rojas-L\'ibano sharpen the same issue from cognitive ontology by making across-context invariance central to simultaneous individuation of mechanisms and capacities \citep{WajnermanPazRojasLibano2026Bidimensional}. Their proposal gives a substantive answer to the question \emph{which stability should matter?}: stability across scientifically relevant contexts can constrain both sides of the mechanism--capacity relation. The present contribution is meta-level and complementary. Before an observed invariance can license a capacity-identity inference, one must still say which changes count as relevant contexts or transformations, which features are preserved across them, at what granularity, and why those choices track the identity question rather than merely the convenience of a representation.

This framing turns independent warrant into a tractable demand rather than a request for an entirely separate evidence stream. Mechanistic constitution, causal intervention, empirically motivated cross-context invariance, explanatory indispensability, or North-style objective perspicuity could each provide a non-circular reason for privileging a preserved structure \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. Brousalis supplies the parallel normative lesson for theory comparison: similarities and differences acquire epistemic force only when the comparison dimensions are relevant to the inference being made \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}. Francken, Slors, and Craver, Krickel, and Koh\'ar likewise show why abstraction, operationalization, mechanism, and system boundary choices must remain tied to concrete explanatory practice rather than silently hardening into a taxonomy \citep{FranckenSlorsCraver2022CognitiveOntology,Krickel2024CognitiveOntology,Kohar2025ScalingUp}.

RII is therefore the paper's primary target. TCI and LIP are derivative warnings: labels cannot pay the structural bridge by themselves, and invariance inside one transformation class cannot be promoted beyond that class without further warrant. The cases below are not offered as a competing ontology. They are tests of what these inferential moves permit.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Designed corpus}

The bounded empirical target contains 60 claims. Forty-eight primary claims are divided evenly across eight historical construct strata. Twelve additional claims form a non-tuning challenge surface.

The primary strata are Memory, Learning, Skill, Attention, Prediction, Control, Belief, and Concept. Challenge pressures include multi-timescale organization, stochastic or approximate structure, constitutive boundary pressure, compositional or systematic structure, embodied or world-coupled organization, normative or criterion structure, hybrid labels, label-coordinate mismatch, multi-condition structure, same-inventory/different-topology pressure, strict-refinement pressure, and high structural heterogeneity. Challenge claims were not permitted to tune the structured-record schema, working basis, or comparison semantics.

This is a literature-based structural comparison, not an experiment or prevalence study. Its role is instead adversarial and structural: the 48 primary claims provide balanced coverage across historically familiar construct strata, while the 12 challenge claims probe predeclared pressures that could break the representation. Consequently, the supported inference is whether reusable structure survives this designed stress-test surface under pre-specified rules, not how prevalent any role or reusable structural subobject is in cognitive science as a whole.

\subsection{Source-grounded structured claim records}

Each selected source claim was represented in a machine-readable structured claim record containing stable source identity, source-local evidence locators, claim scope and modality, typed nodes, typed relations, temporal information, control information, approximation state, and bridge assumptions.

A language model assisted initial structured extraction, but its output was not treated as authority. Every retained record was reviewed against source text, and all 60 current records carry reviewed status. The scientific input to downstream analysis is therefore the source-audited structured claim corpus.

Independent extraction reliability was not established. Historical local two-pass calibration transactions produced zero valid structured claim records in both the v2 and v3 real calibration runs, leaving no valid A/B agreement denominator. The present study consequently claims auditability of the retained corpus, not inter-model agreement, autonomous extraction reliability, or deterministic language-model replay.

\subsection{Pre-specification and anti-circularity}

The comparison basis, whole-claim projection, and bounded-reconstruction rules were fixed before later grammar and prospective outcomes were used to interpret them. Reverse projection did not reread source papers or rewrite the reviewed claim records to improve grammar coverage, and residuals were retained rather than repaired after inspection. The purpose of this staging is not to remove author judgment, which remains present in source review and structural adjudication, but to prevent the final grammar from simultaneously defining and validating its own input representation. Exact repository chronology, immutable hashes, and stage-level provenance are reported in the Supplementary Methods rather than used as argumentative premises in the main text.

\subsection{Generative AI and human accountability}

OpenAI ChatGPT assisted structured extraction, source-analysis workflow, software development, and manuscript drafting. Model output was not treated as source authority or as an independent adjudicator. Every retained claim in the 60-claim reference corpus was reviewed by the author against source text, and the prospective study tied adjudications to explicit source locations and versioned artifacts. Independent extraction reliability and independent human re-adjudication were not established; these remain distinct limitations. Full disclosure and workflow provenance are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

\subsection{Pre-specified structural basis}

The structural comparison suppresses source information that should not determine similarity merely by appearing in a paper. Author identity, institution, venue, citation count, historical construct label, source-local identifiers, and free-text presentation are excluded from comparison.

The pre-specified working basis is
\[
B_{P2}=\{S,\Pi,K,O,T,C,Q,P\},
\]
interpreted as a declared structural coordinate system for comparison rather than an ontology or a representation-neutral basis. Lexical shortcuts are forbidden: a source node named \emph{condition} is not automatically \(C\); a reward-like term does not exhaust \(Q\); temporal precedence is not automatically \(K\); and a partition need not be physical.

\subsection{Whole-claim comparison}

The pre-specified \(\Phi\) projection preserves claim type, modality, scope shape, typed topology, active basis coordinates, temporal presence states, control states, approximation mode, and bridge-grounding multiplicity. It forgets source authority and presentation metadata.

Pairwise relations distinguish equivalence, refinement, forgetting, incomparability, and mutual-embed non-isomorphism. For 60 claims, the unordered comparison surface contains
\[
\binom{60}{2}=1770
\]
pairs.

\subsection{Secondary bounded reconstruction}

Because whole-claim comparison failed universally, the next analysis changed the \emph{unit of reuse}, not the pre-specified source representation. The whole-claim result therefore remains the primary result for the original \(\Phi\)-level question. A secondary post-atlas reconstruction then asked a different question: whether bounded substructures could recur even when complete scientific claims remained incomparable. In that chronological sense the reconstruction is post-atlas; it is not presented as if it had been the successful primary comparison from the outset.

The bounded reconstruction searched for basis-subobjects recoverable under a pre-specified forgetting operator. The operator permits restriction to retained typed substructure but forbids arbitrary rewiring and semantic relabeling. Thus the secondary analysis does not revise the 1,770 whole-claim verdicts, and successful bounded recurrence cannot be used to retrospectively relabel a failed whole-claim pair as equivalent.

Negative and ablation controls tested trivial-core collapse, label dependence, permissive forgetting, richer competing cores, overlapping incomparable cores, cross-label recurrence, compatibility with whole-claim incomparability, and presentation invariance. No post-outcome abstraction threshold was added to manufacture recurrence.

\subsection{Structural role grammar and adversarial tests}

Surviving bounded structures motivated a compact structural role grammar (version 0):
\[
\Gsys=\{\Pi,X,C,Q,\Pin,\Pout,K,T,\rho/O\}.
\]
Here \(X\) is a generic configuration carrier; \(C\) is realizability/admissibility structure; \(Q\) is criterion-conditioned orientation; \(\Pin\) and \(\Pout\) are directional interface roles; \(K\) is typed transformation or dependence; \(T\) is succession or temporal indexing; and \(\rho/O\) is boundary-relative observation or trace.

A mnemonic representation is
\[
X_{n+1}=K_{n;C,Q,\Pin}(X_n),
\qquad
O_n=\rho_{\Pi,C,\Pout,n}(X_n),
\]
without assuming that every claim instantiates every role.

All 60 reviewed claims were reverse-projected into this structural role grammar without rereading source papers or changing the structured claim records, \(B_{P2}\), \(\Phi\), structural adjudications, or reusable-subobject identities. Residuals were classified under a pre-specified taxonomy in which only an unexplained requirement for an additional top-level system role counted as prima facie evidence for extending the role inventory.

Two strict distinction-preservation comparators were tested:
\[
G_{\mathrm{dyn}}=\{X,K,T\},
\qquad
G_{\mathrm{pomdp}}=\{X,P,K,\rho/O,Q,T\}.
\]
These are distinction-loss tests, not non-encodability theorems.


\subsection{Prospective non-selection control}

After the original 60-claim analyses and structural role grammar were fixed, a separate no-substitution roster of 40 cognitive-model papers was reconstructed under the same source-faithful A-state semantics. All 40 were A0: none required reconstruction-added stateless or persistent coordination beyond mechanisms already specified by the source. This remains a source-grounded, single-adjudicator finding; exact DOI separation from the original corpus is not treated as genealogical independence.

For the present argument, the important result is negative. Under the original encoding-permissive semantics, a dynamic backbone, a POMDP-like view, and four single-role ablations also leave all 40 cases at A0. Successful direct reconstruction therefore does not select the exact role inventory. Under a stricter direct-preservation test, the same six weaker representations incur stateless preservation debt because omitted distinctions must be re-exposed. The two controls answer different questions and are reported in full in the Supplementary Methods. Here they serve only to show that reconstruction success and representational privilege are not the same evidential achievement.

\subsection{Lossless non-uniqueness and a genuinely lossy rival representation}

Two different representation-sensitivity questions are kept separate. First, an invertible Tagged Admissibility--Interface Factorization (TAIF) tests whether the exact nine first-class roles are unique when the same information is losslessly refactored. Second, a Coarse Process--Constraint Graph (CPCG) asks whether higher-order bounded-reuse conclusions survive a deliberately information-losing representation.

CPCG has five coarse families: \(N\) for carrier/component structure, merging state-like and partition/boundary structure; \(G\) for guard/orientation, merging constraints and criteria; \(I\) for interface events, merging probes/interventions with observations/readouts; \(E\) for non-temporal transformation/dependence; and \(T\) for temporal presence/order. Fine node-role strings, working-basis control subtypes, non-temporal relation kinds, and detailed temporal subtypes are discarded. The map is therefore non-injective by construction. No retention threshold was declared: survival and collapse are both reportable sensitivity outcomes.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Whole-claim comparison diagnoses excessive granularity}
\label{sec:wholefail}

The pre-specified whole-claim comparator classified all 1,770 unordered pairs as incomparable. In the corresponding 3,540 directed comparisons, 3,518 stopped at the scalar stage, six reached node-count comparison, 16 reached node-label multiset comparison, and none reached topology or embedding.

The philosophical use of this result is therefore diagnostic rather than triumphalist. The rich whole-claim representation distinguishes the corpus so early that it supplies almost no reusable equivalence structure. Universal incomparability is retained because it identifies a failed comparison granularity; it is not presented as evidence that cognitive capacities are globally incommensurable or that graph topology separates every claim.

\subsection{Comparator reachability controls}

The universal whole-claim incomparability result raises an immediate sanity question: can the comparator ever return a positive relation? Three pre-frozen constructed controls answer this without adding corpus evidence. An identifier/presentation renaming of the same structural claim returns \textsc{equivalent}. A presentation-only metadata variant, changing source-facing metadata that the frozen projection explicitly forgets while leaving scientific structure untouched, also returns \textsc{equivalent}. Finally, adding one authorized fixed resource distinction returns \textsc{right-strict-refinement-of-left}, with the forgotten \(C\) axis and resource control recorded in the witness. These are \emph{CONSTRUCTED\_CONTROL} results. They establish reachability of equivalence and refinement states under the comparator; they do not validate a cognitive taxonomy or weaken the frozen \(1770/1770\) corpus result.

\subsection{Bounded reusable structure reappears}

The secondary bounded reconstruction produced 213 within-stratum candidate subobjects and 206 unique reusable structural subobjects. Seven exact cross-stratum equivalence components survived. The global refinement structure contains 470 direct refinement edges and 2,894 strict subobject relations in transitive closure.

The bounded family structure contains
\[
99\ \text{cross-stratum families}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
107\ \text{within-stratum-only families},
\]
with 188 distinct support signatures. Fifty-nine of 60 claims are covered by at least one surviving bounded object. All eight negative and ablation controls pass.

Notably, 1,304 whole-claim pairs share at least one surviving reusable structural subobject despite every whole-claim pair being held as incomparable. Whole-claim identity and reusable substructure are therefore distinct questions on the same corpus.

\subsection{Reverse projection is secondary to the identity argument}

Reverse projection of the frozen 60 claims into the compact role grammar yields 21 FULL, 22 PARTIAL, and 17 RESIDUAL cases. None of the 17 residuals requires a new top-level role under the frozen adjudication. These results remain useful as an audit of the working representation, but they are not evidence that the exact role inventory is uniquely correct. Detailed role frequencies, residual classes, and strict distinction-preservation comparator results are therefore moved to the Supplementary Methods rather than treated as first-class philosophical results.

\subsection{Decisive representation tests: TAIF and CPCG}

The first decisive test is lossless. TAIF is an invertible refactorization of the working representation: it preserves the information needed to reconstruct the original first-class role structure while changing how that structure is factored. The resulting non-uniqueness is enough to block an inference from successful reconstruction to the claim that the exact nine-role inventory is privileged. Lossless recoding need not change the represented scientific content to change which distinctions appear primitive.

The second test is deliberately lossy. CPCG projects fine bounded objects into a coarser signature. Under the working representation, ATT03 and MEM04 have zero bounded overlap. Under CPCG, the ATT03 object \(A\)-53b16535e1c2 and MEM04 object \(A\)-af97ed649c6f collapse to the same coarse temporal-state signature, producing one projected overlap. No capacity identity follows. The point is prior: even the truth of the sentence \emph{these claims share structure} depends on a preservation contract unless the relevant sense of structure has been fixed.

Together, TAIF and the ATT03--MEM04 CPCG case motivate the burden principle more directly than the raw corpus totals. TAIF shows that successful representation underdetermines which factorization is first-class; CPCG shows that an overlap judgment can change when information is intentionally discarded. The question for capacity individuation is therefore not simply whether an invariance or overlap can be exhibited, but why the representation and transformations that exhibit it should govern the identity inference.

\subsection{Secondary prospective control}

The separate 40-paper study supports the same conclusion from another direction. All 40 sources remain directly reconstructable not only under the working basis but also under six weaker encoding-capable views. Under the stricter direct-preservation semantics those weaker views incur stateless preservation debt. This is informative about the relation between encodability and declared preservation, but it is not the paper's primary evidence for the burden principle. Full A-state definitions, tables, and paper-level provenance are retained in the Supplementary Methods and repository.

\section{Capacity-level adjudication}

\subsection{Different labels, shared bounded structure: ATT01 and BLF01}

The converse of the semantic-memory case is supplied by ATT01 and BLF01. ATT01 is Duncan's \emph{Selective attention and the organization of visual information} (DOI 10.1037/0096-3445.113.4.501) \citep{Duncan1984SelectiveAttention}; BLF01 is Behrens et al.'s \emph{Learning the value of information in an uncertain world} (DOI 10.1038/nn1954) \citep{BehrensEtAl2007ValueInformation}. Their inherited labels differ sharply: the former is organized around object-based visual attention, whereas the latter is organized around volatility-sensitive learning and decision updating. Label difference therefore supplies no automatic evidence of non-identity.

Their complete claims remain incomparable under the frozen whole-claim comparator. At a separately declared bounded granularity, however, both directly support object \(A\)-3b814de4da04: a probe \(P\) maps to a response/outcome \(O\) through typed \(K\) structure. This is not a lexical match; the historical labels are excluded from the calculation. The overlap is therefore positive evidence for reusable organization across the two differently labelled claims.

The inference stops there. ATT01's fuller role projection is \(\{\Pi,Q,P_{\mathrm{out}},K,\rho/O\}\) and is partial under the role grammar, whereas BLF01's is \(\{X,Q,P_{\mathrm{in}},P_{\mathrm{out}},K,T,\rho/O\}\) and is full. The complete claims also remain incomparable. One shared substructure consequently does not establish whole-capacity identity. The licensed capacity-level conclusion is asymmetric: different labels do not establish different capacities; shared bounded organization gives a reason to investigate a cross-label bridge; but capacity identity remains open until the granularity and identity-relevance of that shared organization are independently warranted.

\subsection{Downstream application: what does semantic memory warrant?}

The MEM04--CNC05 pair allows the pre-individuation rule to be carried one step beyond a generic warning about labels. Consider a candidate capacity-level inference: because both records are historically indexed by \emph{semantic memory}, that recurrence itself contributes evidence that the two claims support one structurally individuated semantic-memory capacity.

The frozen records show why that evidential route is insufficient. In MEM04, the primary claim characterizes a past-oriented episodic subsystem; the knowledge-oriented subsystem associated with semantic memory appears as a related contrast and dependence. In CNC05, semantic/conceptual knowledge is the direct target of an architectural constraint concerning distributed representations, a shared convergence system, and cross-modal generalization. Thus even the role played by the shared label differs across the two claims.

Their bounded supports are also disjoint. MEM04 supports partition/state/temporal and history-indexed state objects, whereas CNC05 supports state-to-outcome dependence objects. The overlap remains zero after projection into the lossy CPCG representation. The result does \emph{not} show that semantic memory is not one capacity. It changes the evidential status of that thesis: label continuity alone no longer supplies the required bridge.

A unified semantic-memory thesis would therefore need additional evidence that is not contained in the shared term itself. At minimum it must (i) align the target of MEM04's related knowledge-oriented subsystem with the target of CNC05's semantic/conceptual architecture; (ii) state the granularity at which a subsystem distinction and an architectural convergence constraint are to count as the same capacity evidence; (iii) specify which relations are preserved across the two descriptions; and (iv) justify why the resulting bridge survives an appropriate rival representation or why one representation is independently more perspicuous. The capacity thesis remains open, but it now carries explicit preservation debt.


\subsection{Representation-sensitive boundary: ATT03 and MEM04}

A third frozen pair shows why representation choice can change even the presence of apparent overlap. ATT03 is the conflict-monitoring/control claim reconstructed from Botvinick et al.; MEM04 is Tulving's episodic-memory system claim. Under the fine working-basis bounded reconstruction, the two claims share no exact bounded object. ATT03 supports, among other structures, \(A\)-53b16535e1c2, a state/temporal object that preserves persistence plus a time index. MEM04 supports \(A\)-af97ed649c6f, a state/temporal object that instead preserves a history window plus a time index. The fine comparison keeps those temporal distinctions apart.

CPCG deliberately forgets this distinction. Both objects project to the same coarse temporal-state signature, so ATT03 and MEM04 acquire one projected overlap under CPCG despite having zero exact bounded overlap under the working basis. This is not a new discovery of a control--memory capacity. It is a boundary case showing that the proposition \emph{these claims share structure} can change truth value when the preservation contract changes. If persistence versus history-window organization is identity-relevant, CPCG over-unifies this pair; if that distinction is irrelevant to the capacity question, the coarse overlap may be useful. The comparison itself cannot decide which interpretation is warranted.

The source-derived INT-10 and INT-16 perturbation controls are retained in the Supplementary Methods as constructed checks that the A-state rule can leave A0 when a load-bearing interface or history-bearing state is deliberately hidden. They do not alter any certified prospective source outcome.

\section{End-to-end worked example: from source evidence to a capacity inference}

ATT01--BLF01 can be followed through the complete analysis without consulting the repository. In ATT01, the source evidence compares single- and dual-attribute reports within or across perceptual units and relates report accuracy to the perceptual partition. The structured record therefore contains a perceptual-unit partition, experimental conditions, an accuracy outcome, a brief-display probe, and an accuracy criterion. Its reviewed structural adjudication maps these to \(\Pi\), condition coordinates, \(O\), \(P\), and \(Q\), with no required temporal state. In BLF01, outcome history and inferred environmental change alter outcome weighting and subsequent choice. The structured record contains history/information, state variables, a choice outcome, an adaptive criterion, and a changing-contingency probe; its reviewed adjudication additionally preserves time index, history window, future horizon, recurrence, and temporal ordering.

Whole-claim comparison asks whether these complete projected organizations embed under the frozen \(\Phi\) rules. They do not: ATT01 and BLF01 are \textsc{incomparable}, as are all 1,770 corpus pairs. The analysis then changes the question rather than changing that verdict. Under the separately declared bounded forgetting operator, both claims directly instantiate \(A\)-3b814de4da04, whose retained content is a \(P\)-to-\(O\) mapping with typed \(K\) dependence. That object is recurrent beyond these two claims, so it qualifies as reusable bounded organization rather than an ad hoc pairwise resemblance.

Reverse projection then restores the surrounding difference. ATT01 and BLF01 receive different fuller role sets, and their whole claims remain incomparable. The licensed capacity-level inference is therefore: \emph{there is source-grounded evidence that differently labelled attention and learning claims reuse one bounded organization, so label difference alone cannot justify a capacity split and a cross-label identity hypothesis deserves further investigation}. What is not licensed is the stronger statement that object-based attention and volatility-sensitive learning are one capacity. That would require independent warrant that the shared \(P\)-\(K\)-\(O\) organization is constitutive or otherwise identity-relevant at the chosen capacity granularity. The worked example thus exhibits the full evidential chain:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{source evidence}\rightarrow\text{structured claim}
\rightarrow\text{working-basis projection}\\
\Downarrow\\
\Phi\text{ comparison}\rightarrow\text{bounded reuse}
\rightarrow\text{role projection}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{capacity-level constraint}.
\end{gathered}
\]

\section{Boundary condition on the comparison}

The comparison does not supply a universal criterion for where a cognitive system ends. Internal transformation, cross-boundary coupling, and experimental scheduling are kept distinct so that an experimental condition or environmental state is not silently promoted to an internal cognitive role. System boundaries therefore remain application-relative, a point compatible with dynamical, embodied, enactive, and extended approaches. The explicit system--environment--experiment typing and the treatment of experiment start as a cut through a longer history are preserved in the Supplementary Methods.

\section{What could discharge the warrant?}

The closest philosophical relations are not merely precedents; they identify different ways the evidential burden might be discharged or resisted.

Francken, Slors, and Craver distinguish operationalization, abstraction, and boundary problems in cognitive ontology \citep{FranckenSlorsCraver2022CognitiveOntology}. The present procedure does not solve those problems. It makes one consequence explicit: a capacity-to-mechanism or capacity-to-claim comparison cannot borrow its identity criterion from the vocabulary whose individuation is in question. Wajnerman-Paz and Rojas-Líbano make across-context invariance central to simultaneous individuation of mechanism and capacity \citep{WajnermanPazRojasLibano2026Bidimensional}. The present contribution does not compete with that criterion; it identifies a prior evidential condition on its use. A claim of invariance must specify the representation, the relevant context or transformation class, the preservation relation, and the granularity at which stability is assessed, and those choices must be warranted for the identity inference rather than selected merely because they generate stability.

Beni's structural-realist proposal is a more direct positive foil \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. Beni uses predictive processing as a candidate formal basis for identifying invariant relations across divergent consciousness theories. The present results do not show that such a basis is wrong. They show why \emph{successful unification by itself} is insufficient as identity evidence: the prospective 40-paper set remains directly reconstructable under multiple weaker encoding-capable views. A candidate unifying representation therefore requires an additional reason for privileging the structure it makes first-class---for example, a justified preservation criterion, independent empirical invariance, or a perspicuity argument.

That last possibility connects the paper to North. North argues that some representations in physics can be objectively and non-pragmatically more perspicuous than alternatives \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. The present study does not adopt the contrary view that all representations are merely purpose-relative conventions. Instead it separates two questions: what follows from a declared preservation purpose, and whether one representation is independently better aligned with the target. CPCG and TAIF address the first question by exposing sensitivity and non-uniqueness; they do not settle the second. If the working basis is objectively more perspicuous, that requires an argument beyond its ability to encode the corpus.

Brousalis emphasizes that theory comparison depends on identifying similarities and differences that are epistemically relevant to the purpose of comparison \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}. The present preservation contract makes one such relevance decision inspectable rather than tacit. Onishi and Serpico, by contrast, show why causal and structural category boundaries may remain interest-relative \citep{OnishiSerpico2022HPC}. That warning remains in force here: selecting a system boundary, granularity, or preservation criterion is an inquiry-relative methodological act. The proposed remedy is disclosure and sensitivity analysis, not the discovery of a purpose-independent taxonomy.

Krickel's analysis reinforces the same boundary from a different mechanistic direction: mechanisms can be epistemically useful without mechanically determining the correct capacity taxonomy \citep{Krickel2024CognitiveOntology}. Koh\'ar's concrete treatment of scaling-up likewise illustrates why the relevant philosophical dispute should be tied to what explanatory organization a mechanism actually supplies rather than to a binary allegiance between formalisms \citep{Kohar2025ScalingUp}.

The method is therefore neither a replacement cognitive ontology nor a theory of representational perspicuity. It is a constraint on when structural comparison may be used as evidence in those stronger projects.

\section{Limitations and Validation Boundary}

The first limitation is extraction and coding reliability. The 60-claim corpus is source-audited, but independent extractor agreement is not established. Historical construct labels are excluded from the comparison calculation, which blocks direct lexical inheritance at that stage, but this does not establish that the author's source-grounded semantic coding was conceptually blind to construct identity. The current evidence therefore supports auditability of the retained reference corpus, not autonomous extraction reliability or construct-blind coding independence.

Second, the modeled system boundary is application-relative rather than a discovered universal cognitive boundary. The structural typing prevents internal transformation, environmental coupling, and experimental scheduling from being silently conflated, but it does not determine which scientifically appropriate boundary every theory must adopt.

Third, both empirical surfaces are designed rather than probability samples. The original 60 claims cannot estimate the prevalence of roles or structures across cognitive science, and the prospective 40-paper corpus cannot estimate the prevalence of \(A0\), \(A1\), or \(A2\) outcomes in the literature.

Fourth, the basis and forgetting operator are representation choices. TAIF demonstrates that the exact first-class inventory is non-unique under an invertible refactorization. CPCG adds a genuinely lossy sensitivity test: 206 objects become 143 signatures and 63/99 cross-stratum families collide, while 62 cross-stratum signatures and cross-stratum support for 58/60 claims remain. This is stronger evidence that the reuse result is not an artifact of one exact coding, but it is still evidence from only one lossy rival and does not establish representation neutrality.

Fifth, the two prospective representation baselines delimit what the 40/40 A0 result can establish. Under encoding-permissive reconstruction semantics, all six weaker tested representations also remain 40/40 A0. The prospective result therefore does not select the full role inventory. Under the stricter direct-preservation criterion, the same six representations yield 0/40 A0 and 40/40 A1 because omitted distinctions must be explicitly re-exposed. Both controls are deterministic sensitivity checks on already frozen structures, not blinded replications. Together they separate generic encodability and no-added-coordinator claims from the stronger objective of keeping selected distinctions first-class.

Sixth, the system grammar is not a complete scientific claim language. Statements about evidence, model rejection, non-entailment, latent inference, comparison, and scientific uncertainty may require a separate assertion layer even when the underlying system roles are sufficient.

Seventh, the Lean formalization verifies declared typing and dependency structure, relational transition/observation signatures, carry semantics, and the explicit forgetful comparator maps. Its scientific contribution is therefore audit and constraint: it makes some illicit identifications and hidden assumptions mechanically visible. It is not an empirical validation layer and does not prove empirical adequacy, unique semantics, or cognitive truth.

Eighth, the prospective 40-paper corpus is exact-DOI-disjoint from the original 60-claim corpus, but it is not an independent random holdout. The roster was purposively fixed for architectural coverage, and the genealogy audit did not certify pairwise independence. Exact DOI disjointness therefore supports source-identity separation from the original analysis surface but does not license binomial significance, independence-based population inference, or a claim that the 40 papers are independent discoveries.

Ninth, \(A0\) is a structural-fidelity result under mechanisms specified by the source model. It is not a numerical replay of every model, a matched predictive-performance benchmark, or a proof that the source mechanism is empirically correct. Conversely, zero observed \(A1\) and \(A2\) cases does not show that stateless adapters or additional stateful coordinators are impossible in other architectures. The INT-10 and INT-16 real-source-derived controls show that the classifier can leave \(A0\) after declared load-bearing ablations, but these counterfactuals are not new empirical corpus observations.

Finally, independent human re-adjudication was not performed. Procedural auditability is established; inter-rater reliability remains unmeasured. Language-model checks, deterministic CI, formal verification, counterfactual controls, and representation-sensitivity analyses test different properties and are not substitutes for an independent human coder. The frozen human protocol is retained as an open replication target rather than treated as a prerequisite silently satisfied by the author.

A separate M14 protocol now freezes a model-agnostic \emph{cross-model source reconstruction} replay over the same ten-source sample. A replicating model receives only the original source, frozen prompt, protocol, and blank schema; retained ClaimIR and comparison answers remain withheld until all outputs are frozen. Any resulting agreement would measure cross-model reproducibility under the tested models, not human inter-rater reliability. At the present manuscript state this protocol is frozen but has not yet been executed, so no cross-model agreement statistic is reported. The prompt, blank record, input manifest, and comparison rules are public repository artifacts designed so that third parties can replay the test with other model families.

\section{Discussion}

The central result is a constraint on licensed inference. A structural comparison may expose a real similarity or difference while still failing to license a conclusion about cognitive-capacity identity. The missing step is not more formalism for its own sake; it is an argument that the representation, transformation class, preservation rule, and granularity track what matters for the identity question.

ATT03--MEM04 makes the point directly. Fine bounded reconstruction yields zero overlap, whereas the deliberately lossy CPCG projection yields one overlap. Neither result is the representation-independent fact about shared structure. Each is true relative to a declared preservation contract. TAIF reaches the complementary conclusion without information loss: an invertible refactorization preserves reconstructability while showing that the exact first-class inventory is not uniquely forced. These controls jointly undermine RII in its strongest form. Reconstruction success can be genuine while leaving open which represented structure deserves identity-bearing status.

The capacity cases show how the burden constrains downstream interpretation. MEM04 and CNC05 share the inherited label \emph{semantic memory} but lack a preserved bridge under both tested representations; this blocks a label-only route to identity without showing that semantic memory must fragment. ATT01 and BLF01 begin under different labels yet share bounded object \(A\)-3b814de4da04; this supports a bridge hypothesis without establishing whole-capacity identity. The method is therefore asymmetric: it more readily blocks under-warranted identity inferences than establishes new identities.

The 40-paper prospective result is intentionally secondary. Its 40/40 A0 outcome is a real source-grounded compatibility finding, but the same encoding-permissive success survives six weaker representations. It therefore cannot choose the representation that should be treated as identity-bearing. Under direct-preservation semantics the weaker views incur debt, which reveals exactly where a preservation commitment enters. This is a useful negative control, not a reason to make the prospective apparatus the philosophical center of the paper.

The dialogue with recent work can now be stated positively. Beni shows how a candidate formalism can expose structural invariance relevant to theory integration and kind individuation \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. Wajnerman-Paz and Rojas-L\'ibano show why across-context invariance may jointly constrain mechanism and capacity identity \citep{WajnermanPazRojasLibano2026Bidimensional}. The present burden principle asks what licenses those invariances to do identity work. Mechanistic constitution, causal difference-making, empirically motivated context changes, explanatory indispensability, or objective perspicuity could answer that question. Successful formal unification alone cannot.

North and Brousalis mark two complementary routes. A North-style argument could show that one representation is objectively more perspicuous than alternatives, supplying a reason beyond encodability for privileging its structure \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. A Brousalis-style account emphasizes that the similarities and differences chosen for comparison must be epistemically relevant to the inference being made \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}. The present method does less: it does not establish perspicuity or a universal relevance criterion. It makes the burden visible and provides sensitivity tests that show when apparently stable conclusions depend on an unargued representational choice.

This also clarifies the status of TCI and LIP. They are not proposed as three equally documented schools of thought. They are recurrent derivative risks once RII is recognized: labels can smuggle an identity boundary into the input, and local invariance can be promoted beyond the transformation class that generated it. The concrete semantic-memory and CPCG cases show why those shortcuts are unsafe without claiming that any cited author commits them in a simple form.

The empirical basis remains author-mediated. The corpora are purposive, global genealogy is unresolved, only one deliberately lossy rival has been tested, and independent human re-adjudication has not been performed. M14 therefore adds a public cross-model reconstruction protocol rather than disguising model replication as human reliability. The protocol is useful because any third party can rerun the same ten-source extraction with another model family and publish the raw outputs, including disagreement or abstention. At present, however, no such cross-model result is part of the evidence. Procedural auditability is established; inter-rater reliability remains unmeasured.

\section{Conclusion}

An inference from structural comparison to cognitive-capacity identity is epistemically licensed only if the comparison representation, preservation criterion, and granularity are specified and warranted for that inferential use. The relevant warrant is non-circular: reconstruction success cannot by itself establish that the structures made salient by the successful representation are the structures that individuate the capacity.

Two representation tests supply the clearest motivation. TAIF shows lossless non-uniqueness of the exact first-class factorization. ATT03--MEM04 shows that a claim of shared structure can change under a deliberately lossy projection: zero fine bounded overlap becomes positive coarse overlap. The downstream cases then show why this matters for cognitive ontology. A shared label can fail to supply a bridge, while a shared bounded structure across different labels can motivate comparison without settling identity.

The prospective 40-paper study supports a narrower negative point: successful reconstruction persists under multiple weaker encoding-capable representations and therefore does not select the working basis. Its detailed apparatus is secondary to the philosophical argument and is reported primarily in the Supplementary Methods.

The paper does not establish a universal cognitive ontology, a uniquely perspicuous representation, representation-neutral identity, absolute minimality, population prevalence, or independent coding reliability. Independent human validation remains unperformed. A frozen public cross-model reconstruction package now makes a ten-source replay possible with other model families, but no cross-model result is counted as human inter-rater evidence. Procedural auditability is established; inter-rater reliability remains unmeasured.
