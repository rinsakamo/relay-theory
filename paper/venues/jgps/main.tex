\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cognitive ontology; structural comparison; cognitive capacities; pre-individuation; representation; philosophy of cognitive science


\begin{abstract}

Structural comparison can reveal genuine similarities without yet showing that those similarities individuate the same cognitive capacity. This paper proposes a \emph{Discrimination Requirement}: when a structural invariant is used as evidence for capacity identity, the evidential case must discriminate recurrence attributable to the source theories from commonality introduced or amplified by the representation used to compare them. Explanatory and unificatory success remains genuine abductive evidence for a candidate framework; the requirement concerns what that success can establish about the source of the recovered invariance. Beni's structural-realist treatment of consciousness provides a central application. Predictive processing and the free-energy framework may be well supported as a unifying representation while still requiring further evidence before the invariants they expose bear individuative force. The complementary source-first strategy developed here reconstructs source-local commitments before comparison with a candidate framework. Lossless refactorization and a controlled lossy projection show why reconstructability alone does not determine primitive factorization or structural overlap. The resulting proposal is constructive: framework-guided and source-first routes can triangulate on identity-relevant structure, while divergence can localize representational contribution. The paper thus offers a necessary evidential condition on capacity individuation, not a complete theory of cognitive-capacity identity.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cognitive science inherits a large vocabulary of capacities: memory, attention, learning, belief, skill, prediction, control, concepts, and many others. These labels are indispensable for communication and literature organization, but they can also acquire evidential force before the comparison that would justify that force has been made explicit. The same problem arises in the opposite direction when a formal framework reconstructs heterogeneous models successfully: representational unification can look like evidence of a shared capacity structure even when the relevant notion of sameness has not yet been stated.

The construct-validity tradition already denies that a construct validates itself by being named \citep{CronbachMeehl1955ConstructValidity,BorsboomMellenberghVanHeerden2004Validity}, and cognitive-ontology projects explicitly represent relations among concepts, tasks, and measurements \citep{PoldrackEtAl2011CognitiveAtlas,TurnerLaird2012CogPO,PoldrackYarkoni2016CognitiveOntologies}. The question here is narrower and more demanding: \emph{what must be true of a comparison before the resulting similarity or difference can count as evidence for capacity individuation?}

The paper's central contribution is a necessary evidential condition on structural individuation, which I call the \emph{Discrimination Requirement}:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Discrimination Requirement.} When an invariant exposed by structural comparison is used as evidence for cognitive-capacity identity, the evidential case must discriminate source-side recurrence from commonality introduced or amplified by the representation, preservation rule, or granularity through which the comparison is made.
\end{quote}

The requirement does not deny evidential value to explanatory or unificatory success. Such success can support a representation abductively. The point is narrower: if the identity claim depends on the invariant revealed by that representation, the evidential case must do more than show that the representation succeeds at revealing it. Prediction, intervention, mechanistic fit, contrastive superiority, objective perspicuity, or convergence with a differently constructed representation may all contribute to the required discrimination. What is excluded is self-certification, not cumulative evidence.

Cognitive-capacity labels are at most prima facie guides to individuation; neither label continuity nor a successful structural redescription supplies the missing discrimination by itself.

This claim is more specific than the familiar observation that representations preserve different features. It concerns the \emph{epistemology of representation selection for individuation}: which evidence distinguishes an invariant that recurs in the target from one whose apparent commonality depends materially on how the comparison has been constructed? North's account of objective perspicuity is therefore compatible with the proposal rather than opposed to it, and Brousalis' requirement of epistemically relevant similarities is sharpened here into a discrimination condition on identity evidence.

Beni's structural-realist treatment of consciousness provides the clearest contemporary application, but the Discrimination Requirement is not derived from that case and does not depend on completing a PP--FEP-specific empirical comparison. Beni is a demanding positive interlocutor because the proposal explicitly assigns structural invariance a role in kind individuation. The general argument is that any framework-guided route to identity must distinguish source-side recurrence from framework-enabled commonality; a direct PP--FEP/source-first alignment would be an especially informative later application of that principle.

The direct analytical objects in this paper are source-grounded \emph{scientific claims about cognitive capacities}, not capacities themselves. To make the title-level inference explicit, claim-level recurrence becomes evidence for capacity identity only under an additional bridge:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{claim-level recurrent structure}
+\text{ target alignment}\\
&\quad +\text{ capacity-relevant bridge warrant}
+\text{ representational discrimination}\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow\text{ defeasible evidence for capacity identity}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \emph{target alignment} asks whether the source claims genuinely bear on the same capacity-level target rather than merely sharing a label. A \emph{bridge warrant} connects the recurrent structure to the capacity through mechanism, causation, explanation, intervention, or another identity-relevant relation. \emph{Representational discrimination} asks whether the commonality survives relevant alternatives or is otherwise supported as more than a by-product of the chosen comparison. This is an evidential schema, not a sufficient definition of capacity identity. Most of the empirical analysis in this paper supplies and stress-tests the first term; the capacity-level cases show how the remaining terms constrain what may be inferred.

The method suppresses historical construct labels and selected source-authority metadata at the comparison stage, preserves the failure of an initially over-discriminating whole-claim comparison, asks a separately declared bounded-reuse question, and tests representation sensitivity. The large corpus, prospective reconstruction study, formal audits, and model replays are supporting diagnostics rather than the philosophical contribution. Their role is to make the Discrimination Requirement inspectable under controlled changes of representation and procedure, not to estimate population frequencies or independently establish a capacity taxonomy.

\section{The Discrimination Requirement}

The requirement concerns an asymmetry between evidence for a \emph{representation} and evidence for the \emph{identity relevance of an invariant represented within it}. Successful reconstruction can support the former while leaving the latter underdetermined.

The argument is:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Successful reconstruction under a representation \(R\) can provide genuine abductive evidence that \(R\) organizes, explains, predicts, or unifies relevant source material.
\item Capacity individuation requires a more specific conclusion: that an invariant preserved by \(R\) tracks a distinction that matters for the identity of the capacity.
\item When \(R\) helps constitute the comparison space, the appearance of commonality is compatible with multiple sources: recurrence in the source theories, expressive or coarse-graining resources of \(R\), or an interaction between them.
\item Reconstruction success by itself does not discriminate among those sources.
\item Therefore, when the invariant is used for individuation, the evidential case must contain some consideration capable of discriminating relevant source-side recurrence from framework-enabled commonality.
\end{enumerate}

Call step 5 the \emph{Discrimination Requirement}. It is stronger than the generic claim that relevant structure should be justified because it specifies what the relevant justification must be able to do. It must bear on a live alternative explanation of why the common structure appeared. A second piece of evidence need not come from a wholly independent dataset, and reconstruction success itself need not be discarded. The condition is functional: the total evidential package must distinguish the individuation hypothesis from relevant representational alternatives.

A useful corollary is a \emph{Representational Defeater}:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Representational Defeater.} Suppose two scientifically motivated representations remain compatible with the relevant source material but yield different verdicts about an overlap that is being used for capacity individuation. Until the evidential case discriminates which preserved distinctions are capacity-relevant, reconstruction success under the first representation is not sufficient, by itself, to license the identity inference supported only under that representation.
\end{quote}

This is not a global skeptical defeater. It does not erase evidence that a representation is explanatory, predictive, or otherwise useful. It defeats only the move from that representation's success to an identity conclusion when a live, scientifically motivated alternative changes the identity-relevant verdict and remains unresolved.

This formulation also explains why stronger forms of explanatory success matter. Mere accommodation shows that a framework can encode the cases. A novel prediction whose success depends on the proposed invariant can rule out some accommodation stories. An intervention targeting the invariant can supply causal discrimination. A mechanistic bridge can connect the shared structure to the capacity. A North-style perspicuity argument can privilege one representation on grounds other than its ability to fit the present cases. Convergence with a differently constructed reconstruction route can provide cross-representational robustness. These are not interchangeable forms of evidence, but each can do discriminating work that reconstructability alone cannot.

The empirical analyses below therefore test a necessary condition on identity inference rather than a general skepticism about representation. Scientific-representation theory supplies the background fact that representational equivalence and surplus structure depend on what a mapping preserves \citep{Nguyen2017RepresentationEquivalence,NguyenTehWells2020Surplus,FriggNguyen2020ModellingNature,NguyenFrigg2022ScientificRepresentation}. The additional claim here is that, once structural comparison is used for individuation, those preservation choices create a specific evidential burden: the case for identity must discriminate the source of the relevant invariance.

\section{Structural invariance as a live target}

Beni's structural-realist proposal for consciousness science provides the clearest contemporary application of the Discrimination Requirement \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. The application is not imposed on a weak reconstruction of his view. In Sect.~4, Beni explicitly says that structural invariance furnishes an \emph{objective criterion for kind individuation}; Sect.~4.1 then introduces PP--FEP as the formal and empirical scaffolding meant to articulate that invariant organization \citep[Secs.~4--4.1]{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. The relevant proposal should therefore be read in its strongest form. Predictive processing and the free-energy framework are not offered merely as arbitrary notational schemes. Their formal precision and explanatory reach make them scientifically motivated candidates for redescribing otherwise heterogeneous consciousness theories so that relational invariants become visible. If that redescription is coherent and explanatorily fruitful, the success is evidence in favor of the candidate framework. The present argument should not be understood as denying that abductive support. This is not an attribution of a simple fallacy to Beni; the issue is the evidential scope of a genuine explanatory success.

The remaining question is exactly the Discrimination Requirement: \emph{what does that success discriminate?} Suppose a PP--FEP description organizes several theories within a shared inferential architecture and supports illuminating explanations of their relations. At least two epistemic achievements follow. First, PP--FEP has shown itself capable of accommodating and organizing heterogeneous theoretical commitments. Second, if the resulting explanations outperform alternatives, fit independent phenomena, generate novel predictions, or support interventions, PP--FEP may acquire additional evidence as a representation of the target domain. Neither achievement is trivial.

What does not follow automatically is a third claim: that the particular invariants exposed by the PP--FEP redescription are thereby shown to be common to the source theories in a way that is independent of the candidate framework. The problem is not that the explanation is unsuccessful; it is that the source of its success can be mixed. A framework chosen in advance can reveal source-side structure, exploit its own expressive resources to accommodate diverse structures, or do both. Explanatory success becomes evidence for identity-bearing invariance only to the extent that the evidence discriminates among those possibilities.

This leaves room for several strong replies on Beni's behalf. A novel prediction whose success depends specifically on the proposed invariant would narrow the accommodation hypothesis. An intervention that manipulates the invariant and changes the relevant phenomenon would supply causal support. A systematic comparison showing that plausible rival frameworks fail where PP--FEP succeeds would provide a contrast class. A North-style argument that PP--FEP is independently more perspicuous with respect to the target structure would likewise strengthen the case. The burden proposed here is therefore compatible with substantial explanatory evidence; it asks which epistemic possibility that evidence rules out.

The present reconstruction supplies a complementary route. It is \emph{source-first} only in a limited and explicit sense: structured records are built from source-local scientific commitments before any comparison with PP--FEP, and they are not produced by first translating every source into PP--FEP vocabulary. The records still use a representation, a role basis, and explicit preservation rules; the method is not framework-free or representation-neutral.

This yields a triangulation strategy:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{framework-guided:}\quad
&\text{candidate framework} \longrightarrow \text{redescribed theories}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{candidate invariants},\\[1mm]
\text{source-first:}\quad
&\text{source theories} \longrightarrow \text{structured reconstruction}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{recurrent organization}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Convergence between the two routes would not prove a unique ontology, but it would be stronger evidence that the relevant organization is not merely an artifact of one candidate vocabulary. Divergence would also be informative: it would identify where the unifying framework contributes structure not recovered by the source-first route under its different representational commitments. The present paper establishes and stress-tests the second route. A systematic alignment with PP--FEP would be an especially probative application of the Discrimination Requirement, but it is not a premise of the general argument and is not reported as a completed result here.

Wajnerman-Paz and Rojas-L\'ibano sharpen a related issue from cognitive ontology by making across-context invariance central to simultaneous individuation of mechanisms and capacities \citep{WajnermanPazRojasLibano2026Bidimensional}. Their proposal gives a substantive answer to the question \emph{which stability should matter?}: stability across scientifically relevant contexts can constrain both sides of the mechanism--capacity relation. The present contribution is complementary. It specifies why stability observed within one representational route gains evidential force when it survives independently motivated transformations or converges with a differently constructed route.

North's defense of objective or non-pragmatic perspicuity gives one way to discharge the burden \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. If a representation is independently shown to display the target structure more perspicuously than its rivals, that result can combine with explanatory success rather than being replaced by it. The burden principle is therefore not a thesis that every representation is equally interest-relative.

Brousalis makes epistemically relevant similarities and differences central to theory comparison \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}. The present claim adds a discrimination condition to that insight: when similarity is used for individuation, the evidential case should distinguish source-side recurrence from commonality introduced or amplified by the comparison framework. Francken, Slors, and Craver, Krickel, and Koh\'ar likewise show why abstraction, operationalization, mechanism, and system-boundary choices must remain tied to explanatory practice rather than silently hardening into a taxonomy \citep{FranckenSlorsCraver2022CognitiveOntology,Krickel2024CognitiveOntology,Kohar2025ScalingUp}.

The paper therefore targets a reconstruction-to-identity inference in a restricted sense: not every inference that uses successful reconstruction, but one in which reconstruction success does more evidential work than it can discriminate. The additional evidence may include the success itself; what matters is that the identity claim is supported by considerations capable of ruling out relevant framework-induced alternatives.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Designed corpus}

The bounded empirical target contains 60 claims. Forty-eight primary claims are divided evenly across eight historical construct strata. Twelve additional claims form a non-tuning challenge surface.

The primary strata are Memory, Learning, Skill, Attention, Prediction, Control, Belief, and Concept. Challenge pressures include multi-timescale organization, stochastic or approximate structure, constitutive boundary pressure, compositional or systematic structure, embodied or world-coupled organization, normative or criterion structure, hybrid labels, label-coordinate mismatch, multi-condition structure, same-inventory/different-topology pressure, strict-refinement pressure, and high structural heterogeneity. Challenge claims were not permitted to tune the structured-record schema, working basis, or comparison semantics.

This is a literature-based structural comparison, not an experiment or prevalence study. Its role is instead adversarial and structural: the 48 primary claims provide balanced coverage across historically familiar construct strata, while the 12 challenge claims probe predeclared pressures that could break the representation. Consequently, the supported inference is whether reusable structure survives this designed stress-test surface under pre-specified rules, not how prevalent any role or reusable structural subobject is in cognitive science as a whole.

\subsection{Source-grounded structured claim records}

Each selected source claim was represented in a machine-readable structured claim record containing stable source identity, source-local evidence locators, claim scope and modality, typed nodes, typed relations, temporal information, control information, approximation state, and bridge assumptions.

A language model assisted initial structured extraction, but its output was not treated as authority. Every retained record was reviewed against source text, and all 60 current records carry reviewed status. The scientific input to downstream analysis is therefore the source-audited structured claim corpus.

Independent extraction reliability was not established. Historical local two-pass calibration transactions produced zero valid structured claim records in both the v2 and v3 real calibration runs, leaving no valid A/B agreement denominator. The present study consequently claims auditability of the retained corpus, not inter-model agreement, autonomous extraction reliability, or deterministic language-model replay.

\subsection{Pre-specification and anti-circularity}

The comparison basis, whole-claim projection, and bounded-reconstruction rules were fixed before later grammar and prospective outcomes were used to interpret them. Reverse projection did not reread source papers or rewrite the reviewed claim records to improve grammar coverage, and residuals were retained rather than repaired after inspection. The purpose of this staging is not to remove author judgment, which remains present in source review and structural adjudication, but to prevent the final grammar from simultaneously defining and validating its own input representation. Exact repository chronology, immutable hashes, and stage-level provenance are reported in the Supplementary Methods rather than used as argumentative premises in the main text.

\subsection{Generative AI and human accountability}

OpenAI ChatGPT assisted structured extraction, source-analysis workflow, software development, and manuscript drafting. Model output was not treated as source authority or as an independent adjudicator. Every retained claim in the 60-claim reference corpus was reviewed by the author against source text, and the prospective study tied adjudications to explicit source locations and versioned artifacts. Independent extraction reliability and independent human re-adjudication were not established; these remain distinct limitations. Full disclosure and workflow provenance are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

\subsection{Pre-specified structural basis}

The structural comparison suppresses source information that should not determine similarity merely by appearing in a paper. Author identity, institution, venue, citation count, historical construct label, source-local identifiers, and free-text presentation are excluded from comparison.

The pre-specified working basis is
\[
B_{P2}=\{S,\Pi,K,O,T,C,Q,P\},
\]
interpreted as a declared structural coordinate system for comparison rather than an ontology or a representation-neutral basis. Lexical shortcuts are forbidden: a source node named \emph{condition} is not automatically \(C\); a reward-like term does not exhaust \(Q\); temporal precedence is not automatically \(K\); and a partition need not be physical.

\subsection{Whole-claim comparison}

The pre-specified \(\Phi\) projection preserves claim type, modality, scope shape, typed topology, active basis coordinates, temporal presence states, control states, approximation mode, and bridge-grounding multiplicity. It forgets source authority and presentation metadata.

Pairwise relations distinguish equivalence, refinement, forgetting, incomparability, and mutual-embed non-isomorphism. For 60 claims, the unordered comparison surface contains
\[
\binom{60}{2}=1770
\]
pairs.

\subsection{Secondary bounded reconstruction}

Because whole-claim comparison failed universally, the next analysis changed the \emph{unit of reuse}, not the pre-specified source representation. The whole-claim result therefore remains the primary result for the original \(\Phi\)-level question. A secondary post-atlas reconstruction then asked a different question: whether bounded substructures could recur even when complete scientific claims remained incomparable. In that chronological sense the reconstruction is post-atlas; it is not presented as if it had been the successful primary comparison from the outset.

The bounded reconstruction searched for basis-subobjects recoverable under a pre-specified forgetting operator. The operator permits restriction to retained typed substructure but forbids arbitrary rewiring and semantic relabeling. Thus the secondary analysis does not revise the 1,770 whole-claim verdicts, and successful bounded recurrence cannot be used to retrospectively relabel a failed whole-claim pair as equivalent.

Negative and ablation controls tested trivial-core collapse, label dependence, permissive forgetting, richer competing cores, overlapping incomparable cores, cross-label recurrence, compatibility with whole-claim incomparability, and presentation invariance. No post-outcome abstraction threshold was added to manufacture recurrence.

\subsection{Structural role grammar and adversarial tests}

Surviving bounded structures motivated a compact structural role grammar (version 0):
\[
\Gsys=\{\Pi,X,C,Q,\Pin,\Pout,K,T,\rho/O\}.
\]
Here \(X\) is a generic configuration carrier; \(C\) is realizability/admissibility structure; \(Q\) is criterion-conditioned orientation; \(\Pin\) and \(\Pout\) are directional interface roles; \(K\) is typed transformation or dependence; \(T\) is succession or temporal indexing; and \(\rho/O\) is boundary-relative observation or trace.

A mnemonic representation is
\[
X_{n+1}=K_{n;C,Q,\Pin}(X_n),
\qquad
O_n=\rho_{\Pi,C,\Pout,n}(X_n),
\]
without assuming that every claim instantiates every role.

All 60 reviewed claims were reverse-projected into this structural role grammar without rereading source papers or changing the structured claim records, \(B_{P2}\), \(\Phi\), structural adjudications, or reusable-subobject identities. Residuals were classified under a pre-specified taxonomy in which only an unexplained requirement for an additional top-level system role counted as prima facie evidence for extending the role inventory.

Two strict distinction-preservation comparators were tested:
\[
G_{\mathrm{dyn}}=\{X,K,T\},
\qquad
G_{\mathrm{pomdp}}=\{X,P,K,\rho/O,Q,T\}.
\]
These are distinction-loss tests, not non-encodability theorems.


\subsection{Prospective non-selection control}

After the original 60-claim analysis and role grammar were fixed, a separate no-substitution roster of 40 cognitive-model papers was reconstructed under the same source-faithful criteria. All 40 could be reconstructed without adding a new coordinating mechanism beyond what the source model itself specified. This remains a source-grounded, single-adjudicator finding; DOI separation from the original corpus is not treated as genealogical independence.

For the present argument, the important result is negative. The same 40 papers also remain reconstructable under six weaker encoding-capable views. Successful direct reconstruction therefore does not select the exact role inventory. When the criterion is strengthened so that omitted distinctions must remain directly available rather than being reintroduced by an adapter, all six weaker views incur preservation debt. The two tests answer different questions and are reported in full in the Supplementary Methods. Their philosophical role is simply to separate reconstructability from representational privilege.

\subsection{Lossless non-uniqueness and a genuinely lossy rival representation}

Two different representation-sensitivity questions are kept separate. First, an invertible Tagged Admissibility--Interface Factorization (TAIF) tests whether the exact nine first-class roles are unique when the same information is losslessly refactored. Second, a Coarse Process--Constraint Graph (CPCG) asks whether higher-order bounded-reuse conclusions survive a deliberately information-losing representation.

CPCG has five coarse families: \(N\) for carrier/component structure, merging state-like and partition/boundary structure; \(G\) for guard/orientation, merging constraints and criteria; \(I\) for interface events, merging probes/interventions with observations/readouts; \(E\) for non-temporal transformation/dependence; and \(T\) for temporal presence/order. Fine node-role strings, working-basis control subtypes, non-temporal relation kinds, and detailed temporal subtypes are discarded. The map is therefore non-injective by construction. No retention threshold was declared: survival and collapse are both reportable sensitivity outcomes.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Whole-claim comparison diagnoses excessive granularity}
\label{sec:wholefail}

The pre-specified whole-claim comparator classified all 1,770 unordered pairs as incomparable. In the corresponding 3,540 directed comparisons, 3,518 stopped at the scalar stage, six reached node-count comparison, 16 reached node-label multiset comparison, and none reached topology or embedding.

The philosophical use of this result is therefore diagnostic rather than triumphalist. The rich whole-claim representation distinguishes the corpus so early that it supplies almost no reusable equivalence structure. Universal incomparability is retained because it identifies a failed comparison granularity; it is not presented as evidence that cognitive capacities are globally incommensurable or that graph topology separates every claim.

\subsection{Comparator reachability controls}

The universal whole-claim incomparability result raises an immediate sanity question: can the comparator ever return a positive relation? Three pre-specified constructed controls answer this without adding corpus evidence. An identifier/presentation renaming of the same structural claim returns \textsc{equivalent}. A presentation-only metadata variant, changing source-facing metadata that the pre-specified projection explicitly forgets while leaving scientific structure untouched, also returns \textsc{equivalent}. Finally, adding one authorized fixed resource distinction returns \textsc{right-strict-refinement-of-left}, with the forgotten \(C\) axis and resource control recorded in the witness. These are \emph{CONSTRUCTED\_CONTROL} results. They establish reachability of equivalence and refinement states under the comparator; they do not validate a cognitive taxonomy or weaken the held-fixed \(1770/1770\) corpus result.

\subsection{Bounded reusable structure reappears}

The secondary bounded reconstruction produced 213 within-stratum candidate subobjects and 206 unique reusable structural subobjects. Seven exact cross-stratum equivalence components survived. The global refinement structure contains 470 direct refinement edges and 2,894 strict subobject relations in transitive closure.

The bounded family structure contains
\[
99\ \text{cross-stratum families}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
107\ \text{within-stratum-only families},
\]
with 188 distinct support signatures. Fifty-nine of 60 claims are covered by at least one surviving bounded object. All eight negative and ablation controls pass.

Notably, 1,304 whole-claim pairs share at least one surviving reusable structural subobject despite every whole-claim pair being held as incomparable. Whole-claim identity and reusable substructure are therefore distinct questions on the same corpus.

\subsection{Reverse projection is secondary to the identity argument}

Reverse projection of the 60 reviewed claims into the compact role grammar yields 21 FULL, 22 PARTIAL, and 17 RESIDUAL cases. None of the 17 residuals requires a new top-level role under the held-fixed adjudication. These results remain useful as an audit of the working representation, but they are not evidence that the exact role inventory is uniquely correct. Detailed role frequencies, residual classes, and strict distinction-preservation comparator results are therefore moved to the Supplementary Methods rather than treated as first-class philosophical results.

\subsection{Critical representation-sensitivity tests}

The first test is lossless. The invertible refactorization used here preserves the information needed to reconstruct the original role structure while changing how that structure is factored. Successful representation therefore does not uniquely privilege the original first-class inventory: lossless recoding can preserve scientific content while changing which distinctions appear primitive.

The second test is deliberately lossy. Under the fine bounded representation, the conflict-monitoring claim reconstructed from Botvinick et al. and Tulving's episodic-memory claim share no exact bounded object \citep{BotvinickEtAl2001ConflictMonitoring,Tulving2002EpisodicMemory}. A coarser projection forgets the difference between two temporal-state organizations and causes them to collapse to the same coarse signature. The pair therefore moves from zero overlap to positive overlap under the coarser representation.

The coarse projection is not presented as the uniquely correct representation of either source. Its relevance is that the forgotten distinction is scientifically intelligible rather than arbitrary string deletion: both fine objects retain temporally extended state dependence, while the coarse representation declines to treat the difference between persistence-at-time and history-window organization as load-bearing for the comparison. A cognitive scientist interested in broad state-dependent organization could therefore have a prima facie reason to ask the coarser question, whereas a scientist interested in implementation-level temporal organization would have a reason to retain the distinction.

That is enough for the diagnostic. If the temporal difference is capacity-identity-relevant, the coarse representation over-unifies the pair; if it is not, the fine representation may over-distinguish them. The projection itself does not decide which possibility is correct. Instead it exposes a live representational alternative under which the overlap verdict changes. The Discrimination Requirement then asks for evidence that bears on which temporal distinction should carry individuative force.

The effect is not unique to the Botvinick--Tulving pair. Applied to the complete bounded surface, the same pre-specified coarse projection maps 206 fine-grained reusable objects to 143 coarse signatures; 63 of the 99 fine cross-stratum families collide with at least one other original family. Those aggregate collisions do not validate the coarse representation, but they show that the witness is an instance of a corpus-wide consequence of the forgetting rule rather than a pair-specific transformation engineered only to manufacture one overlap.

Together, the lossless and lossy tests do more than restate that representations differ. The lossless test shows that primitive factorization is not selected by reconstructability even without information loss. The lossy test supplies a scientifically interpretable case in which the existence of overlap depends on a contestable preservation decision. Neither result establishes equal adequacy of the rivals. Their philosophical role is to show why a capacity-level identity inference needs discriminating evidence beyond the mere fact that one chosen representation reconstructs successfully.

\subsection{Secondary procedural robustness diagnostics}

Two additional analyses test the stability of the procedure without supplying the paper's primary philosophical evidence. First, a separate prospective source set remains reconstructable under the working basis and under weaker encoding-capable views; when direct preservation of omitted distinctions is required, the weaker views must re-expose what they omitted. This confirms the distinction between generic reconstructability and direct preservation, but the pattern is partly built into the contrast between those criteria. The philosophically relevant point is therefore the criterion distinction itself, not the magnitude of the count.

Second, blinded source-reconstruction replays exposed and then repaired a confound between selecting a focal claim and decomposing that claim. Once the focal claim was fixed, independently run model configurations often recovered compatible identity-relevant dependencies without reproducing the exact first-class factorization. This is reported as a \emph{procedural robustness diagnostic}, not as independent human reliability, cross-provider replication, or evidence of unseen-source generalization. Case-level scores, model provenance, and the source correction triggered by one replay disagreement are reported in the Supplementary Methods.

\section{Capacity-level consequences}

The claim-level analysis constrains capacity individuation only through the evidential schema introduced above. A recurrent structure is not yet a capacity invariant. It becomes evidence for capacity identity only insofar as (i) the source claims are aligned to the relevant capacity-level target, (ii) there is a substantive bridge from the recurrent structure to that target, and (iii) the representation has survived the Discrimination Requirement. The cases below are useful precisely because different terms of this schema fail in different ways.


\subsection{Different labels can share bounded organization}

Duncan's work on object-based visual attention and Behrens et al.'s work on volatility-sensitive learning begin under sharply different historical labels \citep{Duncan1984SelectiveAttention,BehrensEtAl2007ValueInformation}. Their complete structured claims remain incomparable. At a separately declared bounded granularity, however, both instantiate the same general organization: a probe maps to an outcome through typed conditional structure. Because construct labels are excluded from the comparison, this is not a lexical match.

The inference stops there. Their fuller role profiles differ, and their complete claims remain incomparable. The licensed conclusion is therefore asymmetric: label difference does not by itself justify a capacity split, and recurrent bounded organization gives a reason to investigate a cross-label bridge; but one shared substructure does not establish whole-capacity identity. In the schema above, structural recurrence is present, while the capacity-relevant bridge warrant remains open. The case therefore generates a bridge hypothesis rather than an identity verdict.

\subsection{A shared label need not supply the bridge}

Tulving's episodic-memory analysis and Patterson, Nestor, and Rogers' account of semantic/conceptual knowledge both sit near the historical label \emph{semantic memory}, but the label plays different roles in the two source claims \citep{Tulving2002EpisodicMemory,PattersonNestorRogers2007Semantic}. In Tulving's analysis, the primary target is a past-oriented episodic subsystem, with a knowledge-oriented subsystem entering as a contrast and dependence. In Patterson et al., semantic/conceptual knowledge is itself the direct target of an architectural claim involving distributed representations, convergence, and cross-modal generalization.

The bounded structural supports of the two claims are disjoint under the fine representation and remain disjoint under the tested coarse projection. This does \emph{not} show that semantic memory is not one capacity. It diagnoses a failure earlier in the evidential schema: the two source claims are not automatically target-aligned merely because the same historical label appears in their vicinity. Label recurrence therefore cannot supply the bridge by itself.

A unified semantic-memory thesis would therefore need additional evidence that aligns the targets of the two descriptions, states the granularity at which their organizations are to count as evidence about the same capacity, specifies what is preserved across the bridge, and explains why the chosen representation is appropriate for the identity question. The capacity thesis remains open, but the preservation debt is explicit.

\subsection{Representation choice can change the existence of overlap}

The Botvinick et al.--Tulving comparison provides the clearest representation-sensitive boundary case \citep{BotvinickEtAl2001ConflictMonitoring,Tulving2002EpisodicMemory}. Under the fine bounded representation, the conflict-monitoring claim and the episodic-memory claim share no exact bounded object. One preserves persistence with a time index; the other preserves a history window with a time index. The fine comparison keeps those temporal organizations distinct.

The deliberately coarse projection forgets that distinction, so the two temporal-state objects collapse to the same signature and the pair acquires positive overlap. This is not evidence for a newly discovered control--memory capacity. It shows that the sentence \emph{these claims share structure} can change truth value when the preservation contract changes. Here the missing term is representational discrimination: the capacity-level question is not whether overlap can be produced, but whether there is evidence that the preserved equivalence, rather than the forgotten temporal distinction, is identity-relevant.

\section{Worked example: from source evidence to a capacity constraint}

The Duncan--Behrens comparison can be followed through the complete analysis without consulting the repository. Duncan's source evidence relates report accuracy to whether attributes belong to the same or different perceptual units; Behrens et al. relate outcome history and inferred environmental change to subsequent choice \citep{Duncan1984SelectiveAttention,BehrensEtAl2007ValueInformation}. The structured records preserve those source-specific organizations before any bounded comparison is attempted.

At the whole-claim level, the two organizations are incomparable. The analysis then changes the question rather than changing that verdict: under the separately declared bounded forgetting rule, both instantiate the same probe-to-outcome organization with typed conditional dependence. That organization also recurs elsewhere in the corpus, so it is not an ad hoc resemblance invented for this pair.

Restoring the surrounding structure reintroduces the differences. The licensed inference is therefore that differently labelled attention and learning claims can reuse a bounded organization, so label difference alone cannot justify a capacity split and a cross-label identity hypothesis deserves further investigation. What is not licensed is the stronger claim that object-based attention and volatility-sensitive learning are one capacity. That requires further warrant that the shared organization is constitutive or otherwise identity-relevant at the chosen capacity granularity.

\section{Boundary conditions on the comparison}

The comparison does not supply a universal criterion for where a cognitive system ends. Internal transformation, cross-boundary coupling, and experimental scheduling are kept distinct so that an experimental condition or environmental state is not silently promoted to an internal cognitive role. System boundaries therefore remain application-relative, a point compatible with dynamical, embodied, enactive, and extended approaches. The explicit system--environment--experiment typing and the treatment of experiment start as a cut through a longer history are preserved in the Supplementary Methods.

\section{What could satisfy the Discrimination Requirement?}

The requirement is deliberately permissive about the form of additional evidence. What matters is not that the evidence come from a different dataset, but that it discriminate among relevant explanations of the recovered invariant.

A mechanistic result can do this if the recurrent structure is shown to constitute or organize the target capacity rather than merely describe it. Intervention or causal evidence can discriminate if manipulating the proposed invariant changes the capacity-relevant phenomenon in the predicted way. Novel prediction can matter when success depends specifically on the proposed invariant rather than on the general flexibility of the framework. A contrastive representation test can matter when plausible rivals fail to recover the same organization. North-style objective perspicuity can matter if one representation is independently better aligned with target structure \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. Cross-representational convergence can matter when a framework-guided and differently constructed source-first route recover the same organization.

A limited positive illustration already occurs within the analyzed literature. Patterson, Nestor, and Rogers do not merely redescribe semantic knowledge in a preferred architecture; the shared-convergence architecture yields a differential lesion prediction, namely that relatively focal damage to the common convergence system should produce impairment generalized across input and output modalities and item classes, and the review evaluates semantic-dementia evidence against that prediction \citep{PattersonNestorRogers2007Semantic}. This does not by itself establish a cross-source capacity identity. It illustrates the relevant evidential form: a structural proposal gains support because it makes a discriminating empirical commitment against an architectural rival, not merely because the source can be reconstructed in its vocabulary.

These possibilities locate the present proposal relative to nearby work. Wajnerman-Paz and Rojas-L\'ibano use across-context invariance to constrain mechanism and capacity individuation \citep{WajnermanPazRojasLibano2026Bidimensional}; the Discrimination Requirement asks what makes the relevant invariance evidentially discriminating. Brousalis emphasizes epistemically relevant similarities and differences in theory comparison \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}; the present condition specifies what relevance must accomplish when similarity is used for identity. Onishi and Serpico show why natural-kind boundaries can remain interest-relative \citep{OnishiSerpico2022HPC}; the present proposal does not remove inquiry-relative choices but requires their individuative use to survive relevant alternatives. Krickel and Koh\'ar likewise show why mechanistic usefulness need not mechanically determine a taxonomy \citep{Krickel2024CognitiveOntology,Kohar2025ScalingUp}.

Beni remains the clearest positive application because PP--FEP is offered as a substantive unifying framework rather than a neutral notation \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. A direct comparison between PP--FEP-guided reconstructions and the source-first structures studied here would provide especially strong evidence about the source of any convergence. But the Discrimination Requirement does not stand or fall with that future comparison. Its general claim is prior: whenever structural invariance is used for individuation, the evidential package must distinguish relevant target recurrence from commonality attributable to the comparison framework.

\section{Limitations and evidential boundary}

The corpus is purposive and diagnostic. It is not a probability sample, so neither the frequency of structural roles nor the frequency of prospective reconstruction outcomes should be interpreted as population prevalence. The exact counts are useful for auditability and for locating counterexamples, but the philosophical argument depends primarily on the existence of the representation-sensitivity witnesses.

Independent human re-adjudication was not performed. The retained source records were reviewed against source text, and the model replays provide a separate procedural check, but neither substitutes for independent human coding. Procedural auditability is established; inter-rater reliability remains unmeasured.

The modeled system boundary is application-relative rather than a discovered universal cognitive boundary. Structural typing helps prevent internal transformation, environmental coupling, and experimental scheduling from being silently conflated, but it does not determine which boundary every scientifically appropriate theory must adopt.

Only one deliberately lossy rival representation is used in the central sensitivity test. Together with the lossless refactorization, this is enough to show that reconstruction success does not uniquely fix primitive factorization and that overlap can change under controlled information loss. It is not enough to establish representation neutrality or to show that every reasonable rival representation produces the same pattern.

The prospective 40-paper analysis is a non-selection control, not an independent random holdout. The papers were purposively chosen for architectural coverage, and the analysis does not support binomial significance or population inference. Its role is limited to showing that successful reconstruction persists under weaker encoding-capable views and therefore cannot by itself privilege the full working basis.

The formal verification checks declared typing, dependencies, and transformation rules. It is an audit layer rather than an empirical validation layer: it can expose inconsistent formal commitments but cannot establish empirical adequacy, cognitive truth, or a uniquely correct ontology.

Finally, the claim-anchored procedural robustness diagnostic was designed after the initial unanchored replay exposed a claim-selection confound. It is therefore prospective relative to its own outputs but not independent confirmation of the earlier diagnosis. The resulting same-source compatibility is informative about decomposition reproducibility under the tested conditions, not about unseen-source generalization, provider independence, or human reliability. The subsequent source correction was kept separate from the historical replay score and was subjected to a deterministic sensitivity analysis rather than used to rescore the robustness diagnostic post hoc.

\section{Discussion}

The paper's central contribution is the \emph{Discrimination Requirement}. Explanatory and reconstruction success is genuine evidence for a candidate framework, but an individuation claim requires the evidential package to distinguish source-side recurrence from commonality introduced or amplified by the representation used to compare the sources. This is the step that turns familiar representation dependence into a constraint on identity evidence. The associated Representational Defeater gives the constraint an inferential consequence: when a scientifically motivated live alternative changes the identity-relevant overlap verdict, the original reconstruction success is no longer sufficient, by itself, to carry the identity inference until that alternative is discriminated against.

The lossless and lossy tests have a deliberately limited role. The lossless refactorization shows that reconstructability does not uniquely select primitive factorization. The Botvinick--Tulving projection shows that a scientifically intelligible preservation choice can change whether bounded overlap exists. Neither establishes that all representations are equally good, nor that the coarse representation is correct. They establish that reconstructability alone cannot adjudicate which representational distinction should carry individuative force. That is exactly the alternative the Discrimination Requirement requires further evidence to resolve.

The relation to Beni is constructive rather than eliminative \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. PP--FEP may have substantial abductive support as a unifying framework. The question introduced here is whether the particular invariants recruited for kind individuation are also supported as target-side recurrence rather than merely as commonality available within the PP--FEP redescription. A systematic PP--FEP/source-first alignment would be an especially informative test, but the philosophical contribution is not a Beni-specific empirical result. Beni supplies a live and demanding application of a more general evidential condition.

The same point clarifies the relation to North and Brousalis. North shows how a representation might be privileged by objective perspicuity rather than mere reconstructability \citep{North2026Perspicuous}; such an argument can satisfy the requirement if it discriminates relevant alternatives. Brousalis emphasizes epistemically relevant similarity \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}; the Discrimination Requirement specifies what such relevance must do in an individuation argument: it must bear on why the similarity should be attributed to the target rather than to the comparison setup.

The capacity-level cases expose different missing terms in the evidential bridge. Duncan--Behrens supplies recurrent structure but leaves its capacity-level bridge open. Tulving--Patterson shows that label continuity does not secure target alignment. Botvinick--Tulving shows that the overlap verdict itself can depend on a preservation decision, making representational discrimination necessary. The paper therefore does not offer a positive theory of capacity identity. It offers a necessary condition on when claim-level structural evidence may be promoted into a capacity-level identity argument.

The larger corpus, prospective reconstruction study, formal audits, and model replays remain supporting procedural diagnostics. Their exact counts matter for auditability, not for the novelty claim. The philosophical result would survive if those counts were less visually striking: what matters is the demonstrated separation between reconstructability, preservation, and individuation, and the resulting requirement that identity evidence discriminate among relevant sources of apparent invariance.

\section{Conclusion}

Explanatory success and successful structural reconstruction are evidence. The Discrimination Requirement limits what that evidence can establish about cognitive-capacity identity: when an invariant exposed by structural comparison is used for individuation, the evidential case must discriminate source-side recurrence from commonality introduced or amplified by the comparison representation, preservation rule, or granularity.

This requirement is not a complete theory of capacity individuation. Claim-level recurrence becomes capacity-level evidence only when target alignment, a capacity-relevant bridge warrant, and representational discrimination are also in place. Mechanistic evidence, intervention, novel prediction, contrastive representation tests, objective perspicuity, and cross-representational convergence are among the ways those additional conditions might be supported.

Beni provides an important application rather than the sole target. Framework-guided PP--FEP unification and source-first reconstruction can be treated as complementary evidential routes. Convergence would strengthen the case that an invariant is not merely framework-enabled; divergence would identify where representational contribution requires explanation. Performing that systematic alignment would extend the present argument, not supply a missing premise for it.

The paper does not establish a universal cognitive ontology, a representation-neutral identity criterion, a uniquely perspicuous basis, population prevalence, unseen-source replay generalization, or independent coding reliability. Its positive contribution is a necessary evidential condition: identity-relevant structural similarity must be discriminated from similarity that the comparison procedure itself helps create. Procedural auditability is established; inter-rater reliability remains unmeasured.
