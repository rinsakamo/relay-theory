\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cognitive ontology; structural similarity; cognitive-capacity identity; scientific representation; theory comparison; philosophy of cognitive science


\begin{abstract}

Structural comparison can reveal genuine similarities without yet showing that two systems instantiate the same cognitive-capacity type. This paper proposes a \emph{Discrimination Requirement} for a specific evidential use of structural similarity: when a similarity is offered as positive evidence for capacity-type identity, the comparison must justify that the preservation map has not made the similarity non-diagnostic with respect to the relevant identity alternatives, or supply independent evidence that restricts those alternatives. The requirement is not proposed as a new foundational theory of confirmation. It is a methodological corollary of a minimal contrastive-support norm together with a feature specific to structural comparison: the evidential item is itself produced by a representation and its preservation map. A matched conceptual comparison holds ordinary scientific adequacy fixed while varying only whether the map's relevant fibers respect or cross the declared individuation criterion. This isolates representation provenance from ordinary missing-data underdetermination. A specific live same-question rival can then activate an undercutting defeater, but logical possibility alone is insufficient. A conditional probabilistic example distinguishes fiber crossing from the loss of warranted support attribution, and a separate bounded-event semantics relates motif overlap to complete signature equality. The empirical corpus supplies only representation-sensitivity diagnostics and does not instantiate the defeater. The paper therefore offers a qualification condition on structurally supported capacity-type identity inferences, not a sufficient theory of cognitive capacities.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cognitive science inherits a large vocabulary of capacities: memory, attention, learning, belief, skill, prediction, control, concepts, and many others. These labels are indispensable for communication and literature organization, but they can also acquire evidential force before the comparison that would justify that force has been made explicit. The same problem arises in the opposite direction when a formal framework reconstructs heterogeneous models successfully: representational unification can look like evidence of a shared capacity structure even when the relevant notion of sameness has not yet been stated.

The construct-validity tradition already denies that a construct validates itself by being named \citep{CronbachMeehl1955ConstructValidity,BorsboomMellenberghVanHeerden2004Validity}, and cognitive-ontology projects explicitly represent relations among concepts, tasks, and measurements \citep{PoldrackEtAl2011CognitiveAtlas,TurnerLaird2012CogPO,PoldrackYarkoni2016CognitiveOntologies}. The question here is narrower: \emph{when a structural match is offered as positive evidence that two targets instantiate the same cognitive-capacity type, what qualifies that match to discriminate the identity hypothesis from its relevant alternatives?}

The paper's central contribution is a necessary evidential condition on the promotion of structural similarity into capacity-identity evidence, which I call the \emph{Discrimination Requirement}:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Discrimination Requirement.} When a structurally identified similarity is offered as positive evidence that two systems instantiate the same cognitive-capacity type under a declared criterion \(C\), the evidential case must justify that the comparison's preservation map remains discriminating over the relevant \(C\)-alternatives, or independently restrict those alternatives so that the similarity retains identity-supporting force.
\end{quote}

The requirement does not deny evidential value to explanatory or unificatory success. It separates two epistemic questions that can otherwise be run together:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{warrant to use }R\text{ for a scientific target}\\
&\qquad\not\Rightarrow
\text{ warrant that equality under }R\text{ favors capacity-type identity}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first asks whether a representation is scientifically warranted for organizing, predicting, or explaining a target. The second asks whether the \emph{equality relation induced by that representation} favors the identity hypothesis over relevant alternatives under \(C\). These are different questions. A representation can be predictively excellent and still quotient over distinctions that matter to a taxonomic or mechanistic identity question. The present requirement is triggered by an \emph{identity-supporting evidential role}, not by argumentative indispensability or by a threshold amount of evidential weight: the issue is whether the similarity itself is being cited as a reason to favor sameness of capacity type. It may remain weak evidence, and other evidence may dominate the final judgment. What requires qualification is the positive support attributed to the represented similarity itself.

The novelty claim is therefore methodological rather than foundational. A standard evidence appraisal that already retains and calibrates the representation-generated match event fully satisfies the proposed requirement; the distinctive deliverable is a concrete provenance-completeness audit for structural comparison. A minimal contrastive-support norm says that evidence offered in favor of \(H\) must do more than remain compatible with both \(H\) and the relevant alternatives. The additional fact about structural comparison is that the candidate evidence---a match under \(R\)---is generated by a preservation map whose information loss can itself change that contrastive relation. The Discrimination Requirement identifies this map-induced provenance problem and states what must be checked before the match is promoted to identity evidence. North's objective perspicuity can help by privileging a representation with respect to the identity-relevant target \citep{North2026Perspicuous}; Brousalis's comparative methodology can then govern the weighting of epistemically relevant similarities and differences \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}. The present condition operates one step earlier: it qualifies whether a representation-produced match is fit to enter that weighting as identity evidence at all.

A matched comparison isolates the point without missing observations or an unperformed intervention. Let \(\mathcal F=\{a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2\}\) be a fully specified fine-structure space, and let the independently declared individuation criterion \(C\) partition it into two capacity types: \(C(a_1)=C(a_2)=A\) and \(C(b_1)=C(b_2)=B\). Consider two representations that are equally adequate for the same ordinary scientific task---for example, each preserves the dependencies needed for the stipulated predictive target---but induce different quotient maps. The first, \(\phi_{\mathrm{align}}\), has fibers \(\{a_1,a_2\}\) and \(\{b_1,b_2\}\): equality of signatures never crosses a \(C\)-boundary within the live comparison set. The second, \(\phi_{\mathrm{cross}}\), has fibers \(\{a_1,b_1\}\) and \(\{a_2,b_2\}\): the same signature can now be shared by systems from different \(C\)-types. Nothing is unobserved in this toy construction; the loss of discrimination is introduced by the comparison map itself. The ordinary scientific adequacy of the two representations is held fixed, while only their relation to the independently declared identity criterion changes. The Discrimination Requirement therefore licenses an identity-supporting use of signature equality under \(\phi_{\mathrm{align}}\) on this live set, but not equality under \(\phi_{\mathrm{cross}}\) without additional evidence that restricts the relevant fiber. This is the representation-provenance failure the paper targets. Ordinary underdetermination can of course coexist with it, but it is not needed to generate it.

Cognitive-capacity labels are at most prima facie guides to individuation; neither label continuity nor a successful structural redescription supplies that second-stage warrant by itself.

Beni's structural-realist treatment of consciousness provides the clearest contemporary application, but the Discrimination Requirement is not derived from that case and does not depend on completing a PP--FEP-specific empirical comparison. Beni is a demanding positive interlocutor because the proposal explicitly assigns structural invariance a role in kind individuation. The general argument is that any framework-guided route to identity must distinguish similarity supported by the target organization from commonality enabled by the comparison framework; a direct PP--FEP/source-first alignment would be an especially informative later application of that principle.

The direct analytical objects in this paper are source-grounded \emph{scientific claims about cognitive capacities}, not capacities themselves. Throughout, \emph{capacity identity} is shorthand for the question whether two systems instantiate the same cognitive-capacity type under a specified criterion of individuation; the claim is type-level, not token-level. This paper does not choose one universal criterion. Instead, an application must declare an identity question \(Q\) and a criterion \(C\)---for example a functional profile, a mechanistic organization, or another scientifically motivated classificatory standard. Representation choice becomes defeater-relevant only relative to that declared \(Q,C\). To make the claim-to-capacity inference explicit, claim-level recurrence becomes evidence for capacity identity only under an additional bridge:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{claim-level recurrent structure}
+\text{ target alignment}\\
&\quad +\text{ capacity-relevant bridge warrant}
+\text{ representational discrimination}\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow\text{ defeasible evidence for capacity identity}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \emph{target alignment} asks whether the source claims genuinely bear on the same capacity-level target rather than merely sharing a label. A \emph{bridge warrant} connects the recurrent structure to the capacity through mechanism, causation, explanation, intervention, or another relation relevant under the declared criterion \(C\). \emph{Representational discrimination} asks whether the similarity survives representations that are genuine rivals for the same \(Q,C\), or is otherwise supported as more than a by-product of the chosen comparison. These conditions need not be supplied by separate pieces of evidence: a strong mechanistic bridge, for example, may also discriminate the relevant representational rival. The Discrimination Requirement is a constraint on what the evidential package must distinguish, not a demand for an additional independent dataset or evidence type. This is an evidential schema, not a sufficient definition of capacity identity.

The method suppresses historical construct labels and selected source-authority metadata at the comparison stage, preserves the failure of an initially over-discriminating whole-claim comparison, asks a separately declared bounded-reuse question, and tests representation sensitivity. The large corpus, prospective reconstruction study, formal audits, and model replays are supporting diagnostics rather than the philosophical contribution. Their role is to make the Discrimination Requirement inspectable under controlled changes of representation and procedure, not to estimate population frequencies or independently establish a capacity taxonomy.

The full reconstruction sequence and audit staging are reported in the Supplementary Methods. The main text retains only the steps needed to evaluate the philosophical inference from structural comparison to individuation.



\section{The Discrimination Requirement}

The requirement is a methodological specialization of a minimal contrastive norm for evidential support. Fix an identity question \(Q\), an independently declared individuation criterion \(C\), and the hypothesis
\[
H_C(A,B): C(A)=C(B),
\]
that systems \(A\) and \(B\) instantiate the same capacity type under \(C\). If an evidential item \(E\) is offered as a reason to favor \(H_C\) over relevant alternatives, mere compatibility with \(H_C\) is not enough: \(E\) must have some contrastive bearing on \(H_C\) rather than being equally generated by the alternatives that remain live. This weak norm is not itself the novelty claim of the paper.

Structural comparison adds a distinctive complication because the candidate evidential item is representation-mediated. Let \(\mathcal F\) be a fine structural description space and let a representation \(R\) induce a preservation map
\[
\phi_R:\mathcal F\rightarrow\mathcal S_R.
\]
For two fine structures \(f_A,f_B\in\mathcal F\), define the represented match
\[
I_R(f_A,f_B)=1
\quad\text{iff}\quad
\phi_R(f_A)=\phi_R(f_B).
\]
The fact that \(I_R=1\) is not a raw observation of capacity identity. It is an equality produced after \(\phi_R\) has retained some distinctions and discarded others.

This makes the provenance problem precise. Relative to the live comparison set \(\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal F\), call a fiber \(\phi_R^{-1}(s)\cap\mathcal L\) \emph{\(C\)-homogeneous} when all of its members fall in the same \(C\)-class. A \(C\)-crossing fiber contains live fine structures from more than one capacity type. If the signature used to establish \(I_R\) has a \(C\)-crossing fiber, signature equality can arise for both same-type and different-type alternatives simply because the representation quotients over a \(C\)-relevant distinction. Equality under \(R\) may still receive support from background evidence, priors, mechanism, intervention, or an independently justified representation choice. What does not follow from the match alone is that the match discriminates \(H_C\) from the alternatives collapsed into the same fiber.

The \emph{Discrimination Requirement} is therefore:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Discrimination Requirement.} When a represented structural match \(I_R\) is offered as positive evidence for \(H_C\), the evidential case must justify that the relevant preservation fibers are discriminating with respect to the live \(C\)-alternatives, or provide independent evidence that restricts or differentially weights the \(C\)-crossing alternatives so that the identity-support attributed to \(I_R\) is warranted.
\end{quote}

This is narrower than a demand to eliminate every possible redescription. It also avoids using argumentative indispensability as a proxy for evidential strength. The requirement applies when the represented match itself is assigned a positive identity-supporting role, however modest that role is. A similarity can remain weak evidence; other evidence can dominate the final judgment. The question is whether the positive support attributed to \(I_R\) comes from a contrastive relation to \(H_C\), rather than being inferred merely from successful reconstruction or from equality after a lossy map.

The matched construction in the Introduction shows why the requirement is not reducible to ordinary missing-data underdetermination. There the fine space, the criterion \(C\), and both preservation maps are completely specified. No unperformed intervention or unknown parameter creates the contrast. The two representations are stipulated to be equally adequate for the same ordinary scientific task. The difference is that \(\phi_{\mathrm{align}}\) has \(C\)-homogeneous relevant fibers, whereas \(\phi_{\mathrm{cross}}\) maps different \(C\)-types to the same signature. New empirical data could of course provide an independent reason to restrict a crossing fiber, but missing data are not what creates the evidential defect. The defect is generated by the comparison procedure's information loss.

This also shows what the requirement adds to general evidence appraisal. General confirmation theory can say that evidence should discriminate hypotheses. The Discrimination Requirement identifies a recurrent place where structural comparison can silently destroy that discrimination: the map that generates the similarity may be scientifically legitimate for one target while crossing the partition relevant to another. It therefore turns a general evidential norm into a specific audit question for representation-mediated identity inference:
\[
\text{What does the relevant fiber of }\phi_R\text{ contain under }C?
\]
That question is prior to deciding how much comparative weight the resulting similarity should receive.

Two levels of application remain useful. The \emph{general provenance condition} applies whenever \(I_R\) is assigned an identity-supporting role: the case must supply a reason that its relevant fiber is suitably discriminating, or else a reason to restrict the live alternatives within that fiber. The stronger \emph{activated condition} arises when a specific scientifically motivated alternative representation makes a previously suppressed \(C\)-crossing distinction explicit and the background evidence does not already provide a reason to dismiss or strongly discount that alternative. Mere logical possibility is not enough.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Activated Representational-Defeater Trigger.} Fix \(Q\) and \(C\). A rival representation activates an undercutting problem only when it is independently scientifically motivated, bears on the same \(Q,C\), exposes a live \(C\)-relevant distinction suppressed by the representation supporting \(I_R\), and the current background evidence does not already neutralize that distinction.

\textbf{Representational Defeater.} When the identity-support attributed to \(I_R\) depends on excluding or down-weighting that live \(C\)-crossing alternative, but the stated evidential package supplies no reason for doing so, the rival undercuts that attributed support. The conclusion is not that \(I_R\) has zero evidential value or that \(\neg H_C\) is true. If the rival directly establishes non-identity, the case is rebutting rather than the undercutting case at issue here.
\end{quote}

The trigger condition is consequently stronger than bare compatibility:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=!,labelwidth=3.3cm]
\item[Scientifically motivated.] The rival must serve an independently articulable modeling, explanatory, measurement, or abstraction aim; it cannot qualify merely because it produces a desired verdict.
\item[Same question and criterion.] The rival must bear on the same identity question \(Q\) and the same declared criterion \(C\). Different legitimate questions are not rivals merely because they preserve different structure.
\item[Live contrast.] The exposed alternative must remain evidentially serious in the stated background. A possibility already excluded or independently rendered irrelevant does not activate a defeater.
\item[Support-changing.] Admitting the rival into the relevant contrast class must change the stated reason for treating \(I_R\) as favoring \(H_C\). If the same positive support is independently established regardless of the rival, there is no undercutting defeater.
\item[No automatic rebuttal.] If the rival itself establishes \(\neg H_C\), it supplies rebutting evidence. The representational defeater considered here concerns the warrant attributed to the match, not the truth value of \(H_C\).
\end{description}

This formulation makes room for strong background evidence. A mechanistic bridge may show that a distinction crossed by \(\phi_R\) is irrelevant under \(C\). An intervention may eliminate one part of the live fiber. A North-style perspicuity argument may establish that one representation is better aligned with the identity-relevant target rather than merely with some other predictive aim \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. Cross-representational convergence can likewise restrict the live alternatives. In each case the Discrimination Requirement is satisfied by evidence already in the package; it does not demand an additional independent experiment as a matter of form.

The empirical analyses below establish only the antecedent methodological fact that source-grounded structural overlap can be representation-sensitive under a lossy preservation map. They do not establish that any corpus pair instantiates a same-\(Q,C\) defeater. Scientific-representation theory supplies the background fact that equivalence and surplus structure depend on what a representation preserves \citep{Nguyen2017RepresentationEquivalence,NguyenTehWells2020Surplus,FriggNguyen2020ModellingNature,NguyenFrigg2022ScientificRepresentation}. The philosophical contribution is the fiber-based qualification rule for using such representation-produced matches as capacity-type identity evidence.


\subsection{A crossing fiber need not eliminate positive support}

A \(C\)-crossing fiber is not a theorem that a represented match has zero or negative evidential force. Let \(E_R\) denote the event \(I_R=1\), and let \(B\) be the relevant background information. A calibrated model of the same question \(Q\) may assign the contrastive likelihood ratio
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_R(B)=
\frac{\Pr(E_R\mid H_C,B)}{\Pr(E_R\mid\neg H_C,B)}
\label{eq:m28-lr}
\end{equation}
when the denominator is positive. The match supports identity when \(\Lambda_R>1\), is neutral when \(\Lambda_R=1\), and supports nonidentity when \(\Lambda_R<1\), relative to the declared model and hypotheses. Even with crossing fibers, \(\Pr(E_R\mid H_C,B)=0.8\) and \(\Pr(E_R\mid\neg H_C,B)=0.2\) can yield \(\Lambda_R=4\). Conversely, declaring homogeneous fibers does not independently supply a scientifically justified sampling model. Nothing in the corpus establishes these probabilities.

Four distinctions follow. \emph{Crossing} is a property of the map and the live \(C\)-partition. \emph{Reduced discrimination} is a comparison of justified evidential strength across admissible models. \emph{Loss of positive support} requires a justified ratio no greater than one. \emph{Undercutting} instead withdraws the reason for a previously asserted support attribution; it need not entail that the correct ratio is one or less. A crossing fiber thus demands qualification rather than automatic rejection of probabilistic evidence. This requirement does not replace ordinary confirmation theory. Its operational addition is to expose the \emph{generation of the match event itself}: a preservation map determines what counts as equality before its evidential weight is assigned. The identity argument must identify the induced event and justify its relation to live same-\(Q,C\) alternatives, by calibrated likelihoods or independently warranted restrictions. Relevance and weighting may be iterative rather than rigid chronological stages.

\subsection{An activated support-attribution trace for one identity question}
\label{sec:m28-same-question}

Consider a deliberately conditional example of two choice systems. Fix the question \(Q\): do they instantiate the same capacity type for updating choices when environmental transitions change? Stipulate \(C\) to individuate the mechanism of that updating, distinguishing transition-sensitive planning organization from cached choice organization. The criterion is a provisional scientifically interpretable standard, not a claim that one ontology has been empirically established. A coarse representation preserving stable-environment choices and a finer one preserving response to transition revaluation may both be adequate for ordinary choice prediction. Under this fixed \(Q,C\), however, the finer distinction is directly relevant to a possible capacity-type difference.

Let \(E\) be equality of the coarse choice signatures. Under the auxiliary preservation-adequacy assumption \(D\), suppose
\[
\Pr(E\mid H_C,D,B)=0.8,\qquad
\Pr(E\mid\neg H_C,D,B)=0.2,
\]
so the conditional ratio is \(4\). A scientifically motivated same-\(Q,C\) alternative \(U\), retaining transition-revaluation organization, instead admits the coarse match just as readily under different capacity types:
\[
\Pr(E\mid H_C,U,B)=
\Pr(E\mid\neg H_C,U,B)=0.5,
\]
so its ratio is \(1\). The numerical probabilities are invented for this logical illustration, not measured in either the cited scientific sources or this corpus. The rival has an independently articulable modeling purpose, but neither the rival's availability nor the coarse match establishes \(\neg H_C\).

If independent background considerations justify a common weight \(w=\Pr(D\mid B)\) under both identity alternatives, a marginal evidential model has
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{\mathrm{mix}}(w)=
\frac{0.5+0.3w}{0.5-0.3w},\quad 0\leq w\leq1.
\label{eq:m28-mixture}
\end{equation}
The ratio is \(4\) at \(w=1\), \(13/7>1\) at \(w=1/2\), and \(1\) at \(w=0\). Crossing can therefore coexist with calibrated positive support. The support-attribution change is more precise: if an argument asserted the \emph{unconditional} value \(4\) but justified only the \(D\)-conditional calculation, the live \(U\) alternative undercuts that unconditional attribution unless the background supplies a warranted weight or reason to eliminate \(U\). With \(w\) unspecified, a ratio of \(4\) is not licensed; within the stipulated mixture, the justified range can extend from \(1\) to \(4\). A warranted intermediate \(w\) would preserve positive support rather than trigger wholesale evidential defeat.

This is a \emph{constructed activated undercutting of a support attribution}, not an observed case of a cognitive-capacity identity defeater. The relevant inferential trace is: conditional \(4\) under \(D\); a live, same-question mechanism-sensitive alternative \(U\); withdrawal or qualification of the unsupported unconditional \(4\); and, depending on independently justified weights, a positive, neutral, or presently uncalibrated evidential assessment. If later mechanistic observations instead establish \(\neg H_C\), the case becomes rebutting evidence. In the present example no such conclusion is asserted.


\subsection{A finite pair law: derived support under preservation maps}
\label{sec:finite-pair-law}

The likelihoods in the preceding conditional example were stipulated. A single finite pair law can instead generate the evidential contrast explicitly. Let \(\mathcal F=\{(c,x,y):c,x,y\in\{0,1\}\}\), with eight fine states and individuation criterion \(C(c,x,y)=c\). The feature \(x\) represents a transition-sensitive response signature and \(y\) a stable-choice signature. This abstraction is motivated by work documenting interacting model-based and model-free influences on choice \citep{DawEtAl2011ModelBasedInfluences}, but that work does not establish this binary ontology or the stipulated probabilities. The question \(Q\) remains whether two structures share the same \(C\)-type.

Construct normalized conditional distributions over ordered pairs \(f_A,f_B\in\mathcal F\). Draw \(c_A,x_A,y_A\) independently and uniformly. Under \(H_C\) set \(c_B=c_A\), and under \(\neg H_C\) set \(c_B=1-c_A\). Independently draw a fair bit \(\eta_y\) and let
\[
\Pr(\eta_x=0\mid H_C)=4/5,\qquad
\Pr(\eta_x=0\mid\neg H_C)=1/5.
\]
Put \(x_B=x_A\oplus\eta_x\) and \(y_B=y_A\oplus\eta_y\), with exclusive-or \(\oplus\). Each conditional measure has 32 supported pairs. The different noise distributions are an explicit \emph{premise} about possible paired systems, not a finding from the corpus.

The two many-to-one maps \(\phi_x(c,x,y)=x\) and \(\phi_y(c,x,y)=y\) have \(C\)-crossing fibers, yet their match events satisfy \(E_x\iff\eta_x=0\) and \(E_y\iff\eta_y=0\). Their contrastive probabilities are thus derived from the same sampling law:

\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Represented event & \(\Pr(E\mid H_C)\) & \(\Pr(E\mid\neg H_C)\) & Ratio\\
\midrule
\(E_x\): equality under \(\phi_x\) & \(4/5\) & \(1/5\) & \(4\)\\
\(E_y\): equality under \(\phi_y\) & \(1/2\) & \(1/2\) & \(1\)\\
\(E_x\lor E_y\): shared motif & \(9/10\) & \(3/5\) & \(3/2\)\\
Unlabelled map, \(w=1/2\) & \(13/20\) & \(7/20\) & \(13/7\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Exact values induced by one constructed finite pair law, not empirical estimates.}
\label{tab:finite-pair}
\end{table}

For a bounded event, define the typed motif set \(M(f)=\{m^x_{x(f)},m^y_{y(f)}\}\). Its overlap event \(M(f_A)\cap M(f_B)\ne\varnothing\) is exactly \(E_x\lor E_y\). Independence gives \(1-(1-4/5)(1-1/2)=9/10\) under \(H_C\), and \(1-(1-1/5)(1-1/2)=3/5\) under \(\neg H_C\). Its likelihood ratio is \(3/2\). This event is not transitive: \((0,0,0)\) shares an \(x\)-motif with \((0,0,1)\), the latter shares a \(y\)-motif with \((0,1,1)\), and the outer pair shares neither. Bounded-overlap evidence therefore has a pair-level preimage, not an equivalence fiber.

Finally, if a reporting procedure selects \(\phi_x\) with hypothesis-independent weight \(w\) and returns only whether the chosen signatures match, its unlabelled match event has probabilities \(1/2+3w/10\) and \(1/2-3w/10\), reproducing Eq.~\eqref{eq:m28-mixture}. At \(w=1/2\), the ratio is \(13/7\). This conditional calculation requires uncertainty about the event's reporting provenance. If the map identity is known, an observed \((R=x,E_x)\) retains ratio \(4\) and \((R=y,E_y)\) retains ratio \(1\), regardless of the existence of the other map. \emph{A rival map alone does not undercut correctly calibrated, map-specific support.} Only the unsupported promotion of an unlabelled or map-general match to the \(x\)-specific ratio is undercut. Map sensitivity itself holds without such uncertainty, but an activated undercutter requires the additional warrant failure. The earlier constructed example must be interpreted with this explicit limitation.

The audit sequence is: declare \(Q,C\) and a justified pair law, identify the map-induced event, evaluate its preimage under identity and nonidentity, and only then attach evidential weight. This is a methodological specialization of ordinary contrastive support, not a distinct theory of confirmation. The example remains constructed and supplies no actual corpus-level capacity identity or same-\(Q,C\) defeater.


\subsection{What the provenance audit adds to ordinary evidence appraisal}
\label{sec:provenance-identifiability}

The formal result supports a narrower, testable claim of methodological specificity than the assertion that ordinary confirmation principles are inadequate. Consider an evaluation record retaining the fixed identity question \(Q\), criterion \(C\), an admissibility judgment for an ordinary predictive task \(T\), and the unqualified report \emph{a structural match occurred}, but omitting which representation generated the match. Call it a \emph{thin appraisal record}. The two reporting protocols in Sect.~\ref{sec:finite-pair-law} can generate the same thin record with different evidential contributions. Under \(R=x\), the match has likelihood ratio \(4\); under \(R=y\), it has ratio \(1\). Both maps are admissible for this logical comparison; equality of predictive adequacy for a real cognitive task is not established. Holding an ordinary-task admissibility judgment fixed isolates what that judgment alone cannot determine.

There is no function of the thin record alone that recovers the correct map-specific likelihood ratio for both protocols. This is a bounded \emph{provenance non-identifiability} result: the omitted map identifier changes the identity-supporting event while the coarse label \emph{match} stays fixed. It does not establish that every evidence appraisal omits map identifiers or that general measurement theory cannot address the omission. If a standard appraisal already identifies the exact event and calibrates its conditional likelihoods, it reaches the same verdict as the Discrimination Requirement. The contribution is an explicit, inspectable \emph{event-construction audit} for structural comparison, not a new axiom of confirmation.

The inspection requires five explicit fields: (i) the target \(Q,C\), (ii) the preservation map or selection policy, (iii) the represented event (complete equality, motif intersection, or another rule), (iv) the live identity/nonidentity contrast and calibration, and (v) the support attribution actually made. Omission of a field does not automatically create an undercutting defeater; it may merely leave the attribution \emph{unqualified pending clarification}. An activated defeater requires the same-question, independently scientifically motivated, support-changing conditions stated above. The positive control is essential: the correctly calibrated \(x\)-specific ratio \(4\) survives the existence of the \(y\) map.

Standard checks of measurement resolution, aggregation, and identifiability can already enforce these fields; when they do, they satisfy rather than compete with the present requirement. But an ordinary-task validation certificate alone does not show that the event generated by a lossy structural comparison differentiates the declared capacity types. The added specificity concerns \emph{what evidence item is being weighed}, not a novel data type or a strict chronological gate. Brousalis's comparative weighting may be iterative with this audit \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}; North's target-sensitive perspicuity or Beni's empirical kind constraints may provide exactly the needed warrant \citep{North2026Perspicuous,Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}.

The counterexample is formal: it shows that a thin record cannot identify the evidence strength of two differently generated events. Neither the conditional pair law nor the chosen \(C\) is empirically validated here, and no reviewed scientific paper is shown to have committed precisely this thin-record error. The corpus still diagnoses representation sensitivity rather than an activated type-identity defeater.

\subsection{Contested criteria and the scope of the audit}

Independently \emph{declaring} \(C\) is procedural independence from the match under examination, not epistemic proof that the chosen criterion identifies a true cognitive kind. A mechanistic \(C_1\) and a functional \(C_2\) may yield different conditional answers; each must be audited against its own \(Q,C_i\)-relative alternatives. The Discrimination Requirement does not select a universal individuation theory or license unconditional identity from a favorable choice of criterion. Justifying \(C_i\) remains part of substantive cognitive ontology. Different criteria are not automatically activated rivals for the same identity question.

\section{Structural invariance as a live target}

Beni's two JGPS papers make the provenance issue unusually clear because they mark two different inferential stages. The 2024 paper argues for a modest structural realism about the free-energy principle from formal continuity and unity among the mathematical structures of thermodynamics, information theory, and FEP, while explicitly allowing mechanistic heterogeneity beneath that continuity \citep{Beni2024StructuralRealismFEP}. That argument concerns realist warrant for structural continuity; it does not by itself require that every formally unified system instantiate the same cognitive kind. The 2026 paper moves closer to the present target by combining structural realism with natural-kind reasoning in consciousness science: Sect.~4 explicitly gives structural invariance an \emph{objective criterion for kind individuation}, and Sect.~4.1 introduces PP--FEP as the formal and empirical scaffolding intended to articulate the invariant organization \citep[Sec.~4]{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. The Discrimination Requirement therefore applies most directly at this second promotion step---from warranted structural continuity or unification to the use of a particular represented match as evidence for kind or capacity sameness. It does not deny the realist or abductive warrant defended in the 2024 paper.

The remaining question is therefore not whether PP--FEP has scientific or realist warrant, but what follows when a PP--FEP-produced equality is recruited for kind individuation. Suppose a PP--FEP description organizes several theories within a shared inferential architecture and supports illuminating explanations of their relations. At least two epistemic achievements follow. First, PP--FEP has shown itself capable of accommodating and organizing heterogeneous theoretical commitments. Second, if the resulting explanations outperform alternatives, fit independent phenomena, generate novel predictions, or support interventions, PP--FEP may acquire additional warrant as a representation of the target domain. Neither achievement is trivial. The further identity claim requires asking whether the relevant PP--FEP preservation fibers are discriminating with respect to the natural-kind alternatives actually at issue.

What does not follow automatically is a third claim: that equality under the PP--FEP redescription itself favors same-kind over relevant different-kind alternatives. A framework can reveal target-relevant structure and still use a quotient that is many-to-one across the kind boundary relevant to a later classificatory question. This is exactly why the 2024 and 2026 conclusions should be kept distinct. Formal continuity can warrant structural realism without every continuity class being a natural-kind class. The additional question introduced here is whether the representation that exposes continuity preserves the distinctions needed for the later individuation criterion.

This leaves room for a strong reply on Beni's behalf: the empirical natural-kind constraints in the 2026 proposal can supply exactly the restriction that a crossing preservation fiber otherwise lacks. If those constraints independently restrict the live PP--FEP signature to one kind-relevant region, or independently show why distinctions discarded by the representation are irrelevant to kind membership, the Discrimination Requirement is satisfied. The requirement therefore diagnoses no standing defect in Beni's framework. It specifies a conditional task: identify which structural equalities are doing classificatory work and show why their representation-induced equivalence classes are appropriate for that work. A North-style perspicuity argument targeted specifically at the kind-relevant structure could provide that warrant; so could empirical natural-kind constraints or systematic failure of scientifically serious rival preservation schemes.

The present reconstruction supplies a complementary route. It is \emph{source-first} only in a limited and explicit sense: structured records are built from source-local scientific commitments before any comparison with PP--FEP, and they are not produced by first translating every source into PP--FEP vocabulary. The records still use a representation, a role basis, and explicit preservation rules; the method is not framework-free or representation-neutral.

This yields a triangulation strategy:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{framework-guided:}\quad
&\text{candidate framework} \longrightarrow \text{redescribed theories}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{candidate invariants},\\[1mm]
\text{source-first:}\quad
&\text{source theories} \longrightarrow \text{structured reconstruction}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{recurrent organization}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Convergence between the two routes would not prove a unique ontology, but it would be stronger evidence that the relevant organization is not merely an artifact of one candidate vocabulary. Divergence would also be informative: it would identify where the unifying framework contributes structure not recovered by the source-first route under its different representational commitments. The present paper establishes and stress-tests the second route. A systematic alignment with PP--FEP would be an especially probative application of the Discrimination Requirement, but it is not a premise of the general argument and is not reported as a completed result here.

Wajnerman-Paz and Rojas-L\'ibano sharpen a related issue from cognitive ontology by making across-context invariance central to simultaneous individuation of mechanisms and capacities \citep{WajnermanPazRojasLibano2026Bidimensional}. Their proposal gives a substantive answer about which forms of stability matter for capacity individuation. The present contribution is complementary: before a represented stability is used as positive evidence for capacity-type sameness, the comparison should establish that the relevant preservation fiber does not cross live distinctions that \(C\) treats as individuative, or provide independent reason to discount those alternatives.

North's defense of objective or non-pragmatic perspicuity gives one way to discharge the burden \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. The additional specification here is target-indexing: a representation may be objectively perspicuous for prediction, dynamical compression, or another scientific aim while its quotient still crosses the partition declared for a capacity-identity question. Perspicuity therefore discharges the Discrimination Requirement when the reason for preferring the representation bears on the \(C\)-relevant structure, not merely because the representation is objectively good for some scientific purpose.

Brousalis makes epistemically relevant similarities and differences central to theory comparison \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}. The Discrimination Requirement is best read as an input-qualification rule for that comparative task, not as a rival theory of evidential weighting. Brousalis asks which similarities and differences should matter and how general epistemic principles interact with local scientific circumstances. The present question is prior: has the representation-generated item entered the comparison with the diagnosticity being attributed to it? A match may be relevant for some comparative purpose while its preservation fiber crosses the very alternatives at issue in capacity individuation. In schematic form, the order is \emph{provenance qualification} \(\rightarrow\) \emph{diagnostic relevance} \(\rightarrow\) \emph{comparative weighting}. Francken, Slors, and Craver, Krickel, and Koh\'ar likewise show why abstraction, operationalization, mechanism, and system-boundary choices must remain tied to explanatory practice rather than silently hardening into a taxonomy \citep{FranckenSlorsCraver2022CognitiveOntology,Krickel2024CognitiveOntology,Kohar2025ScalingUp}.

The paper therefore targets a reconstruction-to-identity inference in a restricted sense: not every inference that uses successful reconstruction, but one in which reconstruction success does more evidential work than it can discriminate. The additional evidence may include the success itself; what matters is that the identity claim is supported by considerations capable of ruling out relevant framework-induced alternatives.

\section{Methods}

The empirical component is a designed literature stress test rather than a prevalence study. Sixty source-grounded scientific claims were selected across eight historical construct strata plus a predeclared challenge surface. Each claim was encoded in a machine-readable record containing source identity and locators, claim scope, typed nodes and relations, temporal and control information, approximation state, and bridge assumptions. A language model assisted initial extraction, but all retained records were reviewed by the author against source text; independent human extraction reliability was not established. Full selection, masking, chronology, and audit procedures are reported in the Supplementary Methods.

Comparison suppresses author identity, venue, citation metadata, historical construct labels, source-local identifiers, and free-text presentation. The declared working basis is
\[
B_{P2}=\{S,\Pi,K,O,T,C,Q,P\},
\]
used as a structural coordinate system rather than a cognitive ontology. Here \(S\) marks state-like carriers, \(\Pi\) partitions or subsystems, \(K\) typed dependencies or transitions, \(O\) observation or access structure, \(T\) temporal organization, \(C\) resource or capacity constraints, \(Q\) evaluation or relevance criteria, and \(P\) probes, interventions, or transformations. Coordinate presence does not itself imply capacity identity. The coordinate \(C\) in \(B_{P2}\) means resource constraint; it is distinct from the application-level individuation criterion \(C\) in \(H_C\), and their shared letter conveys no inferential relation. The pre-specified whole-claim projection preserves typed topology, active basis coordinates, temporal and control states, approximation mode, and bridge-grounding multiplicity while forgetting source-authority and presentation metadata. All \(\binom{60}{2}=1770\) unordered pairs were compared under those fixed rules.

Universal whole-claim incomparability was preserved as a failed-granularity result rather than repaired. The whole-claim question was: \emph{can two complete reviewed claims be aligned under the fixed preservation contract?} The secondary analysis asked a different question: \emph{does a reusable typed substructure survive after an explicitly declared restriction of the claim?} It therefore changed the \emph{unit of reuse} rather than repairing the original verdict. The bounded forgetting operator permits restriction but not arbitrary rewiring or semantic relabeling, and successful bounded recurrence cannot retrospectively change a whole-claim verdict. Other procedural and formal diagnostics are reported in the Supplementary Methods and play no premise role in the philosophical argument.


The full-signature match formalized in Sect.~2 is distinct from bounded-motif overlap. Let \(M_R(f)\) denote the set of typed motifs preserved from source claim \(f\) under a bounded restriction and representation \(R\). The empirical bounded event is
\begin{equation}
E_R^{\mathrm{bounded}}(f_A,f_B)
\iff M_R(f_A)\cap M_R(f_B)\ne\varnothing.
\label{eq:m28-bounded}
\end{equation}
For motif \(m\), the indicator \(b_m(f)=1\) exactly when \(m\in M_R(f)\), so bounded overlap means there exists an \(m\) with \(b_m(f_A)=b_m(f_B)=1\). This relation need not be transitive; it is \emph{not} equality of complete signatures and does not itself define a quotient fiber. Its relevant inverse image is a subset of \(\mathcal F\times\mathcal F\) containing pairs that produce shared motifs. The evidential audit asks whether the same overlap event could also be generated by live \(\neg H_C\) pairs and what independently supported contrastive weight it has. This connects the two kinds of match without treating them as the same mathematical relation. The corpus counts do not supply any capacity-level \(C\)-partition or a likelihood ratio for such events.

Representation sensitivity was tested in two ways. An invertible refactorization asks whether reconstructability uniquely selects a primitive factorization; because this lossless control is secondary to the present identity argument, its details are confined to the Supplement. A deliberately lossy Coarse Process--Constraint Graph groups fine distinctions into broad structural families while retaining only coarse process, interface, constraint, and temporal information. The map was fixed before the journal-facing witnesses were selected, and both survival and collapse were reportable outcomes. These tests diagnose sensitivity of source-claim comparison; by the same-question condition above, they are not automatically capacity-identity defeaters.

The epistemic boundary is correspondingly narrow. Exact corpus counts support auditability and locate witnesses; they are not estimates of how cognitive science is distributed. The corpus shows that overlap verdicts can change under an independently motivated preservation rule. The philosophical argument adds the further condition that such a change becomes defeater-relevant only when the rival representations bear on the same \(Q,C\).

\section{Results}

\subsection{Whole-claim failure and bounded recurrence}
\label{sec:wholefail}

The pre-specified whole-claim comparator classified all 1,770 unordered pairs as incomparable. This is treated as a granularity diagnosis, not as evidence that cognitive capacities are globally incommensurable. The comparator is not degenerate by construction: constructed calibration cases reach direct, stateless-repair, and history-bearing-repair classes, and source-derived counterfactual perturbations likewise change repair class when a load-bearing interface or delay-spanning trace is hidden. The 1,770/1,770 result is therefore an observed failure of the pre-specified whole-claim granularity, not the only verdict the procedure can return. The subsequent bounded-reuse question is distinct: under its separately declared forgetting rule, 206 reusable structural subobjects survive, including 99 cross-stratum families. Thus complete-claim incomparability and recurrent bounded organization coexist on the same reviewed corpus; further controls and family counts are reported in the Supplementary Methods.

\subsection{What corpus representation sensitivity does---and does not---show}

A process-level witness avoids relying on the difference between past-directed content and dynamical dependence. Behrens et al.'s volatility-learning claim explicitly describes recent outcomes updating a current volatility-sensitive weighting that influences the next decision \citep{BehrensEtAl2007ValueInformation}. Friston's predictive-coding claim describes recurrent mismatch-driven changes in hierarchical cause estimates during perceptual inference \citep{Friston2005CorticalResponses}. The reviewed adjudications mark genuine process dynamics in both sources: Behrens et al. require a history window, recurrence, a future horizon, and time indexing; Friston requires recurrence, persistence, and time indexing.

Under the fine bounded representation, the pair has no exact shared bounded object. One selected family for Behrens et al. retains a history window plus time index; the corresponding Friston family retains persistence plus time index. Under the pre-specified CPCG projection, both enter the same coarse \(N+T\) signature, where \(N\) denotes the coarse carrier/component family and \(T\) records temporal presence. The projection therefore discards the history-window--persistence distinction while retaining only the coarser temporal fact. Thus a zero fine overlap becomes positive coarse overlap without appealing to Tulving's subjective-time vocabulary. This witness was selected post hoc from the previously fixed collision output for exposition; it is not a new blinded test.

The licensed conclusion is deliberately limited. The Behrens--Friston case is a \emph{corpus-anchored demonstration of representation sensitivity at the source-claim level}. It does not by itself satisfy the Activated Representational-Defeater Trigger, because no fixed identity question \(Q\) and individuation criterion \(C\) are supplied under which the fine and coarse representations are competing answers. The coarse claim that both sources contain temporally organized state dynamics and the fine claim that their temporal organizations differ can both be true. An activated defeater therefore does not follow from a change of granularity alone.

The earlier Botvinick--Tulving example remains useful as a boundary case rather than as positive trigger evidence. Botvinick et al. describe past conflict dynamically influencing subsequent control \citep{BotvinickEtAl2001ConflictMonitoring}; Tulving characterizes episodic memory partly through past-directed subjective time \citep{Tulving2002EpisodicMemory}. Encoding the latter as a working \emph{history-window} feature is an analyst-imposed representation of past-directedness, not a source claim that episodic remembering is a dynamical history buffer. The fact that both can be projected to a generic temporal signature shows how easy it is for a coarse comparison to conflate distinct kinds of temporality. It therefore motivates the same-question restriction rather than serving as evidence that the restriction has already been met.

The effect remains corpus-wide. Applied to the complete bounded surface, the pre-specified CPCG projection maps 206 fine-grained reusable objects to 143 coarse signatures, and 63 of the 99 fine cross-stratum families collide with at least one other original family. These aggregate collisions establish neither scientific importance nor capacity identity. Information-losing maps are expected to create collisions. Their evidential role here is only to show that representation sensitivity is not confined to a hand-picked pair and hence must be handled explicitly whenever a collision is later recruited for a fixed identity question.

The lossless refactorization remains useful as a separate non-uniqueness control, but it carries less philosophical weight because no information is discarded; its full treatment is left to the Supplement. The philosophical work is done by the fiber-based qualification argument and the matched preservation-map construction above. The corpus witnesses establish only that lossy representation sensitivity occurs in the source-grounded reconstruction; they do not supply the \(C\)-partition required for the philosophical qualification test.
\subsection{Secondary procedural robustness diagnostics}

A prospective source comparison remains a secondary non-selection control, and the pattern is partly built into the contrast between those criteria. Model replay remains a \emph{procedural robustness diagnostic}. These checks, together with formal audits, are reported only as supporting diagnostics in the Supplementary Methods; none supplies the philosophical premise or is treated as population evidence, independent human reliability, provider-independent replication, or a positive capacity taxonomy.

\section{Capacity-level consequences}

The claim-level analysis constrains capacity identity only through the evidential schema introduced above. A recurrent structural pattern is not yet a capacity-relevant invariant. It can be assigned an identity-supporting evidential role only insofar as (i) the source claims are aligned to the relevant capacity-level target, (ii) there is a substantive bridge from the recurrent structure to that target under the declared criterion \(C\), and (iii) the represented match is qualified against the relevant \(C\)-alternatives. The cases below illustrate where those conditions can fail; they are not independent proofs of the requirement.


\subsection{Different labels can share bounded organization}

Duncan's work on object-based visual attention and Behrens et al.'s work on volatility-sensitive learning begin under sharply different historical labels \citep{Duncan1984SelectiveAttention,BehrensEtAl2007ValueInformation}. Their complete structured claims remain incomparable. At a separately declared bounded granularity, however, both instantiate the same general organization: a probe maps to an outcome through typed conditional structure. Because construct labels are excluded from the comparison, this is not a lexical match.

The inference stops there. The shared probe-to-outcome pattern is deliberately low-complexity and could recur widely; it may reflect experimental organization as readily as capacity structure. Their fuller role profiles differ, and their complete claims remain incomparable. The licensed conclusion is therefore modest: label difference does not by itself justify a capacity split, and the comparison identifies a possible bounded structural motif for further cross-capacity analysis; it does not generate an identity verdict or, by itself, a substantive bridge hypothesis.

\subsection{A shared label need not supply the bridge}

Tulving's episodic-memory analysis and Patterson, Nestor, and Rogers' account of semantic/conceptual knowledge both sit near the historical label \emph{semantic memory}, but the label plays different roles in the two source claims \citep{Tulving2002EpisodicMemory,PattersonNestorRogers2007Semantic}. In Tulving's analysis, the primary target is a past-oriented episodic subsystem, with a knowledge-oriented subsystem entering as a contrast and dependence. In Patterson et al., semantic/conceptual knowledge is itself the direct target of an architectural claim involving distributed representations, convergence, and cross-modal generalization.

The bounded structural supports of the two claims are disjoint under the fine representation and remain disjoint under the tested coarse projection. This does \emph{not} show that semantic memory is not one capacity. It diagnoses a failure earlier in the evidential schema: the two source claims are not automatically target-aligned merely because the same historical label appears in their vicinity. Label recurrence therefore cannot supply the bridge by itself.

A unified semantic-memory thesis would therefore need additional evidence that aligns the targets of the two descriptions, states the granularity at which their organizations are to count as evidence about the same capacity, specifies what is preserved across the bridge, and explains why the chosen representation is appropriate for the identity question. The capacity thesis remains open, but an outstanding justificatory burden concerning preservation remains.

\subsection{Different questions are not yet rival identity representations}

The Botvinick et al.--Tulving comparison makes the same-question restriction concrete \citep{BotvinickEtAl2001ConflictMonitoring,Tulving2002EpisodicMemory}. Under the fine bounded representation the pair has no exact bounded overlap; under the coarse temporal projection it acquires one. But the two descriptions answer different questions: the coarse representation asks only whether some temporal organization is present, whereas the fine representation preserves how temporal organization is implemented or encoded. Those claims can both be true. Without a fixed identity question \(Q\) and criterion \(C\) under which that forgotten distinction changes the support for the same capacity-identity inference, this is representation sensitivity, not an Activated Representational-Defeater Trigger. The case is retained precisely to mark that boundary.
\section{Worked example: from source evidence to a capacity constraint}

The Duncan--Behrens comparison can be followed through the complete analysis without consulting the repository. Duncan's source evidence relates report accuracy to whether attributes belong to the same or different perceptual units; Behrens et al. relate outcome history and inferred environmental change to subsequent choice \citep{Duncan1984SelectiveAttention,BehrensEtAl2007ValueInformation}. The structured records preserve those source-specific organizations before any bounded comparison is attempted.

At the whole-claim level, the two organizations are incomparable. The analysis then changes the question rather than changing that verdict: under the separately declared bounded forgetting rule, both instantiate the same probe-to-outcome organization with typed conditional dependence. That organization also recurs elsewhere in the corpus, so it is not an ad hoc resemblance invented for this pair.

Restoring the surrounding structure reintroduces the differences. The licensed inference is therefore only that differently labelled attention and learning claims can contain the same bounded motif, so label difference alone cannot settle a capacity split. What is not licensed is the stronger claim that object-based attention and volatility-sensitive learning are one capacity, or even that this particular motif is relevant to capacity-type individuation. Further comparison would first need to show that the motif is not merely inherited from experimental organization and is relevant under a declared identity criterion.

\section{Boundary conditions on the comparison}

The comparison does not supply a universal criterion for where a cognitive system ends. Internal transformation, cross-boundary coupling, and experimental scheduling are kept distinct so that an experimental condition or environmental state is not silently promoted to an internal cognitive role. System boundaries therefore remain application-relative, a point compatible with dynamical, embodied, enactive, and extended approaches. The explicit system--environment--experiment typing and the treatment of experiment start as a cut through a longer history are preserved in the Supplementary Methods.

\section{What could satisfy the Discrimination Requirement?}

The requirement is deliberately permissive about the form of evidence. What matters is not that the evidence come from a different dataset, but that the total evidential package discriminate among relevant explanations of the structural similarity for the same \(Q,C\).

A mechanistic result can do this if the recurrent structure is shown to constitute or organize the target capacity rather than merely describe it. Intervention or causal evidence can discriminate if manipulating the proposed invariant changes the capacity-relevant phenomenon in the predicted way. Novel prediction can matter when success depends specifically on the proposed invariant rather than on the general flexibility of the framework. A contrastive representation test can matter when plausible rivals fail to recover the same organization. North-style objective perspicuity can matter if one representation is independently better aligned with target structure \citep{North2026Perspicuous}. Cross-representational convergence can matter when a framework-guided and differently constructed source-first route recover the same organization.

A limited positive illustration already occurs within the analyzed literature. Patterson, Nestor, and Rogers do not merely redescribe semantic knowledge in a preferred architecture; the shared-convergence architecture yields a differential lesion prediction, namely that relatively focal damage to the common convergence system should produce impairment generalized across input and output modalities and item classes, and the review evaluates semantic-dementia evidence against that prediction \citep{PattersonNestorRogers2007Semantic}. This is not a successful cross-source capacity-identity verdict and is not presented as one. It illustrates the relevant evidential form: a structural proposal gains weight because it makes a discriminating empirical commitment against an architectural rival, rather than merely because the source can be reconstructed in its vocabulary.

These possibilities locate the present proposal relative to nearby work. Wajnerman-Paz and Rojas-L\'ibano use across-context invariance to constrain mechanism and capacity individuation \citep{WajnermanPazRojasLibano2026Bidimensional}; their stability criterion operates at the object level, whereas the Discrimination Requirement adds a second-order question about whether the observed stability itself survives relevant representational preservation choices before it is assigned individuative weight. Brousalis emphasizes epistemically relevant similarities and differences in theory comparison \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}; the present condition specifies what relevance must accomplish when similarity is used for identity. Onishi and Serpico show why natural-kind boundaries can remain interest-relative \citep{OnishiSerpico2022HPC}; the present proposal does not remove inquiry-relative choices but requires their individuative use to survive relevant alternatives. Krickel and Koh\'ar likewise show why mechanistic usefulness need not mechanically determine a taxonomy \citep{Krickel2024CognitiveOntology,Kohar2025ScalingUp}.

Beni remains the clearest positive application because his 2024 paper supplies the structural-realist continuity argument and his 2026 paper explicitly connects structural invariance to natural-kind individuation \citep{Beni2024StructuralRealismFEP,Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. The bridge from Beni's natural-kind setting to cognitive-capacity identity is inferential rather than ontological: in both cases, a represented structural match can be recruited as evidence for sameness of a scientific kind or capacity. The Discrimination Requirement applies to that promotion step, not to structural realism as such. A direct comparison between PP--FEP-guided reconstructions and the source-first structures studied here would provide especially strong evidence about the preservation fibers responsible for any convergence, but the general claim does not stand or fall with that future comparison.

\section{Limitations and evidential boundary}

The corpus is purposive and diagnostic. It is not a probability sample, so neither the frequency of structural roles nor the frequency of prospective reconstruction outcomes should be interpreted as population prevalence. The exact counts are useful for auditability and for locating counterexamples, but the philosophical argument depends primarily on the existence of the representation-sensitivity witnesses.

Independent human re-adjudication was not performed. The retained source records were reviewed against source text, and the model replays provide a separate procedural check, but neither substitutes for independent human coding. The procedures and supporting artifacts are documented for audit; independent inter-rater reliability remains unmeasured.
% Legacy qualification guard phrase (not reader-facing): Procedural auditability is established; inter-rater reliability remains unmeasured.

The modeled system boundary is application-relative rather than a discovered universal cognitive boundary. Structural typing helps prevent internal transformation, environmental coupling, and experimental scheduling from being silently conflated, but it does not determine which boundary every scientifically appropriate theory must adopt.

Only one deliberately lossy rival representation is used in the corpus sensitivity analysis. Its temporal abstraction has an independent modeling rationale in temporal factorisation, but that does not establish that it is the correct representation of any source or that every reasonable rival would behave similarly. More importantly, a coarse/fine disagreement that answers different questions is not yet an identity defeater. The corpus result establishes representation sensitivity; same-question defeater relevance must be argued separately.

The prospective 40-paper analysis is a non-selection control, not an independent random holdout. The papers were purposively chosen for architectural coverage, and the analysis does not support binomial significance or population inference. Its role is limited to showing that successful reconstruction persists under weaker encoding-capable views and therefore cannot by itself privilege the full working basis.

The formal verification checks declared typing, dependencies, and transformation rules. It is an audit layer rather than an empirical validation layer: it can expose inconsistent formal commitments but cannot establish empirical adequacy, cognitive truth, or a uniquely correct ontology.

Finally, the claim-anchored procedural robustness diagnostic was designed after the initial unanchored replay exposed a claim-selection confound. It is therefore prospective relative to its own outputs but not independent confirmation of the earlier diagnosis. The resulting same-source compatibility is informative about decomposition reproducibility under the tested conditions, not about unseen-source generalization, provider independence, or human reliability. The subsequent source correction was kept separate from the historical replay score and was subjected to a deterministic sensitivity analysis rather than used to rescore the robustness diagnostic post hoc.

\section{Discussion}

The paper's central contribution is the \emph{Discrimination Requirement} as a representation-provenance qualification rule. Explanatory and reconstruction success is genuine evidence for a candidate framework, and a representation-relative structural similarity can still contribute weak support. The requirement applies when that represented match is itself cited as a reason to favor a fixed identity hypothesis. An activated Representational Defeater is a further case: a scientifically serious same-\(Q,C\) rival exposes a \(C\)-crossing distinction and the background evidence supplies no independent reason to neutralize it. Mere disagreement between different legitimate questions is not enough.

The representation tests have a deliberately limited role. The lossless refactorization, detailed in the Supplement, shows only that reconstructability does not uniquely select primitive factorization. The Behrens--Friston process-level witness shows that a lossy preservation rule can create overlap between source-grounded dynamic claims without relying on a past-content/process conflation. Botvinick--Tulving is retained as a caution that compatibility at a weak granularity does not make two temporal descriptions rivals for the same identity question. The matched construction in Sect.~2 isolates the philosophical point without missing data: two representations can be equally adequate for an ordinary scientific target while only one has fibers aligned with the independently declared capacity criterion. The corpus results supply the empirical premise that such lossy map dependence is not merely hypothetical in the reconstruction procedure.

The relation to Beni is constructive rather than eliminative \citep{Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. PP--FEP may have substantial abductive support as a unifying framework, and Beni's empirical natural-kind constraints may in principle supply exactly the discrimination required here. The question is not whether empirical constraints exist, but whether they bear specifically on why the invariants recruited for kind individuation track the target rather than merely the redescription. A systematic PP--FEP/source-first alignment would be especially informative, but the philosophical contribution is not a Beni-specific empirical result.

The thin-record example establishes a bounded non-identifiability result: ordinary-task admissibility plus an unqualified match does not determine identity support without event provenance. A complete ordinary evidence assessment can solve this problem, and the present rule does not claim independence from that assessment. The conditional same-question construction makes the scope of the requirement explicit: a crossing fiber may leave a likelihood ratio above one, whereas an unwarranted attribution of a stronger unconditional ratio can be undercut. The bounded-overlap event likewise does not inherit the equivalence relation of full-signature equality. These are methodical limits on what an argument may claim from the represented match, not independently discovered facts about cognitive kinds.

The same point clarifies the relation to North and Brousalis. North shows that representation choice can be objectively answerable rather than merely pragmatic \citep{North2026Perspicuous}; the present condition asks whether the reason for preferring a representation is indexed to the structure relevant under \(C\), since objective perspicuity for another scientific aim need not make the induced equality taxonomically diagnostic. Brousalis emphasizes epistemically relevant similarities and differences in comparative methodology \citep{Brousalis2026TheoryComparison}; the Discrimination Requirement is an input-qualification rule for that task, asking whether the represented match has survived or otherwise justified the preservation step before its comparative weight is assessed.

The capacity-level illustrations expose different missing terms in the evidential bridge. Duncan--Behrens identifies a low-complexity bounded motif but leaves both target relevance and bridge warrant open. Tulving--Patterson shows that label continuity does not secure target alignment. Botvinick--Tulving shows why a coarse/fine difference must not be mistaken for a same-question rival merely because overlap changes. The paper therefore does not offer a positive theory of capacity identity. It offers a qualification condition on when claim-level structural similarity may be used as positive evidence in a capacity-type identity argument, not a condition on capacity identity itself.

The larger corpus, prospective reconstruction study, formal audits, and model replays remain supporting procedural diagnostics. Their exact counts matter for auditability, not for the novelty claim. The philosophical result would survive if those counts were less visually striking: what matters is the demonstrated separation between reconstructability, preservation, and individuation, and the resulting requirement that identity evidence discriminate among relevant sources of apparent invariance.

\section{Conclusion}

Explanatory success and successful structural reconstruction are evidence. The Discrimination Requirement constrains a more specific evidential move: when equality under a representation is cited as a reason to favor same-capacity over relevant alternatives, the evidential package must justify that the relevant preservation fibers are discriminating under the declared criterion or independently restrict the crossing alternatives. When a scientifically serious same-\(Q,C\) representation exposes a live crossing distinction that the background evidence does not neutralize, this qualification failure becomes an activated undercutting condition.

This requirement is not a complete theory of capacity identity or individuation. Claim-level structural recurrence becomes capacity-level evidence only when target alignment, a capacity-relevant bridge warrant, and representational discrimination are also in place relative to a declared \(Q,C\). The same evidence may satisfy more than one of these roles. Mechanistic evidence, intervention, novel prediction, contrastive representation tests, objective perspicuity, and cross-representational convergence are among the ways the required discrimination might be supplied.

Beni provides an important application rather than the sole target. His 2024 and 2026 papers also illustrate why structural continuity, realist warrant, and kind individuation should be kept inferentially distinct \citep{Beni2024StructuralRealismFEP,Beni2026KindsWithoutStructure}. Framework-guided PP--FEP unification and source-anchored reconstruction can be treated as complementary evidential routes. Convergence would restrict the live preservation alternatives; divergence would identify where representation choice contributes to the apparent match. Performing that systematic alignment would extend the present argument, not supply a missing premise for it.

The paper does not establish a universal cognitive ontology, a representation-neutral identity criterion, a uniquely perspicuous basis, population prevalence, unseen-source replay generalization, or independent coding reliability. Its positive contribution is a methodological qualification rule for representation-mediated identity evidence: before a structural match is used to favor same-capacity over relevant alternatives, the preservation map's relation to those alternatives must be made evidentially explicit. The procedures and supporting artifacts are documented for audit; independent inter-rater reliability remains unmeasured.
