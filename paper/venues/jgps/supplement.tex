\section{Lossless and lossy representation controls}

The lossless control uses an invertible refactorization of the working basis. It preserves the information required to reconstruct the original role structure while changing which distinctions are first-class. This establishes non-uniqueness of primitive factorization without information loss.

The lossy control deliberately projects the 206 bounded objects into a coarser process--constraint representation. The 206 objects become 143 coarse signatures; 63 of the 99 original cross-stratum families collide; 62 coarse signatures retain cross-stratum support; and 58 of 60 claims participate in at least one cross-stratum coarse signature. These counts show both collapse and survival under one lossy rival and do not establish representation neutrality.

A process-level witness is supplied by Behrens et al.'s volatility-learning source (DOI \path{10.1038/nn1954}) and Friston's predictive-coding source (DOI \path{10.1098/rstb.2005.1622}). Both source records contain explicit dynamical updating. Their fine bounded representations have zero exact overlap for the selected temporal-state families, while the deliberately coarse projection places the selected history-window and persistence families in the same coarse temporal signature. This is evidence of source-claim representation sensitivity, not by itself a capacity-identity defeater.

The coarse temporal question has an independent modeling precedent. Treur's temporal-factorisation framework (Cognitive Systems Research 8(2):57--74, DOI \path{10.1016/j.cogsys.2006.08.002}) treats a past-pattern--future-pattern dependence as factorable through a mediating present-state property and explicitly applies that relation between history-based and state-based descriptions to cognitive dynamics. This does not establish that the fine temporal organizations in any witness are scientifically equivalent. It establishes only that temporal abstraction is an independently articulated cognitive-modeling move rather than a transformation justified solely by the overlap it creates. Such independent motivation is necessary but not sufficient for an Activated Representational-Defeater Trigger: the rival representations must also bear on the same declared identity question and individuation criterion.

\section{Capacity-level witness cases}

Three cases illustrate different inferential failures.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Shared label without a preserved bridge.} Tulving's episodic-memory analysis and Patterson, Nestor, and Rogers' semantic-knowledge analysis (DOI \path{10.1038/nrn2277}) remain structurally disjoint under both tested bounded representations. This does not refute a unified semantic-memory capacity; it shows that label continuity alone does not supply the bridge.
\item \textbf{Different labels with shared bounded organization.} Duncan's object-based attention source (DOI \path{10.1037/0096-3445.113.4.501}) and Behrens et al.'s volatility-sensitive learning source (DOI \path{10.1038/nn1954}) remain incomparable as complete claims but share a recurring bounded probe-to-outcome organization. This identifies a low-complexity motif for further comparison without establishing that the motif is relevant to capacity-type individuation.
\item \textbf{Different temporal questions.} The Botvinick--Tulving comparison has zero fine overlap and positive coarse overlap, but past-directed subjective content and dynamical dependence must not be treated as the same temporal property by default. The case is retained as a boundary marker: a coarse/fine verdict change is representation sensitivity, not yet a same-question defeater trigger.
\end{itemize}

For the question \emph{does each reviewed claim admit some temporal description?}, the coarse projection can be treated as a weak quotient of the reviewed representations without asserting equivalence of their temporal organization. For questions about dynamical implementation, past-directed content, or capacity identity, that compatibility is insufficient by itself. The crucial provenance boundary is explicit: Tulving supplies past-directed temporal content; \emph{history window} is the comparison scheme's encoding of that content. The disagreement therefore records representation sensitivity before the defeater-trigger stage, not a live same-question identity rival.

Across these cases, the source locators and retained record summaries predate this journal-facing rewrite. The Behrens--Friston witness is an expository post hoc selection from previously fixed collision results; no new corpus execution or recoding was performed. The full machine-readable records and representation-control outputs are linked through the supplementary-materials index.

\section{Constructed reachability controls}

Constructed controls test whether the comparison procedures are capable of returning alternatives to the observed corpus outcome. A matched synthetic calibration includes direct cases, cases repairable by an explicit stateless mapping, and cases that require a history-bearing repair. The procedure reaches all three designed classes. Two source-derived counterfactual perturbations likewise show that hiding a load-bearing interface can require a stateless repair, whereas hiding a delay-spanning trace can require added persistent state.

These controls demonstrate reachability of the classification rule. They do not modify the observed prospective source results and are not prevalence estimates.

\section{System--environment--experiment typing}

The structural comparison distinguishes the modeled cognitive system \(G_{\mathrm{cog}}\), environment \(W\), cross-boundary coupling \(\Gamma\), experimental protocol \(E_{\mathrm{exp}}\), and a realized finite run/history \(R\). Three non-identities are preserved:

\[
K\neq\Gamma,\qquad
C\neq\text{experimental condition},\qquad
P_{\mathrm{in}}/P_{\mathrm{out}}\neq\text{experimental schedule}.
\]

The distinction is architectural rather than isolationist. A dynamical, embodied, enactive, or extended model may draw the system boundary broadly and may treat environment coupling as constitutive. The method requires the selected boundary to be stated so that an experimental manipulation or environmental carrier is not automatically redescribed as an internal cognitive-system role.

A finite experiment is also treated as a cut \(t_0\) through a potentially longer history rather than as the beginning of the cognitive system. Admissibility of \(X_{t_0}\), the realized state at the cut, and the experimental preparation that helped produce it are kept distinct.

\section{Independent human-coding boundary}

Independent human re-adjudication has not been performed. A pre-specified human-coding protocol exists for source-level reconstruction and downstream comparison, but no independent human response file is part of the evidence reported here.

The stronger source-level protocol requires a second human to begin from the original publication and independently construct a structured scientific claim without access to the retained reference reconstruction, bounded memberships, role-projection verdicts, capacity-case outcomes, or answer key. Only after the independent records are complete may alignment with the retained records be scored.

Metadata masking of already constructed records would test a different and weaker question. It cannot establish that the original semantic coding was conceptually blind to the constructs under discussion. For this reason no language-model replay, deterministic CI check, or formal verification result is counted as independent human inter-rater evidence.

\section{Conditional evidence calibration and motif-event audit}

The worked same-question example in the main article is deliberately \emph{conditional}. It supplies no empirical likelihood estimates and does not classify any source in the reviewed corpus. The question concerns type identity for transition-sensitive choice updating under a provisional mechanistic criterion \(C\). The coarse observed event \(E\) is equality of stable-choice signatures. The two conditional likelihood specifications are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Condition & \(\Pr(E\mid H_C,B)\) & \(\Pr(E\mid\neg H_C,B)\)\\
\midrule
\(D\): diagnostic coarse preservation & \(0.8\) & \(0.2\)\\
\(U\): mechanism-sensitive live rival & \(0.5\) & \(0.5\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All four entries are stipulated for conceptual analysis. Under a common weight \(w\) for condition \(D\) on either hypothesis, the marginal likelihood ratio is \((0.5+0.3w)/(0.5-0.3w)\). It is \(4\), \(13/7\), or \(1\) for \(w=1\), \(1/2\), or \(0\), respectively. If admissible conditional model weights differ by hypothesis, the marginal formula changes and requires explicit justification. Without such support, the earlier unconditional claim of a ratio \(4\) is unwarranted even though positive evidence is not ruled out. This is an audit of an inferential entitlement, not an empirical posterior calculation or a claim that the two illustrative mechanisms exhaust the scientifically relevant competitors.

Complete-signature equality and bounded reuse refer to different binary events. In the first case equality of \(\phi_R(f_A)\) and \(\phi_R(f_B)\) creates a genuine equivalence relation. In the second, \(\exists m\in M_R(f_A)\cap M_R(f_B)\) establishes a shared typed motif. The latter need not be transitive and therefore cannot be treated as an equality fiber. In particular, one pair may share motif \(m_1\), another pair \(m_2\), and their outer pair none. A pair-level inverse-image audit can examine overlap under live identity and nonidentity alternatives, but the present corpus does not establish a distribution over such pairs conditional on cognitive-capacity type.

The individuation criterion is declared procedurally for a fixed identity question rather than inferred as a uniquely true taxonomy. The example establishes no actual same-question defeater among the source papers, changes no corpus count, and does not add independent human recoding.


\section{Finite pair-law derivation and representation-specific events}

The new finite example is an exact constructed probability model, not a source-based empirical estimate. There are eight fine states \(\mathcal F=\{0,1\}^3\), with \(f=(c,x,y)\) and provisional individuation \(C(f)=c\). For either hypothesis \(h\in\{H_C,\neg H_C\}\), take independent uniform \(c_A,x_A,y_A,\eta_y\), and a conditional bit \(\eta_x\) with match probability \(q_h\), where \(q_{H_C}=4/5\) and \(q_{\neg H_C}=1/5\). Set \(c_B=c_A\) under \(H_C\), \(c_B=1-c_A\) under \(\neg H_C\), and set \(x_B=x_A\oplus\eta_x\), \(y_B=y_A\oplus\eta_y\).

These rules define normalized probability distributions and give an explicit mass for every supported ordered pair:
\[
\Pr(f_A,f_B\mid h)=
\begin{cases}
q_h/16,&x_A=x_B\ \text{and the specified }c_B,y_B,\\
(1-q_h)/16,&x_A\ne x_B\ \text{and the specified }c_B,y_B,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Here the specified \(c_B\) means equality or complement according to \(h\), and the \(y_B\) outcome is either fair-bit possibility. For each \(h\), exactly 16 pairs satisfy \(x_A=x_B\) and 16 satisfy \(x_A\ne x_B\), so the total mass is \(16q_h/16+16(1-q_h)/16=1\). The pointwise mass is not the marginal probability of a match. The pair law deliberately stipulates \(q_h\); the subsequent probabilities are derived from that rule rather than inserted as independent likelihoods.

Both preservation maps \(\phi_x(f)=x\) and \(\phi_y(f)=y\) have two fibers of size four, each containing two \(c=0\) and two \(c=1\) states. For \(E_x\) the pair probabilities are \(q_h\); for \(E_y\), they are \(1/2\). Define distinct motif symbols \(m^x_0,m^x_1,m^y_0,m^y_1\) and \(M(f)=\{m^x_{x(f)},m^y_{y(f)}\}\). The shared-motif event is \(E_x\cup E_y\), which has probability \(q_h+1/2-q_h/2\), hence \(9/10\) under \(H_C\) and \(3/5\) under \(\neg H_C\), with a likelihood ratio \(3/2\). Nontransitivity is witnessed by \((0,0,0),(0,0,1),(0,1,1)\), whose adjacent pairs share respectively \(x\)- and \(y\)-motifs, but whose outer pair shares none.

The unlabelled-report mixture is a \emph{different evidential event}. Draw a reporting-map indicator \(R=x\) with weight \(w\), independent of the two identity hypotheses, or \(R=y\) otherwise. If the indicator is discarded and only the selected signature-match event \(E_R\) is reported, then \(\Pr(E_R\mid h)=wq_h+(1-w)/2\). This yields ratio \((1/2+3w/10)/(1/2-3w/10)\), including \(13/7\) when \(w=1/2\). If the indicator is observed, the joint report \((R=x,E_x)\) has probabilities \(w(4/5)\) and \(w(1/5)\), and therefore ratio \(4\) when \(w>0\). A legitimate rival map does not erase that support. The preceding numerical \(D/U\) conditional-model illustration and the present map-indexed construction share the same arithmetic but must not be identified without the explicit reporting provenance law; one compares conditional models and the other compares map-generated events under a common pair law.

The example does not establish that \(\phi_x\) and \(\phi_y\) are equally adequate for the \emph{identity} target, identify the correct \(C\), or estimate how real systems distribute over the stipulated eight structures. Empirical mechanisms may combine model-based and model-free strategies. The construction is a finite audit of a conditional inferential form, not evidence that any analyzed literature pair instantiates a same-question undercutting defeater.


\section{Provenance non-identifiability and audit controls}

The finite pair law permits a direct test of what a summary of ordinary scientific adequacy and represented similarity actually determines. Fix \(Q,C\) and the pair law. Let a target-observation procedure sample a fine source state \(f=(c,x,y)\) uniformly and an independent fair selector \(J\), and define \(T=x\) if \(J=0\), and \(T=y\) otherwise. A representation returning \(x\) predicts \(T\) correctly with probability
\[
\Pr(T=x)=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\Pr(x=y)
=\tfrac12+\tfrac14=\tfrac34.
\]
The \(y\)-map has the same accuracy \(3/4\) by symmetry. Since \(x\) and \(y\) are independent fair bits in the source-state marginal under either identity hypothesis, the result holds under both. A common, explicitly chosen admissibility threshold \(2/3\) therefore yields \(A_T=1\) for both protocols. This is a \emph{derived match in ordinary-task predictive accuracy within the toy model}, not an empirical finding about any real cognitive task. A complete reporting record includes \((Q,C,A_T,R,E_R)\), where \(R\in\{x,y\}\) identifies the preservation map. The \emph{thin} record deletes \(R\), retaining \((Q,C,A_T,E_R)\).

For a positive match, the two complete records \((R=x,E_x=1)\) and \((R=y,E_y=1)\) project to the same thin record. Yet their likelihood ratios under the common, explicit pair law are respectively \(4\) and \(1\). Consequently, no single function of the thin record can return the correct map-specific likelihood ratio for both complete records. This is a counterexample to recovering the support attribution from a match label plus ordinary-task admissibility, \emph{not} a theorem that all ordinary evidence evaluation necessarily loses provenance.

If the reporting map is unknown but chosen with independently justified probability \(w\) conditional on neither hypothesis, the thin event has ratio \((1/2+3w/10)/(1/2-3w/10)\). Without a justified \(w\) it has no unique calibrated ratio, though the stipulated family bounds it between \(1\) and \(4\). If \(R=x\) is retained in the report, it gives a correctly qualified likelihood ratio of \(4\) and must not be defeated simply because \(R=y\) was another defensible mapping. Conversely, if a competent generic measurement audit records \(R\), the exact event preimage, relevant pair distributions, and a supported \(C\), that audit is sufficient for the Discrimination Requirement; no extra independent evidence or novel confirmation principle is required.

For reproducibility the audit records five fields: target \((Q,C)\), map or map-selection rule, match-event semantics, admissible identity contrasts and calibration, and the claimed support attribution. Its outputs are \emph{qualified}, \emph{neutral}, \emph{underqualified}, or \emph{outside the fixed question}; an activated undercutter is a further epistemic diagnosis requiring a live, scientifically motivated, support-changing same-question rival. Missing provenance without such a rival is an incomplete evidential case, not automatically a positive defeater. The bounded-motif event has a known pair-level preimage and ratio \(3/2\) in the constructed law despite its nontransitivity. No positive control upgrades that construction to a real-world capacity identity result.

The constructed ordinary-task prediction is deliberately selected for analytic symmetry and does not by itself establish scientific appropriateness. The underlying scientific comparison, its task-adequacy judgment, the appropriateness of \(C\), and the toy distribution remain separate evidential obligations. It is not established that the \(x\) and \(y\) representations have equal empirical predictive adequacy in a real cognitive task. The diagnostic does not claim that routine instrument calibration, aggregation audit, or likelihood-based evidence appraisal is generally defective; it demonstrates where a structurally compressed comparison record can become insufficient.


\section{Source-anchored semantic architecture and conditional capacity identity}

The article distinguishes Patterson, Nestor and Rogers' actual scientific model comparison from a subsequently posed \emph{conditional capacity-identity question}. Their 2007 \emph{Nature Reviews Neuroscience} review (8:976--987, DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2277}{10.1038/nrn2277}) asks whether the modality-specific cortical network is sufficient for semantic knowledge (section A semantic hub?, Fig.~1, pp.~976--977). Fig.~1 contrasts task-gated distributed-only processing with a distributed-plus-hub account adding a common convergence stage across semantic categories and modalities. Both views acknowledge distributed modality-specific processing. The review states a contrastive prediction: damage to a common hub should impair semantic knowledge across input and output modalities, whereas the distributed-only account as formulated in the review does not predict comparably generalized impairment from a focal lesion (p.~977). The authors review clinical semantic-dementia evidence concerning generalized conceptual impairment and relatively circumscribed anterior-temporal atrophy (p.~978).

This source contrast is not itself a capacity-type comparison among two observed systems. We construct the fixed \(Q_{\mathrm{sem}}\): when two systems can perform ordinary semantic naming and classification tasks, does that represented task-level similarity support their instantiating the same \emph{mechanistically individuated cross-modal generalization capacity}? The declared \(C_{\mathrm{sem}}\) distinguishes generalization depending on a shared convergence mechanism from organization by modality-linked, task-dependent pathways. This is the \emph{present author's stipulation}, not a cognitive-kind classification claimed to be proven by the 2007 source. Under coarse \(\phi_{\mathrm{task}}\), similar routine competence need not preserve this mechanistic distinction; under richer \(\phi_{\mathrm{lesion}}\), predicted modality-spanning effects of localized ATL damage become relevant. The question and criterion are held fixed; the candidate evidential event is changed.

The lesion contrast is not an automatic proof of hub uniqueness. Gainotti (2012, \emph{Cortex} 48:521--529, DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2011.06.019}{10.1016/j.cortex.2011.06.019}) emphasizes early lateralized and modality-associated semantic deficits, offering an alternative interpretation of later bilateral impairment. This is a serious scientific challenge to a unique, fully amodal hub, not proof that the original reviewed clinical observations are false. Naturally occurring neurodegeneration is not a randomized lesion intervention. Disease progression, bilateral involvement and possible distributed network effects are relevant.

\paragraph{Evidential result and boundary.}
The 2007 review supplies a source-grounded \emph{mode of discrimination}: lesion-associated cross-modal impairment is more diagnostic between the specified rival architectures than mere ordinary task success. The DR audit therefore marks a generic task-success match \emph{underqualified} for \(C_{\mathrm{sem}}\), and identifies clinically grounded observations capable of restricting live alternatives. It does \emph{not} assert that two real observed systems were paired for a same-\(Q_{\mathrm{sem}},C_{\mathrm{sem}}\) identity hypothesis, calculate a cognitive-kind likelihood ratio, or establish a source-based activated defeater. Those steps require independently justified \(C\), two specified target systems, empirical response profiles and a contrastive bridge from mechanism to type identity. A functionally specified \(C\) could yield another verdict; mechanism is not declared a universal criterion. No new corpus analysis or MAIN40 result is derived from this documentary case.


\section{Partial-knowledge operation and distinction between two mixtures}

The main text's \(D/U\) example stipulates two background assumptions for the \emph{same} coarse match event. Marginalizing over these assumptions is legitimate only after specifying their probabilities conditional on the competing identity hypotheses. The \(x/y\) finite pair model instead defines \emph{two distinct map-generated events} and a separate reporting procedure whose map label may be observed or withheld. Their numerical mixture ratios coincide because the constructed parameter values coincide, not because uncertainty about an explanatory background model is interchangeable with uncertainty about which event was reported. In particular, a known \((R=x,E_x)\) retains its properly justified ratio \(4\) despite \(R=y\) being an admissible alternative.

The useful additional negative control is \emph{known-map evidential promotion}. In the finite law the map \(R=y\), the event \(E_y\) and the ordinary-task accuracy \(3/4\) can all be known and correctly reported. Even so, \(E_y\) is equally likely under \(H_C\) and \(\neg H_C\), giving identity-specific ratio \(1\). A false positive identity attribution would therefore be an unjustified \emph{transfer} of task warrant to a different claim rather than only a report missing its provenance metadata. The example is constructed, not a claim that the scientific corpus contains this documented error.

For incompletely specified empirical models the audit uses three separate statuses. \emph{Qualified}: a justified same-\(Q,C\) comparison, with an appropriate bridge and sufficiently calibrated or restricted evidence, warrants the particular level of attributed support. \emph{Underqualified}: the evidence item and map may be known, but the capacity-relevant bridge, live alternative restrictions or sampling calibration remains unspecified; no exact likelihood ratio or automatic undercutter follows. \emph{Activated undercutting}: a scientifically serious same-question rival exposes an actual support-changing omission in the reasons offered for the claimed positive identity support. A fully specified toy distribution supplies exact calculations; a clinical literature synthesis may supply diagnostic constraints without a numerical probability law. DR does not impose a universal numeric sufficiency threshold or independently choose the right \(C\).
