\section{Lossless and lossy representation controls}

The lossless control uses an invertible refactorization of the working basis. It preserves the information required to reconstruct the original role structure while changing which distinctions are first-class. This establishes non-uniqueness of primitive factorization without information loss.

The lossy control deliberately projects the 206 bounded objects into a coarser process--constraint representation. The 206 objects become 143 coarse signatures; 63 of the 99 original cross-stratum families collide; 62 coarse signatures retain cross-stratum support; and 58 of 60 claims participate in at least one cross-stratum coarse signature. These counts show both collapse and survival under one lossy rival and do not establish representation neutrality.

The philosophically central witness concerns the conflict-monitoring source by Botvinick et al. (DOI \path{10.1037/0033-295X.108.3.624}) and Tulving's episodic-memory source (DOI \path{10.1146/annurev.psych.53.100901.135114}). Their fine bounded representations have zero overlap, but a deliberately coarse temporal-state projection produces positive overlap by forgetting a distinction between temporal organizations.

The coarse temporal question has an independent modeling precedent. Treur's temporal-factorisation framework (Cognitive Systems Research 8(2):57--74, DOI \path{10.1016/j.cogsys.2006.08.002}) treats a past-pattern--future-pattern dependence as factorable through a mediating present-state property and explicitly applies that relation between history-based and state-based descriptions to cognitive dynamics. This does not establish that the two fine temporal organizations in the present witness are scientifically equivalent. It establishes only that abstracting across explicit history and state-mediated temporal dependence is an independently articulated cognitive-modeling move rather than a transformation justified solely by the overlap it creates.

\section{Capacity-level witness cases}

Three cases illustrate different inferential failures.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Shared label without a preserved bridge.} Tulving's episodic-memory analysis and Patterson, Nestor, and Rogers' semantic-knowledge analysis (DOI \path{10.1038/nrn2277}) remain structurally disjoint under both tested bounded representations. This does not refute a unified semantic-memory capacity; it shows that label continuity alone does not supply the bridge.
\item \textbf{Different labels with shared bounded organization.} Duncan's object-based attention source (DOI \path{10.1037/0096-3445.113.4.501}) and Behrens et al.'s volatility-sensitive learning source (DOI \path{10.1038/nn1954}) remain incomparable as complete claims but share a recurring bounded probe-to-outcome organization. This motivates a bridge hypothesis without establishing capacity identity.
\item \textbf{Representation-sensitive overlap.} The Botvinick--Tulving comparison has zero fine overlap and positive coarse overlap. The change is a representation-sensitivity diagnostic rather than a new capacity assignment.
\end{itemize}

\section{Constructed reachability controls}

Constructed controls test whether the comparison procedures are capable of returning alternatives to the observed corpus outcome. A matched synthetic calibration includes direct cases, cases repairable by an explicit stateless mapping, and cases that require a history-bearing repair. The procedure reaches all three designed classes. Two source-derived counterfactual perturbations likewise show that hiding a load-bearing interface can require a stateless repair, whereas hiding a delay-spanning trace can require added persistent state.

These controls demonstrate reachability of the classification rule. They do not modify the observed prospective source results and are not prevalence estimates.

\section{System--environment--experiment typing}

The structural comparison distinguishes the modeled cognitive system \(G_{\mathrm{cog}}\), environment \(W\), cross-boundary coupling \(\Gamma\), experimental protocol \(E_{\mathrm{exp}}\), and a realized finite run/history \(R\). Three non-identities are preserved:

\[
K\neq\Gamma,\qquad
C\neq\text{experimental condition},\qquad
P_{\mathrm{in}}/P_{\mathrm{out}}\neq\text{experimental schedule}.
\]

The distinction is architectural rather than isolationist. A dynamical, embodied, enactive, or extended model may draw the system boundary broadly and may treat environment coupling as constitutive. The method requires the selected boundary to be stated so that an experimental manipulation or environmental carrier is not automatically redescribed as an internal cognitive-system role.

A finite experiment is also treated as a cut \(t_0\) through a potentially longer history rather than as the beginning of the cognitive system. Admissibility of \(X_{t_0}\), the realized state at the cut, and the experimental preparation that helped produce it are kept distinct.

\section{Independent human-coding boundary}

Independent human re-adjudication has not been performed. A pre-specified human-coding protocol exists for source-level reconstruction and downstream comparison, but no independent human response file is part of the evidence reported here.

The stronger source-level protocol requires a second human to begin from the original publication and independently construct a structured scientific claim without access to the retained reference reconstruction, bounded memberships, role-projection verdicts, capacity-case outcomes, or answer key. Only after the independent records are complete may alignment with the retained records be scored.

Metadata masking of already constructed records would test a different and weaker question. It cannot establish that the original semantic coding was conceptually blind to the constructs under discussion. For this reason no language-model replay, deterministic CI check, or formal verification result is counted as independent human inter-rater evidence.

\section{Named-model procedural robustness diagnostics}

The model-based robustness diagnostic was conducted in two stages on the same ten-source sample.

\subsection{Unanchored replay}

The first replay withheld retained structured answers but allowed each model configuration to select one focal cognitive claim from each source. This design was intended to test independent source reconstruction, but it confounded two tasks: selecting which claim to reconstruct and decomposing that claim.

The resulting retained-reference comparison was:

These counts are not interpreted as a reliability rate. Case inspection showed that many substantive differences arose because the replay and the retained reference had selected different valid claims from the same paper.

\subsection{Claim-anchored replay}

A second replay was specified prospectively relative to its own outputs. Each case supplied a source locator and a minimally identifying proposition fixing the target scientific claim while continuing to withhold the retained structural decomposition. \section{Source correction and sensitivity analysis}

The sole retained-reference substantive case in the claim-anchored replay concerned Karpicke and Blunt's retrieval-practice study (DOI \path{10.1126/science.1199327}). Both model configurations independently identified the same timing qualification.

Experiment 1 is exactly time matched:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item open-book relational-diagram activity: 5 + 25 = 30 minutes;
\item closed-book retrieval activity: 5 + 10 + 5 + 10 = 30 minutes.
\end{itemize}

Experiment 2 is not exactly matched:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item open-book relational-diagram activity: 5 + 20 = 25 minutes;
\item closed-book retrieval activity: 5 + 7 + 5 + 7 = 24 minutes.
\end{itemize}

The successor source record therefore restricts the exact matched-time condition to Experiment 1 and records the Experiment 2 qualification. The earlier retained record and historical replay score were not rewritten post hoc.

A deterministic sensitivity analysis then recomputed every potentially affected structural result. The source correction changed the semantic/provenance digest of the structured record but left its whole-claim projection exactly unchanged. Consequently:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item all 59 pairwise comparisons involving this claim were unchanged;
\item the full whole-claim result remained 1,770/1,770 incomparable;
\item the bounded reconstruction remained 206 objects and 99 cross-stratum families, with no membership change for the corrected claim;
\item the role projection for the corrected claim remained full with the same role inventory, while the resource/admissibility condition was narrowed to its source-supported scope.
\end{itemize}

This analysis separates source fidelity from structural sensitivity. The correction is scientifically real at the source-description level but terminates below the granularity used by the whole-claim comparator.
