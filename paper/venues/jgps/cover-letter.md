# Cover Letter — Journal for General Philosophy of Science

Editors-in-Chief  
Journal for General Philosophy of Science

Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, **“Toward a Construct-Label-Neutral Structural Comparison Basis for Cognitive Capacities,”** for publication as a full-length research article in the *Journal for General Philosophy of Science*.

The manuscript addresses a methodological problem in the philosophy of cognitive science: whether historically named cognitive capacities can be compared on a common structural basis without treating construct labels themselves as evidence of individuation. It develops and audits a source-grounded, construct-label-neutral procedure that suppresses inherited construct labels and selected authority-bearing metadata while explicitly adopting a declared system-theoretic structural representation. The neutrality claim is therefore narrower than theory-, ontology-, or representation-neutrality.

I believe the manuscript fits JGPS particularly well because its central contribution is methodological and epistemological rather than a new domain-specific cognitive theory or a full theory of measurement. It asks how heterogeneous scientific claims can be compared when inherited construct labels may obscure both common structure and genuine difference, and it treats the resulting framework as a structural comparison basis with explicit preservation and forgetting relations. This aligns with the journal’s interest in methodological and philosophical foundations shared across scientific fields.

The manuscript also engages directly with a recent JGPS contribution by Majid D. Beni, “Kinds Without Structure Are Blind; Structure Without Kinds Is Empty: Remarks on the Methodology of Consciousness Science” (2026). Beni proposes a structural-realist framework for consciousness science and uses predictive processing as a candidate unifying formal basis. The present manuscript takes a complementary but different inferential route: it does not select a unifying cognitive theory in advance, but reconstructs a role grammar from a source-audited heterogeneous claim corpus after preserving the failure of an initially pre-specified whole-claim comparison.

The manuscript’s principal methodological result is the preserved failure/recovery trajectory: all 1,770 whole-claim pairs are incomparable under the pre-specified rich comparison surface; a secondary bounded reconstruction recovers 206 unique reusable structural subobjects and 99 cross-stratum families; reverse projection across 60 claims yields 21 full, 22 partial, and 17 residual cases; and residual adjudication establishes that no residual claim requires an additional top-level role. It also reports a separately pre-specified, single-adjudicator, source-identity-separated prospective compatibility test on 40 cognitive-model papers with zero exact DOI overlap with the original 60-claim corpus. Under a predeclared direct/stateless/stateful reconstruction order, all 40 were reconstructed directly at A0, including 24/24 component papers and 16/16 integrated papers, with no reconstruction-added stateless adapter or additional persistent coordinator. A later matched Dynamic/POMDP-like discriminability stress test demonstrates that A1 and A2 are reachable under the same reconstruction-addition semantics, while remaining explicitly separate from the prospective chronology. The manuscript does not treat these as 40 independent replications and does not claim inter-rater reliability: genealogy remains incompletely resolved, the corpus is purposive, and independent human re-adjudication was not performed. Coarser dynamical and POMDP-like projections are described only as strict distinction-loss tests. The manuscript explicitly states that the structural role grammar is not a demarcation criterion for cognition and does not claim a universal ontology, a universal direct-composition result, a full measurement theory, absolute mathematical minimality, representative population coverage, or independent extraction reliability.

This work has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. I am the sole author and take full responsibility for the manuscript. The use of OpenAI ChatGPT as a structured research assistant for initial structured claim extraction, software/workflow assistance, and manuscript drafting/editing is disclosed in the manuscript; all retained source claims were reviewed against source text by the author, and model outputs were not treated as scientific authority.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
