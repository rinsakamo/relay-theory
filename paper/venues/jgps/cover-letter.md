Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, **“Toward a Concept-Neutral Measurement Basis for Cognitive Capacities,”** for publication as a full-length research article in the *Journal for General Philosophy of Science*.

The manuscript addresses a methodological problem in the philosophy of cognitive science: whether historically named cognitive capacities can be compared on a common structural basis without treating construct labels themselves as evidence of individuation. It develops and audits a source-grounded procedure that suppresses nominal and authority-bearing information, compares frozen claim structures, preserves a complete whole-claim comparison failure, reconstructs bounded reusable substructures, and tests the resulting system-role grammar against residual and comparator challenges.

I believe the manuscript fits JGPS particularly well because its central contribution is methodological and epistemological rather than a new domain-specific cognitive theory. It asks how heterogeneous scientific claims can be compared when their inherited conceptual vocabularies may obscure both common structure and genuine difference. This aligns with the journal’s interest in the methodological and philosophical foundations shared across scientific fields.

The manuscript also engages directly with a recent JGPS contribution by Majid D. Beni, “Kinds Without Structure Are Blind; Structure Without Kinds Is Empty: Remarks on the Methodology of Consciousness Science” (2026). Beni proposes a structural-realist framework for consciousness science and uses predictive processing as a candidate unifying formal basis. The present manuscript takes a complementary but different inferential route: it does not select a unifying cognitive theory in advance, but reconstructs a role grammar from a source-audited heterogeneous claim corpus after preserving the failure of an initially frozen whole-claim comparison.

The manuscript’s principal bounded results are: all 1,770 whole-claim pairs are incomparable under the frozen rich comparison surface; a secondary bounded reconstruction recovers 206 unique Archetype/XLike objects and 99 cross-lane families; reverse projection across 60 claims yields 21 FULL, 22 PARTIAL, and 17 RESIDUAL cases; and residual adjudication establishes no additional top-level role gap. The manuscript explicitly limits these findings to the designed corpus and does not claim a universal ontology of cognition, absolute mathematical minimality, representative population coverage, or independent extraction reliability.

This work has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. I am the sole author and take full responsibility for the manuscript. The use of OpenAI ChatGPT as a structured research assistant for initial ClaimIR extraction, software/workflow assistance, and manuscript drafting/editing is disclosed in the manuscript; all retained source claims were reviewed against source text by the author, and model outputs were not treated as scientific authority.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
