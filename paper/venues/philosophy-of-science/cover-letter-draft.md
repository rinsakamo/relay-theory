Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript **“When May a Formal Difference Enter an Individuation Inference? Target Factorization, Restricted Tests, and a UMI Case”** for publication as an Article in *Philosophy of Science*.

The manuscript addresses a formal-methodological question at the intersection of scientific representation, individuation, and observational equivalence: when may a difference inside a formal representation legitimately count as evidence for a difference in the represented target? The contribution is intentionally narrow. It does not propose a new equivalence theory or derive metaphysical numerical identity. Instead, it formulates a necessary-condition framework for individuation inferences. The manuscript separates structural target factorization from the independent scientific warrant required to accept it, connects representation-level observations to target-level responses, develops a counterfactual relevance audit that distinguishes renaming from assignment perturbation, illustrates the distinction with UMI-based molecular counting, and uses a small Lean development as a dependency audit.

The manuscript engages directly with work on scientific representation and target-directed inference, representation independence, behavioral equivalence, identity and discernibility, and scientific individuation. The closest prior art is treated explicitly in the Related Work section, and the novelty claim has been narrowed accordingly.
