Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript **“When May a Formal Difference Enter an Individuation Inference? Target Factorization, Restricted Tests, and Cross-Domain Audits”** for publication as an Article in *Philosophy of Science*.

The manuscript addresses a formal-methodological question at the intersection of scientific representation, individuation, and operational individuation: when may a difference inside a formal representation legitimately count as evidence for a difference in the represented target? The contribution is intentionally narrow. It does not propose a new equivalence theory or derive metaphysical numerical identity. Within an exact deterministic representation-to-target model, it characterizes structural eligibility by the three-way equivalence among fiber invariance, universal soundness of feature-difference inference, and quotient descent; more generally, it requires a warranted route from a formal distinction to the declared target. The manuscript develops a feature-level audit that separates structural descent, test-specific transfer, independent scientific warrant, and counterfactual representation checks. A bibliographic-record example motivates the problem, UMI-based molecular counting provides a cross-domain scientific stress test, and evidence synthesis shows how the same discipline applies to report, study, and evidence-unit distinctions. A small Lean development serves as a dependency audit rather than as a claim of mathematical depth.

The manuscript engages directly with work on scientific representation and target-directed inference, including Frigg and Nguyen's DEKI account, Suárez's contemporary inferential account, representation independence, behavioral equivalence, identity and discernibility, and scientific individuation. It does not claim target-directed representation, invariance, quotient mathematics, or practice-relative individuation as novel in isolation. The narrower contribution is their integration into a single auditable protocol for individuation inferences. The construct-level outlook is explicitly prospective: nominal differences between theoretical constructs do not by themselves establish differences between measured capacities. The closest prior art is treated explicitly in the Related Work section.

This manuscript has not been published and is not under review elsewhere. [Confirm before submission.]
